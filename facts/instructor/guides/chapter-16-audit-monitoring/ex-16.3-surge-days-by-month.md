# Exercise 16.3: Surge Days by Month

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

Part III traced the growth of the plant's overtime to surge days, days on which every hourly manufacturing employee clocked exactly the same hours, and Chapter 12 found that the Production Manager approved every surge-day entry. The audit committee wants to know each month whether they continue. The exercise is an analytical test, an anomaly test in the chapter's terms, and its technical lesson is a limit of Desktop's Group By. The analytics type is diagnostic. The judgment lesson is in Requirement (5): who should see the result.

Students can:

- group clock entries by day and count the distinct employees and the distinct values of total hours by editing the formula bar (Requirements 2 and 3);
- chart a count and a share by month with the right denominator (Requirement 4);
- describe when a pattern began, how often it recurs and how its share has moved (Requirement 5);
- explain why a test belongs in internal audit's report and not on a manager's page (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (data mining and analysis, visualization and communicating results); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Business Acumen and Operations (operational knowledge); IFAC IES 2 (b) (management accounting) and IES 3 (b) (communicating effectively); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Before you assign

- **Files.** The copy after Tutorial 16.3 with its Employee query and Date table. The model answer is page Ex 16.3 of the project, the two queries, and the display folder Ex 16.3.
- **Data conditions.** On every surge day all hourly manufacturing employees clock the same regular and overtime hours (TimeClockEntry), and each entry carries an approval by the Production Manager on the work date. {{ dup|capitalize }} employee-days of the plant have two clock entries, none on a surge day, so a count of rows is not a count of employees in general. The test counts the employees who clocked in, as H8 does, and a count against Employee shows they are the whole hourly crew.
- **Verify first.** Employee carries CostCenterID, and the model answer filters ClockEntries to the Manufacturing cost center's ID, {{ x.center }}.

## How to run it

Individual or pairs, about 75 minutes: 15 for Requirement (1), 25 for (2) and (3), 15 for (4), 10 for (5), 10 spare. Do not tell students that Group By cannot count distinct values until they have tried: the failure teaches the limit. Collect the .pbix and the note.

## What a strong answer says

- **(5)** The pattern with its numbers, as above, and the reason for the test's home: the approver of every flagged entry is the person the report would otherwise reach, so it goes to the people who oversee the control.
- It separates what the test shows (hours identical for the whole crew, approved by one person) from what it does not (why), and asks the production side for the explanation through internal audit.

A strong answer ties the count, the share and the approver together. A weak one lists the months.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Count Distinct Rows.** *Symptom:* an empty SurgeDays. *Response:* ask what the rows of one day have in common.
- **Formula-bar edit skipped.** *Symptom:* the same empty table. *Response:* ask what HoursValues counts.
- **All cost centers.** *Symptom:* {{ x.n_all_centers }} surge days. *Response:* ask who else clocks that day.
- **Wrong denominator.** *Symptom:* a share of `100%`. *Response:* ask what the share is a share of.
- **Axis into the empty future.** *Symptom:* months with no columns after the data end. *Response:* ask where the Date table ends.
- **A count of entries read as employees.** *Symptom:* none today. *Response:* ask what a second entry for one person would do.
