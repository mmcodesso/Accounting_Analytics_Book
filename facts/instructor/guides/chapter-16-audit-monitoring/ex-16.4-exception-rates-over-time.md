# Exercise 16.4: Exception Rates over Time

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

The audit committee asks whether the controls behind the tests are getting better or worse. Counts cannot answer that when the number of documents changes from year to year, and the exceptions already explained, the year-end closes and the recurring payroll test, hide what changes in the rest. The exercise builds the rate that sets those two aside, and its technical lesson is KEEPFILTERS. The analytics type is descriptive, the reading of the trend is diagnostic, and the note's recommendation is prescriptive.

Students can:

- show exceptions, populations and rates by process and year, and say why a total row shows no rate (Requirement 1);
- write a rate that leaves out expected and recurring exceptions and still works when a reader selects a test (Requirement 2);
- chart rates over time (Requirement 3);
- explain why a count and a rate can tell different stories (Requirement 4);
- write a short note on whether each process is improving (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution ISC M2 (tests of internal controls) and Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results); IMA Reporting and Control (internal control) and Strategy, Planning, and Performance (performance management); IFAC IES 2 (g) (governance, risk management and internal control) and (i) (using ICT to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** The rate is the process's exceptions over its own documents; the total row is blank because the populations differ.
- **(2)** The measure's meaning stated in its label, and the KEEPFILTERS reason: it intersects a reader's selection with the exclusions.
- **(4)** The count rose and the rate did not, because the denominator grew.
- **(5)** A verdict for each process, naming the documents behind the journal entries' movement and the design finding behind payroll's flat rate.

A strong answer says what the unexplained rate adds: it separates the movement from the two groups already understood. A weak one repeats the rates.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **No KEEPFILTERS.** *Symptom:* {{ x.probe }} at {{ x.probe_wrong|num(1) }}. *Response:* ask which exceptions the row counts.
- **Open as unexplained.** *Symptom:* payroll at {{ x.open_years['PR'][0] }} exceptions, because its deficiencies and follow-ups are reviewed. *Response:* ask which dispositions the exercise leaves out.
- **A rate in the total row.** *Symptom:* {{ x.blend_rate|num(1) }}. *Response:* ask which population it divides by.
- **Count read as decline.** *Symptom:* "orders got worse". *Response:* ask how many orders there were.
- **Shared axis.** *Symptom:* a flat purchase order line. *Response:* ask what scale the line needs.
- **No label.** *Symptom:* a measure named "Rate". *Response:* ask what a committee would think it excludes.

## Extensions and discussion questions

- Add the rate by test and month, and say which test the January cluster belongs to.
- Add a check that each year's two year-end closes still appear, so a missing expected exception is itself flagged.
- Discuss: should the committee see the unexplained rate alone? What does hiding the recurring test cost?
- Discuss: how many exceptions would it take to call a change in a rate real, and who decides?
