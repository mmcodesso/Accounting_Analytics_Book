# Exercise 11.2: Monthly Revenue with Year-to-Date and Prior-Year Comparisons

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

Chapter 1 promised that SQL would support the period-over-period comparisons behind a forecast. The controller asks for a monthly revenue report by product line, from the ledger, with a year-to-date total and a comparison with the same month of the prior year. It is the chapter's two most common windows in one query: a SUM partitioned by year, and a LAG partitioned by the month. The report must start from the same numbers as the statements, so it ends with a tie to the trial balance. The analytics type is descriptive, moving to diagnostic in the Furniture question.

By the end, students can:

- total the ledger by fiscal year, period and account and name each product line with CASE (outcome 1);
- add a year-to-date total and a prior-year comparison with the right partition and order (outcome 3);
- tie the December year to date to the trial balance, and read a decline against its base (outcomes 3 and 4).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 T3 to T4 (data mining and analysis of financial data) and M4 (financial statement analysis and performance measures); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data analysis) and Strategy, Planning, and Performance (performance management); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (i); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Revenue by month and product line

**Step 1. Total credits less debits for the five accounts, without the closes.**

```sql
-- Requirement (1): revenue by fiscal year, period, and account, without the closes
-- Population: postings to 4010-4040 and 4080, fiscal 2024-2026; expected: 180 rows
WITH Monthly AS (
    SELECT gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber,
        CASE a.AccountNumber
            WHEN 4010 THEN 'Furniture'
            WHEN 4020 THEN 'Lighting'
            WHEN 4030 THEN 'Textiles'
            WHEN 4040 THEN 'Accessories'
            WHEN 4080 THEN 'Services'
        END AS ProductLine,
        SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit) AS Revenue
    FROM GLEntry AS gl
        INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
    WHERE a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
        AND gl.FiscalYear BETWEEN 2024 AND 2026
        AND gl.VoucherNumber NOT IN (
            SELECT EntryNumber
            FROM JournalEntry
            WHERE EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%')
    GROUP BY gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber
)
SELECT ProductLine, AccountNumber, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod,
    ROUND(Revenue, 2) AS Revenue
FROM Monthly
ORDER BY ProductLine, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod;
```

**Check:** {{ g.n_rows }} rows. **Watch for:** the closes are posted in period 12 of the same accounts, so leaving them in does not change the row count, only the December figures: Furniture's December revenue of the last year reads -${{ (g.in_dec[1]|abs)|money }} and its December year to date zero. Students who filter with `strftime('%Y', PostingDate) = {{ g.y }}` find {{ g.n_num|count }} rows, because the year comes back as text.

### Requirement (2): The year-to-date total

**Step 2. Add a running total by product line and year.** In the model script the column is `SUM(Revenue) OVER (PARTITION BY ProductLine, FiscalYear ORDER BY FiscalPeriod)`. A product line has one row for each period of a year, so the default window has no ties and gives a plain running total.

**Check:** December's value is the year's total (Step 4 ties it). **Watch for:** without `FiscalYear` in the partition the total runs through every year of the window, and Furniture's last December reads ${{ g.cum|money }}.

### Requirement (3): The same month of the prior year

**Step 3. Add LAG and the percentage change.** The Compared CTE holds both window columns.

```sql
-- Requirement (3): the same month of the prior year with LAG, and the percentage change
-- Population: the monthly revenue; expected: no prior year for fiscal 2024
WITH Monthly AS (
    SELECT gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber,
        CASE a.AccountNumber
            WHEN 4010 THEN 'Furniture'
            WHEN 4020 THEN 'Lighting'
            WHEN 4030 THEN 'Textiles'
            WHEN 4040 THEN 'Accessories'
            WHEN 4080 THEN 'Services'
        END AS ProductLine,
        SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit) AS Revenue
    FROM GLEntry AS gl
        INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
    WHERE a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
        AND gl.FiscalYear BETWEEN 2024 AND 2026
        AND gl.VoucherNumber NOT IN (
            SELECT EntryNumber
            FROM JournalEntry
            WHERE EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%')
    GROUP BY gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber
),
Compared AS (
    SELECT ProductLine, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod, Revenue,
        SUM(Revenue) OVER (PARTITION BY ProductLine, FiscalYear
            ORDER BY FiscalPeriod) AS RevenueYTD,
        LAG(Revenue) OVER (PARTITION BY ProductLine, FiscalPeriod
            ORDER BY FiscalYear) AS PriorYear
    FROM Monthly
)
SELECT ProductLine, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod,
    ROUND(Revenue, 2) AS Revenue, ROUND(RevenueYTD, 2) AS RevenueYTD,
    ROUND(PriorYear, 2) AS PriorYear,
    ROUND(100.0 * (Revenue - PriorYear) / PriorYear, 1) AS PctChange
FROM Compared
ORDER BY ProductLine, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod;
```

**Check:** {{ g.null_years }} rows with a NULL PriorYear, the five lines of the first year. **Watch for:** three variants run without an error.

- A LAG ordered by year and period with no partition by period compares October with September: Furniture's October of the last year reads ${{ g.prev_month|money }} as its prior year, which is its September.
- A LAG with no partition by product line compares Furniture with another line: the same October reads ${{ g.wrong_line|money }} on the SQLite used for these notes. Its prior-year figure is not any Furniture month.
- A WHERE on the year in the same SELECT as the LAG runs before the window, so every PriorYear is NULL. Filter in an outer query or an earlier CTE.

### Requirement (4): The tie to the trial balance

**Step 4. Compare December's year to date with the trial balance.**

```sql
-- Requirement (4): December 2026 year to date against the trial balance of Tutorial 10.2
-- Population: the five revenue accounts at 2026-12-31; expected: no differences
WITH Monthly AS (
    SELECT gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber,
        CASE a.AccountNumber
            WHEN 4010 THEN 'Furniture'
            WHEN 4020 THEN 'Lighting'
            WHEN 4030 THEN 'Textiles'
            WHEN 4040 THEN 'Accessories'
            WHEN 4080 THEN 'Services'
        END AS ProductLine,
        SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit) AS Revenue
    FROM GLEntry AS gl
        INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
    WHERE a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
        AND gl.FiscalYear BETWEEN 2024 AND 2026
        AND gl.VoucherNumber NOT IN (
            SELECT EntryNumber
            FROM JournalEntry
            WHERE EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%')
    GROUP BY gl.FiscalYear, gl.FiscalPeriod, a.AccountNumber
),
YearToDate AS (
    SELECT ProductLine, AccountNumber, FiscalYear, FiscalPeriod,
        SUM(Revenue) OVER (PARTITION BY ProductLine, FiscalYear
            ORDER BY FiscalPeriod) AS RevenueYTD
    FROM Monthly
),
TrialBalance AS (
    SELECT a.AccountNumber,
        ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS Balance
    FROM GLEntry AS gl
        INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
    WHERE a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
        AND gl.PostingDate <= '2026-12-31'
        AND gl.VoucherNumber NOT IN ('JE-2026-000296', 'JE-2026-000297')
    GROUP BY a.AccountNumber
)
SELECT y.ProductLine, y.AccountNumber,
    ROUND(y.RevenueYTD, 2) AS DecemberYTD, tb.Balance AS TrialBalance,
    ROUND(y.RevenueYTD - tb.Balance, 2) AS Difference
FROM YearToDate AS y
    INNER JOIN TrialBalance AS tb ON tb.AccountNumber = y.AccountNumber
WHERE y.FiscalYear = 2026 AND y.FiscalPeriod = 12
ORDER BY y.AccountNumber;
```

**Check:** five rows with a Difference of zero; the total is ${{ total|money }}. The trial balance of operating revenue is larger by ${{ freight|money }}, the freight account, which is not a product line.

## What a strong answer says

- **Start from the tie.** The December year to date equals each account's balance in the trial balance, so the report starts from the statements' numbers. A strong note says that the closes are the only journal entries to these accounts.
- **Read the decline against its base.** {{ down.month }} fell {{ down.pct|spct }}, but its prior-year month was Furniture's {{ base }} month of that year, so part of the decline is a high base.
- **Name the next evidence.** The Furniture promotion priced orders in {{ priced|join(' and ') }} and its orders were invoiced through {{ later|join(' and ') }}, so a strong note looks at revenue by invoice month and promotion, the order dates of the lines invoiced in each month, and the volume and price per unit behind each month, as the bridge of Tutorial 6.3 separates them.
- **Call it a screen.** A ledger comparison locates the months; it does not say why they moved.

A weak note explains the decline with a cause the query cannot show, or reports percentages without the amounts.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Closes left in.** *Symptom:* a year to date of zero in December. *Response:* ask what posts to revenue on the last day.
- **A running total across years.** *Symptom:* ${{ g.cum|money }} at the last December. *Response:* ask which partition restarts the total.
- **LAG on the wrong partition.** *Symptom:* a prior year of ${{ g.prev_month|money }} or ${{ g.wrong_line|money }}. *Response:* ask which rows share a partition.
- **A year filter beside the LAG.** *Symptom:* every PriorYear NULL. *Response:* ask when WHERE runs.
- **A year compared as a number.** *Symptom:* {{ g.n_num|count }} rows from `strftime('%Y', ...) = {{ g.y }}`. *Response:* ask what type strftime returns.
