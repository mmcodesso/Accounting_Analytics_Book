# Exercise 15.1: From the Income Statement to the Entry

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

An income statement answers how much. A reader who sees a line that appears in two years and no others asks what it is, and the report should answer in two clicks. The exercise builds that path: a drill-through page keyed on the account subtype, a read of the entry behind the number, and a query that lists every posting of the entry. The line is {{ g.acct_number }} {{ g.acct_name }}, one disposal in each of {{ d.P }} and {{ g.y }}. The analytics type is descriptive, with a diagnostic habit: do not accept a total until you have seen its entries.

Students can:

- build a drill-through page on a field of the visual they will right-click (Requirement 1);
- drill through from a matrix cell and read the entry (Requirement 2);
- list every posting of one entry in DAX query view, with RELATED (Requirement 3);
- explain a loss on disposal from the accounts of its entry (Requirement 4);
- say which filters a drill-through passes and what a missing one costs (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions) and M8 (visualization and communicating results); IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping) and Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1 (technology integration).

## Before you assign

- **Files.** The copy after Tutorial 15.3, saved as `Ch15 Exercises.pbix`, with the Income Statement page of Tutorial 14.3. The model answer holds the hidden page Ledger Detail (saved as left after a drill-through, so it lists both years), the DAX query tab Ex 15.1 and a Notes page.
- **Data conditions.** The subtype Other Income or Expense holds two accounts in the chart, and only {{ g.acct_number }} has postings: one loss entry in {{ d.P }} and one in {{ g.y }}. Each year's close also credits the account, and GL Amount leaves the closes out. Account columns come from Account through a relationship, which is why RELATED works on a posting.
- **Verify first.** The Income Statement matrix has AccountType and AccountSubType on **Rows** and Year on **Columns**, and P&L Amount excludes the closes (Tutorial 14.3). If AccountSubType is missing from the matrix, the **Drillthrough** option will not appear.

## How to run it

Individual, about 60 minutes: 15 for the page, 10 for the two drill-throughs, 10 for the query, 15 for the note, 10 spare. Hold back Requirement (5) until the student has drilled through once from a value cell and once from the row's label, which passes no year, because the contrast answers it. Collect the .pbix, the query text and the note, not screenshots.

### Requirement (4): How the amount was calculated

**Step 4. Write the explanation.** The loss is the asset's cost less its accumulated depreciation less the proceeds, and the entry holds each term: {{ now.number }} credits {{ now.cost.account }} with ${{ now.cost.amount|money }}, debits {{ now.acc.account }} with ${{ now.acc.amount|money }}, and received nothing, so the loss is ${{ now.loss|money }}. The entry retires asset {{ now.asset }}, "{{ now.event_desc }}". {{ before.number }} gives ${{ before.cost.amount|money }} less ${{ before.acc.amount|money }} less ${{ before.proceeds|money }} of cash, a loss of ${{ before.loss|money }}: a different asset, "{{ before.event_desc }}", and proceeds that cut the loss.

### Requirement (5): Why the year matters

**Step 5. Explain what the click passes.** A drill-through passes every filter of the data point: the row's AccountSubType, which the page's well holds, and the column's Year, which travels with the click although the page's well does not hold it. Without the year the subtype alone selects the account, and the account holds both entries, so the page lists both, which is how the model answer is saved (the Checks row "entries listed without a year filter" proves the two vouchers). A student sees the same by right-clicking the row's label, which carries no year. The closes are absent in every case, because P&L Amount leaves them out.

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** Names both entries with their dates, says each is a loss on the disposal of an asset, and gives the sign.
- **(4)** The three terms of the loss, from the entry's own lines, and the proceeds as the reason the two years differ.
- **(5)** The click carries the column's year as well as the row; without it the page lists both entries; the closes never appear because the measure leaves them out.

A strong answer reads the entry, not the total. A weak one repeats the account name.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **No Drillthrough option.** *Symptom:* the right-click menu lacks it. *Response:* ask whether AccountSubType is a field of the matrix the student clicked.
- **Closes on the page.** *Symptom:* four rows, or two on the {{ g.y }} page. *Response:* ask which column of the table is not the measure.
- **One row from the query.** *Symptom:* only the loss line. *Response:* ask what the other lines of the entry are.
- **A gain read from a negative.** *Symptom:* "income of ${{ now.loss|money }}". *Response:* ask which side of the entry the loss account is on.
- **A smaller asset blamed.** *Symptom:* "the {{ d.P }} loss is smaller because the asset cost less". *Response:* ask which line the {{ g.y }} entry does not have.
- **The year called a slicer.** *Symptom:* "the matrix is filtered by year". *Response:* ask what the page would show if the click had come from the row's label.
