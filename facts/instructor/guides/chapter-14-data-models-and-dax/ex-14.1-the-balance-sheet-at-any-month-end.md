# Exercise 14.1: The Balance Sheet at Any Month-End

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller wants a balance sheet beside the income statement of Tutorial 14.3, at the end of whichever month the reader selects and before that year's closing entries, so that it compares with Chapter 6's pre-closing balance sheet. The exercise is the cumulative measure: a balance is the sum of every posting to a date, so the measure removes the Date filter and puts its own bound back. It also settles what the closes do to a balance sheet. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- say what each of a cumulative measure's three filter arguments does (Requirement 1);
- build a month-end matrix that totals zero and ties to net income and to Chapter 6 (Requirement 2);
- explain why revenue and expense hold only the current year at a month-end (Requirement 3);
- compare three treatments of the closes and name the post-closing one (Requirement 4);
- say why signs taken from NormalBalance misstate subtotals (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics: data models and joins, dashboards); IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping, financial statement preparation) and Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

**Check:** the measure sits in the folder Ex 14.1. The model note, one sentence for each argument:

- Removing the filters on Date drops the month in the filter context, so the balance can reach back to the first posting.
- The date bound keeps every posting up to the last day selected, which makes the balance cumulative.
- The third argument leaves out a closing entry only when it is posted on that last day, so a year-end reads before its close while the closes of earlier years, dated before it, stay in and move each year's income to retained earnings.

**Watch for:** a balance that does not accumulate, which carries the month's filter. The matrix then reads the month's own movement, assets of ${{ g.month_asset|money }} against ${{ g.right_asset|money }}, and still totals zero, because the postings of one month balance. A total of zero does not test the cumulation.

### Requirement (3): June and the first year-end

**Step 3. Change the month.** Select `{{ g.y }}-06` in the slicer, then `{{ g.f }}-12`.

**Check:** both months total zero. In June, revenue and expense hold six months of postings; at the first year-end, they hold the year, a net income of ${{ ni_first|money }}. The reason is the third argument: the closes of earlier years are dated before the as-of date, so they stay in the balance and set revenue and expense to zero at each earlier year-end. ## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** Three sentences, one for each argument, and the reason for the third: it keeps a year-end before its close and the earlier closes after theirs.
- **(2)** The signs reversed, the three ties (zero, assets, net income) and the source of each.
- **(3)** The cumulation reaches back, and the earlier closes zero the earlier years, so revenue and expense show the current year only.
- **(4)** Names Balance with Closes as post-closing and Balance on GL Amount as the one that never moves income to equity, with the equity figures as evidence.
- **(5)** The accounts by name, the overstatement by contra accounts, and that a statement signs by type.

A strong answer ties each figure to a source. A weak one reports the matrix and stops at "it totals zero".

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **A balance for the month.** *Symptom:* assets of a few hundred thousand dollars. *Response:* ask what a balance is the sum of.
- **The zero as proof.** *Symptom:* "it totals zero, so it is right". *Response:* ask whether a month of balanced postings would also total zero.
- **The closes left in.** *Symptom:* revenue and expense of zero at the year-end. *Response:* ask which balance sheet Chapter 6 built.
- **GL Amount as the base.** *Symptom:* equity at its opening level and {{ years }} years of revenue. *Response:* ask where the year's net income went.
- **A multiple selection.** *Symptom:* the page reads the last day of what was selected. *Response:* turn on **Single select**.
- **Signs by NormalBalance.** *Symptom:* assets above Chapter 6's. *Response:* ask what accumulated depreciation does to the assets it reduces.
