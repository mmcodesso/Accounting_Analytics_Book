# Exercise 13.4: A Page for the Sales Manager

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

The sales manager reads one page in two ways: in meetings, to see which segments and regions matter, and on calls, to quote exact figures. The exercise turns Vessey's theory of cognitive fit into a design decision, then makes students operate the page the way a reader would: click a bar, watch what follows, and change what follows with Edit interactions. It closes on the hidden-filter rule of the chapter: the choice a reader makes while reading belongs in a slicer, and a restriction that belongs to the report belongs in the Filters pane with a title that says so. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- match a visual to each of two tasks and say why (Requirement 1);
- build a page of a bar chart, a matrix, a card and a slicer for one year (Requirement 2);
- predict and change how the visuals filter one another (Requirement 3);
- decide between a slicer and the Filters pane (Requirement 4);
- title every visual and describe the chart in alternative text (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics: models and joins, dashboards); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Leadership (communication skills); IFAC IES 2 (b) and IES 3 (b); AACSB A5 parts 5.1 and 5.2 (technology judgment, interpretation and communication).

## How to run it

Individual, about 70 minutes: 10 for (1), 25 for (2), 15 for (3), 10 for (4), 10 for (5). Hold back Edit interactions until students have clicked every bar and seen the matrix change. Collect the page and the note on design choices.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Which visual for which task

**Step 1. Match the visual to the task.** In meetings the manager compares segments and regions at a glance, a spatial task, so a sorted bar chart fits. On calls the manager quotes exact figures, a symbolic task, so a matrix fits. A strong answer names the theory (cognitive fit) and says the page needs both, as accountants often do.

**Check:** two or three sentences that assign one visual to each task and give the reason in terms of the task, not of taste.

### Requirement (2): The page

**Step 2. Fix the year for the page.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.4`.
b. In the **Filters** pane, drag FiscalYear from **SalesInvoice** into **Filters on this page** and select {{ g.y }}.

**Step 3. Add the bar chart, the matrix, the card and the slicer.**

a. Select **Clustered bar chart**. Drag CustomerSegment from **Customer** into **Y-axis** and LineTotal into **X-axis**, sort it from the largest, and turn on **Data labels** in **Format visual**.
b. Select **Matrix**. Drag CustomerSegment into **Rows**, Region into **Columns** and LineTotal into **Values**.
c. Select **Card** and drag DiscountAmount into **Values**. Under **Format visual > Callout values**, set **Display units** to **None**.
d. Select **Slicer**, drag Region into **Field**, and set **Style** to **Dropdown** under **Slicer settings**.

**Check:** the bar chart ranks {% for s in segments %}{{ s.name }}{{ ", " if not loop.last else "" }}{% endfor %}, from the largest. The matrix reaches ${{ g.rev_total|money }}, and its largest cell is {{ cell.segment }} in the {{ cell.region }}, ${{ cell.rev|money }}. The card reads ${{ g.disc_total|money }} until a bar is clicked. The regions rank {% for r, v in regions %}{{ r }}{{ ", " if not loop.last else "" }}{% endfor %}. **Watch for:** the year left out. The matrix and the card then cover every year: ${{ g.all_rev|money }} of revenue and ${{ g.all_disc|money }} of discounts.

### Requirement (3): How the visuals filter one another

**Step 4. Click each bar in turn.** Read the card and the matrix after each click, and click the bar again to clear it.

**Check:** a click filters the card to that segment's discounts and narrows the matrix to that segment; the bar chart itself highlights the selected bar against the faded rest.

{{ g.zero_segment }} shows no discounts: services carry no promotion.

**Step 5. Keep the matrix whole.**

a. Select the bar chart, and choose **Format > Edit interactions**.
b. On the matrix, choose the option that leaves it unaffected, **None**.
c. Click a bar again.

**Check:** the card still follows the click and the matrix keeps all {{ g.n_segments }} segments. The setting is better when the manager wants one segment's discounts beside the full table of exact figures. **Watch for:** the setting made from the wrong end. Select the visual that is clicked, here the bar chart, and set the option on the visual that should react, here the matrix; setting it with the matrix selected changes how the matrix acts on the others.

### Requirement (4): A slicer or the Filters pane

**Step 6. Place each restriction.** Region is a choice the manager makes while reading, so it sits in a slicer the manager can see and change. The fiscal year is fixed for the page, so it sits in **Filters on this page**, and every title names it. A restriction that belongs to the report, such as leaving the {{ g.zero_segment }} segment out of a merchandise page or keeping only sellable items, also belongs in the Filters pane, provided the title says so.

**Check:** the answer gives the reader's control as the test: a slicer is visible and changeable, a Filters pane entry is neither.

### Requirement (5): Titles and alternative text

**Step 7. Name each visual and describe the bar chart.**

a. Select each visual, and under **Format visual > General > Title** type a title that says what it shows and for which year, such as `Revenue by customer segment, fiscal {{ g.y }}`.
b. Select the bar chart, and in **Format visual > General** type its **Alt text**: Revenue by customer segment in fiscal {{ g.y }}, sorted from the largest. {{ segments[0].name }} is the largest segment, well ahead of {{ segments[1].name }}; {{ segments[3].name }} and {{ segments[4].name }} are the smallest.

**Check:** every title carries the measure and the year, and the card's title says it follows the bar chart's selection.

## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** The bar chart for the comparison, a spatial task, and the matrix for the figures, a symbolic task, with the theory named.
- **(2)** A page that ties to the Validation page, with sorted bars, a slicer and titles. The exercise asks for a card "by segment", which one card cannot show: a strong answer lets the card follow the bar chart and says so in its title.
- **(3)** The two behaviors described correctly, and a reason to keep the matrix whole.
- **(4)** The reader's control as the test, with an example of each kind of restriction.
- **(5)** Titles that carry the measure and the year, and alternative text that says what the chart shows and what it means.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **No year.** *Symptom:* ${{ g.all_rev|money }} on the matrix. *Response:* ask which year the title promises.
- **Region in the Filters pane.** *Symptom:* no slicer on the page. *Response:* ask how the manager would change the region.
- **Interaction set from the wrong end.** *Symptom:* the matrix still narrows. *Response:* ask which visual was selected when the option was chosen.
- **An unsorted bar chart.** *Symptom:* segments in no order. *Response:* ask what the meeting task needs.
- **Alt text that names the type.** *Symptom:* "bar chart". *Response:* ask what a listener would learn.
