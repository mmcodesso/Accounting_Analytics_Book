# Exercise 13.6: Reviewing a Draft Report Page

{{ header() }}

## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller asks for a review before a colleague's page goes to the board. None of its numbers is wrong. Each of its five elements misleads through a choice made by default or by habit: an encoding, an average of rates, an axis and a starting point, a second scale and a filter nobody can see. The exercise is Tutorial 13.3's review without the tutorial's help: students rebuild the draft exactly, say what a reader would conclude and why that is wrong, and replace each element. It is also where the chapter admits a limit: the correct discount rate needs a measure that Chapter 14 teaches. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- rebuild a described page without softening it (Requirement 1);
- state, for each element, the reader's conclusion, the error and the correct figure (Requirement 2);
- replace each element with an honest equivalent, and say what cannot yet be corrected (Requirement 3);
- apply contrast, a second cue, alternative text and titles (Requirement 4).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Leadership (communication skills); IFAC IES 4 (a) (professional skepticism and judgment) and IES 3 (b); AACSB A5 parts 5.1 and 5.2.

## Before you assign

- **Files.** The copy after Tutorial 13.3, whose Review page holds the corrected charts. The model answer is the pages Ex 13.6 Draft and Ex 13.6 Revised.
- **Data conditions.** Revenue is steady within a narrow band except the start-up month, the first of the window; services carry no promotion. Fiscal {{ g.y }} service revenue is the amount the hidden filter removes.
- **Verify first.** Students rebuild the draft exactly as described, with the hidden filter on the combo chart only. A softened draft hides the defects.

## How to run it

Pairs, about 90 minutes: 25 for the draft, 25 for the review notes, 25 for the revised page, 15 for accessibility. Ask each pair to write what a reader would conclude before they read any figure. Collect both pages and the notes, one for each element.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The draft page

**Step 1. Rebuild the five elements.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.6 Draft`.
b. Add a **Pie chart** with ItemGroup from **Item** as the slices and LineTotal as the value. Filter it to fiscal {{ g.y }} in **Filters on this visual**, and turn its labels off.
c. In **Table view**, select **SalesInvoiceLine** and click the Discount header. On **Column tools**, set **Format** to **Percentage** with 2 decimal places, so that the card shows the rate as the draft presents it.
d. Add a **Card** with Discount from **SalesInvoiceLine** in **Values**. Click the arrow beside it and choose **Average**. Title it `Average discount`, and filter it to fiscal {{ g.y }}.
e. Add a **Line chart** with Month in **X-axis** and LineTotal in **Y-axis**, with no filter.
f. Add a **Line and clustered column chart** with Month in **X-axis**, LineTotal in **Column y-axis** and DiscountAmount in **Line y-axis**. In **Filters on this visual**, select fiscal {{ g.y }} and every item group except Services.

**Check:** the pie has {{ n_slices }} slices, the card reads {{ avg|pct(2) }}, the line chart spans the whole window, and the combo chart shows twelve months on two axes. The format changes how the average is shown, not the average itself, which still weights every line equally.

### Requirement (2): The review notes

**Step 2. Pie chart.** A reader tries to rank the slices: {% for name, s in slices %}{{ name }} {{ s|pct }}{{ ", " if not loop.last else "" }}{% endfor %}. The large ones are clear and the small ones cannot be ranked by eye, which is angle's weakness (Cleveland and McGill). **Fix:** a bar chart sorted from the largest, with labels.

**Step 3. Average discount card.** A reader takes {{ avg|pct(2) }} as the discount rate. The card averages the rate of every line, so a small line counts as much as a large one. Discount dollars over the dollars before discount are {{ ratio|pct(2) }} ({{ of_list|pct(2) }} of list), and Furniture's average rate alone is {{ f_avg|pct(2) }}. **Fix:** the right figure is a ratio of totals. Power BI can calculate it, but a ratio that follows the reader's filters needs a measure, which the book first teaches in Chapter 14, so until then show the discount dollars beside the list amount, a reference total and not the rate's denominator. In **DAX query view**, the Checks tab's expression for the ratio is:

```dax jinja
EVALUATE ROW ( "Discount over dollars before discount", {{ g.dax_ratio }} )
```

**Step 4. Line chart.** A reader sees growth of {{ growth|pct(0) }} from the first month to the last of the window. The first month is the start-up month, ${{ start|money }}, against ${{ low|money }} to ${{ high|money }} in every other month, and the default axis starts above zero. Whole years changed by {% for c in changes %}{{ c|spct }}{{ " and " if not loop.last else "" }}{% endfor %}. **Fix:** an axis from zero, and whole years.

**Step 5. Combo chart.** A reader sees discounts as large as revenue, because the second axis stretches the discount line to the height of the columns. In {{ peak.month }} discounts were {{ peak.share|pct }} of that month's revenue, and {{ peak_shown|pct }} of the revenue the draft chart shows, which leaves Services out: name the denominator. **Fix:** one axis, or two charts.

**Step 6. Hidden filter.** Leaving Services out removes ${{ services|money }} ({{ services_share|pct }}) of fiscal {{ g.y }} revenue, so the chart's revenue totals ${{ g.shown|money }}, and nothing on the page says so. **Fix:** remove the filter, or make it a visible slicer and say so in the titles.

**Check:** a note for each element in the order *conclusion, error, correct figure*. **Watch for:** a note that calls a figure miscalculated. Every figure reproduces the data, and the notes say what the reader infers from them. A note that calls an element wrong for its stated purpose, or misleading, and names the weighting, the denominator or the scale behind it, is a full answer.

### Requirement (3): The revised page

**Step 7. Build the revised page.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.6 Revised`.
b. Add a **Clustered bar chart** with ItemGroup in **Y-axis** and LineTotal in **X-axis**, filtered to fiscal {{ g.y }}, sorted from the largest, with **Data labels** on, and the title `{{ g.revised_title }}`.
c. Add a **Card** with DiscountAmount and ListAmount from **SalesInvoiceLine** in **Values**. In **Filters on this visual**, select fiscal {{ g.y }}, because the revised page has no page filter. Set **Display units** to **None**, and give it a title that puts the discounts beside revenue at list price and names the year. The list amount is a reference total, not the denominator of the promotional rate.
d. Add a **Clustered column chart** with FiscalYear in **X-axis**, LineTotal in **Y-axis** and **Data labels** on.
e. Add a **Line chart** with Month and LineTotal, and under **Format visual > Y-axis > Range** type `0` in **Minimum**.
f. Add a **Line chart** with Month in **X-axis** and both LineTotal and DiscountAmount in **Y-axis**, filtered to fiscal {{ g.y }}.

**Check:** the cards read ${{ t.disc|money }} of discounts beside ${{ t.list|money }} at list price. Services is a bar of its own, and no filter is hidden. **Watch for:** the card left without its year filter. It then reads ${{ g.all_disc|money }} of discounts and ${{ g.all_list|money }} at list price, the amounts of every year. Do not add a page filter for the year instead, because the column chart of the fiscal years and the monthly line chart cover every year on purpose. Also DiscountAmount left in **Secondary y-axis** after switching to the line chart, which keeps the discount line climbing: drag it into **Y-axis** below LineTotal.

**Step 8. Apply the four practices and list the changes.**

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** Five notes, each in the order conclusion, error, correct figure, and each with a figure from the student's own report.
- **(3)** An honest equivalent for each element, and a candid statement that the discount rate waits for a measure. A strong answer shows discount dollars beside list amount in the meantime.
- **(4)** Titles that carry the measure and the period, alternative text that says what the chart means, data labels instead of color, and a contrast check.
- **Across the review.** Every figure reproduces the data. A weak answer calls a number miscalculated. Accept "wrong for the purpose" or "misleading" when the note names the weighting, the denominator or the scale.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Calling a figure miscalculated.** *Symptom:* "the card's number is incorrect", with no reason. *Response:* ask which figure disagrees with the data. "Wrong for the purpose", with the equal weighting of the lines named, is a correct note.
- **Only the axis fixed.** *Symptom:* a zero axis and the same growth claim. *Response:* ask what period the claim starts from.
- **A softened draft.** *Symptom:* the combo chart shows every group or all years. *Response:* ask what the description said.
- **Formatting as the fix.** *Symptom:* a review note that treats the percentage format as the correction. *Response:* percentage formatting does not change the equal weighting of the lines.
- **A second axis kept.** *Symptom:* a discount line at the height of the revenue. *Response:* ask which well holds DiscountAmount.
- **Alt text that names the type.** *Symptom:* "line chart". *Response:* ask what a listener learns.
