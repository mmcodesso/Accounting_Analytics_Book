## Purpose and learning outcomes

An auditor will not rely on a report whose data were profiled on a sample. The exercise repeats Tutorial 13.1's profile on four Tables and counts the difference: the first rows of the invoice tables are the earliest records, dated before any promotion and almost all paid, so they look cleaner and simpler than the Tables are. It also trains the judgment that follows a profile. A blank can be meaningful, a defect, or a follow-up, and a profile describes what is in the Tables without saying why. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- import Tables for profiling only, correcting the types that the first 200 rows get wrong, and keep them out of the model (Requirement 1);
- record empty and distinct values on the top rows and on the entire data set (Requirements 2 and 3);
- explain why the first rows are not representative (Requirement 3);
- classify blanks as meaningful, expected to exist or a follow-up (Requirement 4);
- document a profile in a memo with an attached table (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (extracting, transforming and cleansing financial data) and ISC M4 (use and management of data); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data governance and data literacy); IFAC IES 2 (f) (audit) and IES 2 (i) (judging the adequacy of information technology processes and controls); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Four queries that do not load

**Step 1. Import the four Tables.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook**, select CharlesRiver.xlsx, and in the **Navigator** select **T17_SalesInvoiceLine**, **T16_SalesInvoice**, **T44_Item** and **T4_Customer**. Click **Transform Data**.
b. In the **Queries** pane, rename them `Profile_Lines`, `Profile_Invoices`, `Profile_Items` and `Profile_Customers`.
c. In Profile_Lines, click the type icon on Discount, choose **Decimal Number**, and click **Replace current**. In Profile_Invoices, do the same for FreightAmount.
d. Keep every column. Open each query's context menu in the **Queries** pane and clear **Enable load**.

**Check:** the type entries of the model answer, with PromotionID left as Power Query typed it, because a profile needs no key:

```m
// Profile_Lines, step Changed Type
{"Discount", type number}
{"PromotionID", type any}
// Profile_Invoices, step Changed Type
{"FreightAmount", type number}
```

**Watch for:** Discount left as a whole number. Every rate rounds to zero, so Discount shows 1 distinct value on the entire data set as well as on the top rows, and the profile hides the very difference the exercise measures. A second slip is leaving the queries loaded, which shows as four extra tables in the **Data** pane.

### Requirement (2): The top 1,000 rows

**Step 2. Profile on the default basis.**

a. Select Profile_Lines. On the **View** tab, select **Column quality**, **Column distribution** and **Column profile**.
b. Check that the status bar reads `Column profiling based on top 1000 rows`.
c. Read the empty share of every column under its header, and the distinct count in the distribution. Select a column to read its statistics in the **Column profile** pane.
d. Record, for each query, every column that has empty values, and the distinct values of PricingMethod, Discount (Profile_Lines) and Status (Profile_Invoices).
e. Repeat for the other three queries.

**Check:** the left column of the table below. PricingMethod has 4 distinct values, Discount 1 and Status 1 (Settled). Each share is of the rows on its basis: PromotionID, empty on all 1,000 rows, reads 100.0%.

### Requirement (4): Meaningful, expected or follow-up

**Step 4. Classify three columns.** The model classification:

- PromotionID: a meaningful blank, because most lines carry no promotion.
- PaymentDate: meaningful on the 876 open invoices, a follow-up on the 35 Settled invoices that show no date.
- PriceListLineID and ShipmentLineID: empty on the same 439 lines, the Base List lines, which are design services priced from the base list and shipping nothing.
- CollectionName: empty on three Furniture items, a follow-up, because FUR-CON-0003 was ordered under the Alder promotion.

**Check:** a reason that cites the column and the table, not "it looks normal". Accept other reasoned classifications. The other Item blanks depend on each item's type and supply mode, so read them by those before calling them errors.

### Requirement (5): The memo

**Step 5. Write the profile.** One page in the form of Chapter 10's data profile: the Tables and the basis, the tests, the results of Steps 2 and 3 in an attached table, what changed, why the first rows mislead, the classification, and the conclusion. The conclusion is limited to what a profile can say: every line finds its invoice, item and customer, the blanks are explained or marked for follow-up, Quantity is fractional on 26,797 lines (Chapter 5's validity check, not a blank), and 218 distinct customer names stand for 220 customers (Brown LLC and Smith Ltd twice), so complete does not mean unique.

## What a strong answer says

- **(2) and (3)** The four columns that change, with both figures, and the columns that do not. The explanation is the billing order, the dates and the promotions, not "a sample is small".
- **(4)** Each classification names the column, the table and the reason. A strong answer separates "blank by design" from "blank that should hold a date".
- **(5)** The memo says what the profile covers and what it cannot say: it describes the Tables, and does not show why an exception occurred.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Discount left whole.** *Symptom:* 1 distinct value on both bases. *Response:* ask what the type icon shows and what a whole number does to a rate of `0.08`.
- **A conclusion from the top rows.** *Symptom:* "no line carries a promotion". *Response:* ask what the status bar says.
- **The wrong denominator.** *Symptom:* PromotionID's 1,000 empty values read against 27,933 rows, 3.6%. *Response:* ask which rows the share is of.
- **Every blank an error, or none.** *Symptom:* a list of blanks to fix, or "all fine". *Response:* ask which are blank by design.
- **Queries loaded.** *Symptom:* four extra tables in the model. *Response:* clear Enable load.
- **No attached table.** *Symptom:* figures only in prose. *Response:* ask where the auditor will look.
