## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller asks for a review before a colleague's page goes to the board. None of its numbers is wrong. Each of its five elements misleads through a choice made by default or by habit: an encoding, an average of rates, an axis and a starting point, a second scale and a filter nobody can see. The exercise is Tutorial 13.3's review without the tutorial's help: students rebuild the draft exactly, say what a reader would conclude and why that is wrong, and replace each element. It is also where the chapter admits a limit: the correct discount rate needs a measure that Chapter 14 teaches. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- rebuild a described page without softening it (Requirement 1);
- state, for each element, the reader's conclusion, the error and the correct figure (Requirement 2);
- replace each element with an honest equivalent, and say what cannot yet be corrected (Requirement 3);
- apply contrast, a second cue, alternative text and titles (Requirement 4).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Leadership (communication skills); IFAC IES 4 (a) (professional skepticism and judgment) and IES 3 (b); AACSB A5 parts 5.1 and 5.2.

## Before you assign

- **Files.** The copy after Tutorial 13.3, whose Review page holds the corrected charts. The model answer is the pages Ex 13.6 Draft and Ex 13.6 Revised.
- **Data conditions.** Revenue is steady within a narrow band except the start-up month, the first of the window; services carry no promotion. Fiscal 2026 service revenue is the amount the hidden filter removes.
- **Verify first.** Students rebuild the draft exactly as described, with the hidden filter on the combo chart only. A softened draft hides the defects.

## How to run it

Pairs, about 90 minutes: 25 for the draft, 25 for the review notes, 25 for the revised page, 15 for accessibility. Ask each pair to write what a reader would conclude before they read any figure. Collect both pages and the notes, one for each element.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The draft page

**Step 1. Rebuild the five elements.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.6 Draft`.
b. Add a **Pie chart** with ItemGroup from **Item** as the slices and LineTotal as the value. Filter it to fiscal 2026 in **Filters on this visual**, and turn its labels off.
c. Add a **Card** with Discount from **SalesInvoiceLine** in **Values**. Click the arrow beside it and choose **Average**. Title it `Average discount`, and filter it to fiscal 2026.
d. Add a **Line chart** with Month in **X-axis** and LineTotal in **Y-axis**, with no filter.
e. Add a **Line and clustered column chart** with Month in **X-axis**, LineTotal in **Column y-axis** and DiscountAmount in **Line y-axis**. In **Filters on this visual**, select fiscal 2026 and every item group except Services.

**Check:** the pie has five slices, the line chart spans the whole window, and the combo chart shows twelve months on two axes.

![A mock of the draft page as the exercise describes it, before any correction.](../figures/chapter-13-power-bi-essentials/IF-13-6-A.pdf)

### Requirement (2): The review notes

**Step 2. Pie chart.** A reader tries to rank the slices: Furniture 58.5%, Lighting 20.2%, Textiles 11.0%, Accessories 6.3%, Services 4.1%. The large ones are clear and the small ones cannot be ranked by eye, which is angle's weakness (Cleveland and McGill). **Fix:** a bar chart sorted from the largest, with labels.

**Step 3. Average discount card.** A reader takes 0.85% as the discount rate. The card averages the rate of every line, so a small line counts as much as a large one. Discount dollars over the dollars before discount are 1.20% (1.08% of list), and Furniture's average rate alone is 1.98%. **Fix:** the right figure is a ratio of totals, which needs a measure (Chapter 14), so until then show the discount dollars beside the list amount. In **DAX query view**, the Checks tab's expression for the ratio is:

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Discount over dollars before discount", CALCULATE ( DIVIDE ( SUM ( SalesInvoiceLine[DiscountAmount] ), SUMX ( SalesInvoiceLine, SalesInvoiceLine[Quantity] * SalesInvoiceLine[UnitPrice] ) ), SalesInvoice[FiscalYear] = 2026 ) )
```

**Step 4. Line chart.** A reader sees growth of 79% from the first month to the last of the window. The first month is the start-up month, $1,408,516.50, against $2,093,775.13 to $2,719,018.86 in every other month, and the default axis starts above zero. Whole years changed by +7.6% and +1.3%. **Fix:** an axis from zero, and whole years.

**Step 5. Combo chart.** A reader sees discounts as large as revenue, because the second axis stretches the discount line to the height of the columns. In October discounts were 6.0% of that month's revenue, and 6.3% of the revenue the draft chart shows, which leaves Services out: name the denominator. **Fix:** one axis, or two charts.

**Step 6. Hidden filter.** Leaving Services out removes $1,213,344.00 (4.1%) of fiscal 2026 revenue, so the chart's revenue totals $28,543,076.08, and nothing on the page says so. **Fix:** remove the filter, or make it a visible slicer and say so in the titles.

**Check:** a note for each element in the order *conclusion, error, correct figure*. **Watch for:** a note that calls a number wrong. Every figure is right, and the notes say what the reader infers from them.

### Requirement (3): The revised page

**Step 7. Build the revised page.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.6 Revised`.
b. Add a **Clustered bar chart** with ItemGroup in **Y-axis** and LineTotal in **X-axis**, filtered to fiscal 2026, sorted from the largest, with **Data labels** on, and the title `Revenue by item group, fiscal 2026 (all item groups, including Services)`.
c. Add a **Card** with DiscountAmount and ListAmount in **Values**, **Display units** set to **None**, and a title that puts the discounts beside revenue at list price.
d. Add a **Clustered column chart** with FiscalYear in **X-axis**, LineTotal in **Y-axis** and **Data labels** on.
e. Add a **Line chart** with Month and LineTotal, and under **Format visual > Y-axis > Range** type `0` in **Minimum**.
f. Add a **Line chart** with Month in **X-axis** and both LineTotal and DiscountAmount in **Y-axis**, filtered to fiscal 2026.

**Check:** the cards read $362,110.75 of discounts beside $33,589,137.45 at list price. Services is a bar of its own, and no filter is hidden. **Watch for:** DiscountAmount left in **Secondary y-axis** after switching to the line chart, which keeps the discount line climbing: drag it into **Y-axis** below LineTotal.

![A mock of the revised page, each draft element replaced by an honest equivalent.](../figures/chapter-13-power-bi-essentials/IF-13-6-B.pdf)

### Requirement (4): Accessibility

**Step 8. Apply the four practices and list the changes.**

a. Under **Format visual > General > Title**, give every visual a title that says what it shows and for which period.
b. Under **Format visual > General > Alt text**, say what each visual shows and what it means. The model answer's line chart reads: Monthly revenue over three years on an axis from zero. The first month, the start-up month, is far below the others; after it, revenue moves within a narrow band.
c. Check that no meaning rests on color alone: data labels carry the values, and the bar chart is sorted.
d. Leave the theme and check its text against the 4.5 to 1 contrast threshold.

**Check:** the list of changes names each practice and the visual it touched.

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** Five notes, each in the order conclusion, error, correct figure, and each with a figure from the student's own report.
- **(3)** An honest equivalent for each element, and a candid statement that the discount rate waits for a measure. A strong answer shows discount dollars beside list amount in the meantime.
- **(4)** Titles that carry the measure and the period, alternative text that says what the chart means, data labels instead of color, and a contrast check.
- **Across the review** None of the draft's numbers is wrong. The weak answer says it is.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Calling a number wrong.** *Symptom:* "the card is incorrect". *Response:* ask which figure disagrees with the data.
- **Only the axis fixed.** *Symptom:* a zero axis and the same growth claim. *Response:* ask what period the claim starts from.
- **A softened draft.** *Symptom:* the combo chart shows every group or all years. *Response:* ask what the description said.
- **Formatting the card.** *Symptom:* Average of Discount shown as a percent. *Response:* ask what a percent of an average is.
- **A second axis kept.** *Symptom:* a discount line at the height of the revenue. *Response:* ask which well holds DiscountAmount.
- **Alt text that names the type.** *Symptom:* "line chart". *Response:* ask what a listener learns.
