## Purpose and learning outcomes

The marketing manager wants the promotions by name, with what each gave away and when, before the next calendar is discussed. The exercise brings a fifth table into the model and then reads it carefully: a row labeled (Blank) is a meaningful blank and not a broken key, invoices dated after a promotion's end date are not violations, and one end date is simply wrong. It ends where the report stops: the cost of a promotion is on the page and its payoff is not. The analytics type is descriptive, with the first step toward diagnosis (where the discounts fell).

Students can:

- import and relate a lookup table whose key was typed with care (Requirement 1);
- build a matrix with two values and read a (Blank) row correctly (Requirement 2);
- compare invoice dates with a promotion's dates without mistaking billing for pricing (Requirement 3);
- spot a master-data error in a date (Requirement 4);
- state what the report shows and what it cannot (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results; data mining and analysis); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Strategy, Planning and Performance (performance management); IFAC IES 2 (b) (management accounting) and IES 3 (b) (communication); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The promotions, related to the lines

**Step 1. Import PromotionProgram.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook**, select CharlesRiver.xlsx, and in the **Navigator** select the Table **T7_PromotionProgram**. Click **Transform Data**.
b. Rename the query `PromotionProgram`.
c. Choose **Home > Choose Columns** and keep PromotionID, PromotionCode, PromotionName, ScopeType, DiscountPct, EffectiveStartDate and EffectiveEndDate.
d. Set EffectiveStartDate and EffectiveEndDate to **Date**, as Step 4 of Tutorial 13.1 did for InvoiceDate.
e. Choose **Home > Close & Apply**.

**Check:** the model answer's steps are these.

```m
    #"Removed Other Columns" = Table.SelectColumns(#"Changed Type",{"PromotionID", "PromotionCode", "PromotionName", "ScopeType", "DiscountPct", "EffectiveStartDate", "EffectiveEndDate"}),
    #"Changed Type1" = Table.TransformColumnTypes(#"Removed Other Columns",{{"EffectiveStartDate", type date}, {"EffectiveEndDate", type date}})
```

**Step 2. Relate the lines to the promotions.**

a. In **Model view**, drag PromotionID from **SalesInvoiceLine** onto PromotionID in **PromotionProgram**.
b. If a dialog opens, check that **Cardinality** reads **Many to one (\*:1)** and **Cross filter direction** reads **Single**.

**Check:** a line with 1 at PromotionProgram and an asterisk at the lines. **Watch for:** a key that is not whole number on the lines. The first 200 lines carry no promotion, so Power Query leaves PromotionID untyped until Step 3c of Tutorial 13.1 types it; compare the two entries below, and look at the type icon before blaming the relationship.

```m
// Profile_Lines: the type Power Query chose, because the first rows are empty
{"PromotionID", type any}
// SalesInvoiceLine after Tutorial 13.1, Step 3c
{"PromotionID", Int64.Type}
```

### Requirement (2): The matrix, and the (Blank) row

**Step 3. Build the matrix for the year.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.3`.
b. Select **Matrix**. Drag PromotionName from **PromotionProgram** into **Rows**, Month from **SalesInvoice** into **Columns**, and DiscountAmount and SalesInvoiceLineID from **SalesInvoiceLine** into **Values**.
c. Click the arrow beside SalesInvoiceLineID and choose **Count**.
d. Drag FiscalYear into **Filters on this visual** and select 2026. Give the matrix a title that names the year.

**Check:** the matrix lists 5 promotions and a (Blank) row; it scrolls sideways across twelve months. Furniture Seasonal reads Sep 167 lines $95,996.97; Oct 249 lines $136,935.59; Nov 119 lines $51,965.03; Dec 41 lines $11,155.99. The (Blank) row holds 8,726 lines, $26,479,419.83 of revenue and no discount: lines sold without a promotion, a meaningful blank (Chapter 5) and not a key that found no match. **Watch for:** the year left out. All 9 promotions appear and the (Blank) row grows to 25,825 lines.

### Requirement (3): Lines invoiced after the end date

**Step 4. Add the dates and read them against the months.**

a. Drag EffectiveStartDate and then EffectiveEndDate from **PromotionProgram** into **Rows**, beneath PromotionName.
b. Click the plus sign beside each promotion to show its dates.
c. For each promotion, compare the months that carry lines with its end date.

**Check:**

| Promotion | Start to end | Lines | Invoiced after the end date |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Textiles Seasonal | 2025-09-01 to 2025-10-31 | 10 | 10, in January |
| Wholesale Customer | 2025-11-01 to 2025-11-30 | 54 | 54, in January, February, March |
| Harbor Collection | 2026-03-01 to 2026-02-28 | 68 | 68, in March, April, May, June, August |
| Furniture Seasonal | 2026-09-01 to 2026-10-31 | 576 | 160, in November, December |
| Design Trade Customer | 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-30 | 88 | 27, in December |

The model answer confirms the count for Furniture Seasonal in **DAX query view** with this expression from the Checks tab:

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Furniture Seasonal lines after its end date", COUNTROWS ( FILTER ( SalesInvoiceLine, RELATED ( SalesInvoice[FiscalYear] ) = 2026 && RELATED ( PromotionProgram[PromotionID] ) = 8 && RELATED ( SalesInvoice[InvoiceDate] ) > RELATED ( PromotionProgram[EffectiveEndDate] ) ) ) )
```

**Watch for:** reading late lines as errors. The discount is set when the order is priced, so invoicing in November does not remove it: every late line of Furniture Seasonal was ordered within the promotion's dates (Chapter 5). The two earlier promotions have all their fiscal 2026 lines after their end dates, because they ended the year before.

### Requirement (4): The odd dates

**Step 5. Find the promotion that ends before it starts.** Read the date pairs of Step 4.

**Check:** Harbor Collection starts 2026-03-01 and ends 2026-02-28, so all 68 of its lines fall after its end date. They were invoiced from March to August and ordered in March and April, its likely season. An end date before the start cannot be right, and the order months make the end date the likely error, to confirm against the approved promotion. The lines carry the promotion because the order was priced with it, which does not by itself show that the discount was authorized. **Watch for:** concluding that discounts were given outside the promotion, or that the stored lines prove they were approved.

### Requirement (5): The memo

**Step 6. Outline the memo to the marketing manager.** It says that in fiscal 2026 the promotions gave away $362,110.75: Furniture Seasonal $296,053.58; Harbor Collection $30,478.57; Design Trade Customer $28,057.57; Wholesale Customer $6,858.14; Textiles Seasonal $662.89. By item group the discounts were Furniture $347,864.64, Lighting $7,825.54, Textiles $4,098.06, Accessories $2,322.51, with Services none. The item-group and collection promotions stayed in their own group, while Wholesale Customer and Design Trade Customer reached Accessories, Furniture, Lighting, and Textiles: a name does not say where the discount fell. The report shows what each promotion cost and when, and not whether it paid off: that needs the margin given up against the volume it added (Chapter 7 and the Part II case).

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** The (Blank) row is the lines sold without a promotion, with revenue and no discount: a meaningful blank, not a defect.
- **(3)** The months after each end date, and why they are not errors: pricing happens at the order. Chapter 5's finding about the orders is the evidence.
- **(4)** The promotion by name, the dates, the likely season from the order months, and the approved promotion as the record that would settle it.
- **(5)** Dollars, months and groups from the matrix, with the limit stated: cost without lift or margin.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **No year.** *Symptom:* 9 promotions and a (Blank) row of 25,825 lines. *Response:* ask which year the title promises.
- **A key of the wrong type.** *Symptom:* no relationship, or every line under (Blank). *Response:* ask what type the icon on PromotionID shows in each table.
- **(Blank) as a defect.** *Symptom:* a request to "fix" the row. *Response:* ask what a line sold without a promotion should show.
- **Late lines as violations.** *Symptom:* a memo that reports discounts outside the window. *Response:* ask when the order was priced.
- **Cost reported as payoff.** *Symptom:* "the Furniture promotion worked". *Response:* ask what volume the report shows.
