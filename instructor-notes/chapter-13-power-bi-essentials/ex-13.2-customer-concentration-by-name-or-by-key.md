## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller wants to know whether any customer is a concentration that the audit committee should hear about. The answer is no, and the exercise earns its place by what happens on the way: a table grouped by CustomerName treats two different customers as one, and the Top N filter that selects the ten largest is invisible on the page. Chapter 3's rule that a name is not a key meets Chapter 13's rule that a filter the reader cannot see belongs in the title. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- rank customers with a Top N filter inside a table visual (Requirements 1 and 2);
- explain a difference between a list by name and a list by key (Requirement 2);
- find the names that belong to more than one CustomerID and state their revenue (Requirement 3);
- compute concentration shares and compare them with a policy (Requirement 4);
- state a hidden filter in a title (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (data mining and analysis of financial data) and ISC M4 (use and management of data); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data literacy) and Reporting and Control (financial statement analysis); IFAC IES 2 (a) and IES 4 (a) (professional skepticism and judgment); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The ranked list by name

**Step 1. Build the table and its Top N filter.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 13.2`, as in Exercise 13.1.
b. Select **Table**, and drag CustomerName from **Customer** and LineTotal from **SalesInvoiceLine** into **Columns**.
c. In the **Filters** pane, drag FiscalYear from **SalesInvoice** into **Filters on this page** and select 2026.
d. Drag CustomerName into **Filters on this visual**, choose the filter type **Top N**, set it to the top 10 items by LineTotal, and apply it.
e. Sort the table by LineTotal, largest first, and give it the title `Top 10 customers by revenue, fiscal 2026 (grouped by name)`.

**Check:** ten rows. Brown LLC is ninth with $468,165.51, and the total row reads $5,345,943.80. **Watch for:** the year missing from the filters. The amounts and the total row then cover every year, and the page no longer ties to the Validation page.

![A mock of the two ranked lists side by side, which differ by one customer.](../figures/chapter-13-power-bi-essentials/IF-13-2-A.pdf)

### Requirement (2): The ranked list by key

**Step 2. Rank by CustomerID.**

a. Add a second **Table** with CustomerID, CustomerName and LineTotal.
b. Click the arrow beside CustomerID in **Columns** and choose **Don't summarize**.
c. Drag CustomerID into **Filters on this visual**, choose **Top N**, set the top 10 items by LineTotal, and apply it. Sort and title the table as in Step 1, with `(grouped by CustomerID)`.

**Check:** the tenth customer is Willis and Sons (200), $420,365.88. Neither of Brown LLC's two customers is on the list: CustomerID 173 ranks fifteenth and CustomerID 5 ranks 117th. The lists differ by one customer: the name list holds Brown LLC and the key list holds Willis and Sons, which grouping by name pushes to eleventh. The key is right, because a name is not an identity. The total row reads $5,298,144.17. **Watch for:** a lower total. If Top N ranked over a different set of years than the amounts show, the 2026 revenue of the ten best customers over all years adds to $4,751,841.46. Also CustomerID left at Sum. The table then groups by CustomerName alone, the list becomes the one by name, and Brown LLC shows CustomerID 178, the sum of 5 and 173.

### Requirement (3): Names that carry two keys

**Step 3. Count the CustomerIDs behind each name.**

a. Add a **Table** with CustomerName and CustomerID from **Customer**, and set CustomerID to **Count (Distinct)**. Sort it from the largest count.
b. Add another **Table** with CustomerName, CustomerID, CustomerSegment and Region from **Customer** and LineTotal. Filter it to fiscal 2026, and in **Filters on this visual** select the two names with a count of 2.

**Check:** the first table puts Brown LLC and Smith Ltd on top with a count of 2, and every other name has one: 218 names for 220 customers. The second shows Brown LLC twice: CustomerID 173 (Midwest, Strategic) $380,740.63 and CustomerID 5 (West, Design Trade) $87,424.88. Smith Ltd appears once, CustomerID 170, $14,093.15. **Watch for:** reading Smith Ltd as a second merged name. Only one of its two customers had fiscal 2026 revenue, so its by-name figure is the same as its by-key figure.

### Requirement (4): The shares

**Step 4. Divide.** A share is a ratio of totals, which a visual cannot give before Chapter 14's measures, so read the numbers and divide.

a. Read the largest customer's row in the by-key table and its total row.
b. Read fiscal 2026 revenue, $29,756,420.08, from the Validation page.
c. Divide, and compare with the policy's threshold of $1,487,821.00.

**Check:** the largest customer, Glenn-Estrada (136), holds 2.2% of revenue and the second, Wright PLC, 2.0%. The ten largest by key hold 17.8%, and by name 18.0%. No customer reaches the policy, and the largest is under half of it. **Watch for:** dividing by the all-years total, $86,446,845.84, which makes the largest customer 0.76% and understates every share.

### Requirement (5): The title

**Step 5. State the filter.** Under **Format visual > General > Title**, make each title name the ranking and the period, `Top 10 customers by revenue, fiscal 2026`, plus the grouping.

**Check:** a reader of the published page can tell that the table is a top ten and which year it covers. In **DAX query view**, the Checks tab's row for the name's rank runs this expression:

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Rank of Brown LLC by name", CALCULATE ( RANKX ( ALL ( Customer[CustomerName] ), CALCULATE ( SUM ( SalesInvoiceLine[LineTotal] ) ) ), Customer[CustomerName] = "Brown LLC", SalesInvoice[FiscalYear] = 2026 ) )
```

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** The two lists differ by one customer. Grouping by name merged two customers, so the list overstates one name and pushes a real customer out of the ten. A strong answer ranks by key and shows the name beside it.
- **(3)** Brown LLC is two customers, 173 and 5, with their revenue; Smith Ltd is two customers of which one sold.
- **(4)** The shares, the threshold and the conclusion that nothing reaches it. A strong answer notes that the by-name share is slightly higher only because of the merge.
- **(5)** A title that states the top ten, the measure and the year.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Summed CustomerID.** *Symptom:* a CustomerID of 178 on one row. *Response:* ask what a sum of identifiers means.
- **Ranking by name and calling it customers.** *Symptom:* Brown LLC reported as one customer at $468,165.51. *Response:* ask which CustomerIDs sit behind the name.
- **No year.** *Symptom:* amounts that match no fiscal 2026 figure. *Response:* ask which filter holds the year.
- **A share against the wrong total.** *Symptom:* a largest customer at 0.76%. *Response:* ask which revenue the policy refers to.
- **An unstated Top N.** *Symptom:* a title that says only `Customers`. *Response:* ask what the reader would think the table lists.
