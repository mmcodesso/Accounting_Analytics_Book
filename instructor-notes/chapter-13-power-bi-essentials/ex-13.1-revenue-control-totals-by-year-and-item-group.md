## Purpose and learning outcomes

Tutorial 13.1 reconciled the model to Chapter 6 once, on one page. This exercise adds a second, independent control before the report is shared: revenue by item group from the invoice lines, and the same years from the invoice table itself. The two agree on SubTotal and disagree on GrandTotal, and the reason is the lesson: freight and sales tax are billed on the invoice and are not revenue from the goods and services sold. It is also the chapter's first type trap, a column typed from its first rows. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- build a matrix with totals and tie its fiscal 2026 row to Chapter 6 and to the Validation page (Requirement 1);
- import a Table again under a new name, correct a type, and keep it out of the relationships (Requirement 2);
- explain why the lines reconcile to SubTotal and not to GrandTotal, and name the revenue amount (Requirement 3);
- argue for a control table that the model's filters cannot reach (Requirement 4).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (extracting, transforming and cleansing financial data; visualizing and communicating results); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques) and Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (i); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Before you assign

- **Files and software.** Power BI Desktop, CharlesRiver.xlsx, and the student's copy of Charles River Reports saved after Tutorial 13.3. A late student starts from End of Chapter 13 in Chapter13-companion.zip and follows its README (open the project, point the queries at CharlesRiver.xlsx, refresh). The model answer is page Ex 13.1 (the matrix, the header table and a Model answer text box) and the query InvoiceHeaders.
- **Data conditions.** SalesInvoice.SubTotal equals the sum of its lines' LineTotal, and GrandTotal is SubTotal plus FreightAmount plus TaxAmount. FreightAmount is whole dollars in the first 200 rows, so Power Query types it as a whole number, although 155 invoices of fiscal 2026 carry cents.
- **Verify first.** The copy's Validation page shows $29,756,420.08 as the fiscal 2026 line total.

## How to run it

Individual or pairs, about 60 minutes: 15 for (1), 20 for (2), 15 for (3), 10 for (4). Hold back the type correction until the GrandTotal check disagrees, and the argument of (4) until the table is built. Collect the Ex 13.1 page and the short note.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The matrix of revenue by year and item group

**Step 1. Add the page and the matrix.**

a. In **Report view**, click the plus sign beside the page tabs, double-click the new tab and rename it `Ex 13.1`.
b. Select **Matrix** in the **Visualizations** pane.
c. Drag FiscalYear from **SalesInvoice** into **Rows**, ItemGroup from **Item** into **Columns** and LineTotal from **SalesInvoiceLine** into **Values**.
d. Under **Format visual > General > Title**, type a title such as `Revenue (line totals) by fiscal year and item group`.

**Check:** the matrix has 3 year rows, five group columns and the total row and column, which the matrix shows by default. The fiscal 2026 row reads Accessories $1,872,273.66, Furniture $17,395,054.38, Lighting $5,997,536.93, Services $1,213,344.00, Textiles $3,278,211.11 for a total of $29,756,420.08: Chapter 6's revenue by item group and the Validation page's line total. **Watch for:** a later step puts FiscalYear from InvoiceHeaders on the matrix. The two tables are unrelated, so every row repeats the all-years total, $86,446,845.84, and the years no longer differ.

### Requirement (2): The header table, imported again

**Step 2. Import T16_SalesInvoice as InvoiceHeaders.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook**, select CharlesRiver.xlsx and click **Open**.
b. In the **Navigator**, select the Table **T16_SalesInvoice** only and click **Transform Data**.
c. In the **Queries** pane, double-click the query's name and type `InvoiceHeaders`.
d. Click the type icon on the FreightAmount header, choose **Decimal Number**, and click **Replace current**.
e. Choose **Home > Choose Columns** and keep SalesInvoiceID, InvoiceDate, SubTotal, FreightAmount, TaxAmount and GrandTotal.
f. Choose **Add Column > Custom Column**, name it `FiscalYear`, enter `Date.Year([InvoiceDate])`, and set its type to **Whole Number**.
g. Choose **Home > Close & Apply**.

**Check:** the **Applied Steps** end as the model answer's query does, with FreightAmount typed in the first Changed Type step.

```m
    #"Removed Other Columns" = Table.SelectColumns(#"Changed Type",{"SalesInvoiceID", "InvoiceDate", "SubTotal", "FreightAmount", "TaxAmount", "GrandTotal"}),
    #"Added Custom" = Table.AddColumn(#"Removed Other Columns", "FiscalYear", each Date.Year([InvoiceDate])),
    #"Changed Type1" = Table.TransformColumnTypes(#"Added Custom",{{"FiscalYear", Int64.Type}})
```

**Watch for:** the type left as Power Query chose it. The first 200 invoices have whole-dollar freight, so the first Changed Type step types the column as a whole number, as in the project's SalesInvoice query (the second entry below). The corrected entry is the first:

```m
// InvoiceHeaders, step Changed Type: the entry after Replace current
{"FreightAmount", type number}
// SalesInvoice, step Changed Type: the entry Power Query chose from the first rows
{"FreightAmount", Int64.Type}
```

The whole-dollar total is within a few dollars of the exact one every year, so a glance at a total passes. The error shows only as cents: the freight totals differ from the exact ones by $0.21, $0.67, $1.22 in the three years, 155 invoices of fiscal 2026 lose their cents, and the column has 533 distinct values instead of 901. **Replace current** matters as in Tutorial 13.1: **Add new step** would round first.

**Step 3. Build the header table and check the model for a relationship.**

a. In **Model view**, confirm that no relationship line touches InvoiceHeaders. If one appears, Autodetect new relationships was probably on in the copy: delete the line.
b. In **Report view**, add a **Table** visual and drag FiscalYear and SubTotal from **InvoiceHeaders** into **Columns**.
c. In **Table view**, select InvoiceHeaders and set the thousands separator and two decimals on the four amounts, as Step 9 of Tutorial 13.1 did.

**Check:** SubTotal by year is $27,313,723.48, $29,376,702.28, $29,756,420.08, equal to the matrix's year totals. **Watch for:** FiscalYear dragged from SalesInvoice: the header amounts repeat $86,446,845.84 on every year row.

### Requirement (3): Why GrandTotal does not tie

**Step 4. Add the other amounts.** Drag FreightAmount, TaxAmount and GrandTotal from **InvoiceHeaders** into **Columns**.

**Check:** fiscal 2026 reads freight $214,588.22, tax $1,948,116.12 and GrandTotal $31,919,124.42. GrandTotal exceeds SubTotal by $2,162,704.34, which is exactly freight plus tax, so the lines reconcile to SubTotal and not to GrandTotal. **Watch for:** reading GrandTotal as revenue overstates fiscal 2026 by that amount.

**Step 5. Name the revenue amount.** SubTotal is revenue from the sale of goods and services. Freight billed to the customer posts to its own revenue account, `4050`, and sales tax is collected for the state and posts to a liability, `2050`; the receivable, `1020`, carries the whole of GrandTotal. Chapter 10's trace of SI-2025-007947 showed the three postings.

### Requirement (4): Why InvoiceHeaders stays unrelated

**Step 6. Show that the control does not move with the model.** Add a table with ItemGroup from **Item** and SubTotal from **InvoiceHeaders**.

**Check:** all five item groups show $86,446,845.84, the all-years SubTotal: the Item filter has no path to InvoiceHeaders. That independence is what makes the table a control: its totals come from one table, so they test the model instead of depending on it. Relating it would not make the path ambiguous, but it would add a second table for the same invoices whose FiscalYear could filter the lines beside SalesInvoice's, and an Item filter would still not reach it. To check a student's model in **DAX query view**, run the expression of the Checks tab's row for the unrelated table:

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "SubTotal under a filter on Item", CALCULATE ( SUM ( InvoiceHeaders[SubTotal] ), 'Item'[ItemGroup] = "Furniture" ) )
```

(4) InvoiceHeaders is a control: its totals come from the invoice table alone, so they test the model instead of depending on its relationships. Relating it would not create an ambiguous path, but it would give the model a second table for the same invoices, whose FiscalYear could filter the lines alongside SalesInvoice's, and its header amounts would still not respond to an Item filter, which flows only toward the lines. Accept any answer that keeps the control separate for a stated reason.

## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** The fiscal 2026 row equals Chapter 6 group by group and the Validation page in total, and the answer says what that shows: the lines agree with the earlier work.
- **(2)** SubTotal by year equals the line totals exactly. A strong answer adds that annual totals could agree while single invoices differ, the point of Step 10 in Tutorial 4.1, and that the invoice-by-invoice test needs a relationship or a measure.
- **(3)** GrandTotal adds freight and sales tax. Revenue is SubTotal, because freight posts to its own account and tax is a liability.
- **(4)** A stated reason for keeping the control separate. Accept any such reason; reject "it would create an error".

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Two FiscalYear columns.** *Symptom:* every row of the matrix or of the header table repeats $86,446,845.84. *Cause:* the year came from the unrelated table. *Response:* ask which table each field comes from.
- **Freight left whole.** *Symptom:* the GrandTotal tie is off by cents. *Cause:* Power Query typed FreightAmount from its first rows. *Response:* ask what the status of the type icon said before the columns were chosen.
- **GrandTotal as revenue.** *Symptom:* $31,919,124.42 reported for fiscal 2026. *Response:* ask what the extra $2,162,704.34 consists of.
- **A relationship drawn "to be safe".** *Symptom:* a line in Model view on InvoiceHeaders. *Response:* ask what the control would then depend on.
