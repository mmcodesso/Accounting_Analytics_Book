## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller wants Chapter 6's reconciliation of invoice revenue to the ledger to be rerunnable at every year-end. The exercise practices a four-table inner join tested by its row count, GROUP BY with SUM, and a join from the ledger to its documents that names the document type. The analytics type is diagnostic: a difference is traced to the invoices that cause it.

By the end, students can:

- confirm that a four-table join keeps every row by comparing its count with the table's (outcome 1);
- total a subledger by group and agree it with a control total (outcomes 3 and 6);
- total the ledger by revenue account through the Account table, restricted to one kind of document (outcomes 1 and 6);
- explain a difference with the documents behind it, by joining ledger rows to the invoices (outcomes 1 and 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution BAR M10 (data models and joins) and Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts, transactions); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques) and Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping, financial statement preparation); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (i); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Before you assign

- **Files and software.** CharlesRiver_Work.sqlite and a script `Ex 10.1.sql`. Students need Tutorials 10.1 and 10.2 (joins, GROUP BY, the document type in ON, scalar subqueries) and Chapter 6's control total, $29,756,420.08.
- **Data conditions.** Every invoice line matches its invoice, item and customer. The lines are selected by SalesInvoice.InvoiceDate, since SalesInvoice has no fiscal-year column, and the ledger by GLEntry.FiscalYear, which follows the posting date. Revenue accounts are named by AccountNumber through the Account table, because AccountID is not the number. Freight is billed on the invoice header, so its 4050 postings carry no SourceLineID.
- **Verify first.** `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM SalesInvoiceLine;` returns 27,933 rows.

## How to run it

Individual, about 90 minutes: 15 for (1) and (2), 20 for (3), 25 for (4), 10 for (5) and 20 for the explanation. The routine for every step: type the two comment lines and the query at the end of the script, select them, click **Execute all/selected SQL** and read the message pane. Hold back the side-by-side hint of Step 7 until students have both totals, and the freight query until (4) is finished. Collect the script and the explanation.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The four-table join and its test

**Step 1. Join the lines to their invoices, items and customers.**

```sql
-- Requirement (1): the invoice lines with their invoices, items and customers
-- Population: SalesInvoiceLine, every line; expected: 27,933 rows, one per line
SELECT sil.SalesInvoiceLineID, si.InvoiceNumber, si.InvoiceDate,
    c.CustomerName, i.ItemCode, i.ItemGroup, sil.LineTotal
FROM SalesInvoiceLine AS sil
    INNER JOIN SalesInvoice AS si
        ON si.SalesInvoiceID = sil.SalesInvoiceID
    INNER JOIN Item AS i ON i.ItemID = sil.ItemID
    INNER JOIN Customer AS c ON c.CustomerID = si.CustomerID;
```

**Step 2. Count the lines, to test the join.**

```sql
-- Requirement (1): the number of invoice lines, to test the join
-- Population: SalesInvoiceLine; expected: 27,933, the rows of the join
SELECT COUNT(*) AS InvoiceLines
FROM SalesInvoiceLine;
```

**Check:** 27,933 rows from the join and 27,933 from the count, so no line was dropped. **Watch for:** a customer key matched to the invoice key returns 328 rows and no error. The comparison is the only alarm.

### Requirement (2): The year's lines by item group

**Step 3. Total the lines of the year by item group.**

```sql
-- Requirement (2): fiscal 2026 invoice lines by item group
-- Population: lines of invoices dated in 2026; expected: 5 groups, total 29,756,420.08
SELECT i.ItemGroup, COUNT(*) AS Lines,
    ROUND(SUM(sil.LineTotal), 2) AS Revenue
FROM SalesInvoiceLine AS sil
    INNER JOIN SalesInvoice AS si
        ON si.SalesInvoiceID = sil.SalesInvoiceID
    INNER JOIN Item AS i ON i.ItemID = sil.ItemID
WHERE si.InvoiceDate BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-12-31'
GROUP BY i.ItemGroup
ORDER BY Revenue DESC;
```

**Step 4. Compute the control total.**

```sql
-- Requirement (2): the control total of fiscal 2026 invoice lines
-- Population: as above; expected: 9,522 lines, 29,756,420.08 (Chapter 6)
SELECT COUNT(*) AS Lines,
    ROUND(SUM(sil.LineTotal), 2) AS Revenue
FROM SalesInvoiceLine AS sil
    INNER JOIN SalesInvoice AS si
        ON si.SalesInvoiceID = sil.SalesInvoiceID
WHERE si.InvoiceDate BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-12-31';
```

**Check:** 5 rows, Furniture $17,395,054.38, Lighting $5,997,536.93, Textiles $3,278,211.11, Accessories $1,872,273.66, Services $1,213,344.00; the control total is $29,756,420.08 over 9,522 lines, Chapter 6's figure. **Watch for:** `SUM(si.SubTotal)` over the join counts each invoice's subtotal once for every line: $55,120,357.09 from 9,522 lines of 7,171 invoices. Sum the line's own column.

### Requirement (3): The ledger by revenue account

**Step 5. Total the SalesInvoice postings of the year by revenue account.**

```sql
-- Requirement (3): SalesInvoice postings of fiscal 2026 to the revenue accounts
-- Population: GLEntry, SalesInvoice, fiscal 2026; expected: 5 accounts
SELECT a.AccountNumber, a.AccountName,
    ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS Revenue
FROM GLEntry AS gl
    INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
WHERE gl.SourceDocumentType = 'SalesInvoice'
    AND gl.FiscalYear = 2026
    AND a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
GROUP BY a.AccountID, a.AccountNumber, a.AccountName
ORDER BY a.AccountNumber;
```

**Step 6. Total the ledger's revenue.**

```sql
-- Requirement (3): the ledger total of fiscal 2026
-- Population: as above; expected: 29,761,002.45
SELECT ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS Revenue
FROM GLEntry AS gl
    INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
WHERE gl.SourceDocumentType = 'SalesInvoice'
    AND gl.FiscalYear = 2026
    AND a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080);
```

**Check:** 5 rows, 4010 $17,398,287.56, 4020 $5,998,649.16, 4030 $3,278,211.11, 4040 $1,872,510.62, 4080 $1,213,344.00; total $29,761,002.45. **Watch for:** without the SourceDocumentType condition the total reads $0.00, because the year's closing entry posts 5 rows to the same accounts and cancels the revenue. Adding 4050 to the list gives $29,975,619.11 and a difference of $219,199.03 instead of $4,582.37. A filter on `AccountID` with the account numbers returns 0 rows.

**Step 8. Find the invoices dated before the year whose revenue posts in it.**

```sql
-- Requirement (4): fiscal 2026 revenue postings of invoices dated before 2026-01-01
-- Population: GLEntry, SalesInvoice, fiscal 2026; expected: three invoices, 4,582.37
SELECT si.InvoiceNumber, si.InvoiceDate, gl.PostingDate,
    a.AccountNumber,
    ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS Revenue
FROM GLEntry AS gl
    INNER JOIN SalesInvoice AS si
        ON gl.SourceDocumentType = 'SalesInvoice'
        AND si.SalesInvoiceID = gl.SourceDocumentID
    INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
WHERE gl.FiscalYear = 2026
    AND a.AccountNumber IN (4010, 4020, 4030, 4040, 4080)
    AND si.InvoiceDate < '2026-01-01'
GROUP BY si.SalesInvoiceID, si.InvoiceNumber, si.InvoiceDate,
    gl.PostingDate, a.AccountID, a.AccountNumber
ORDER BY si.InvoiceNumber, a.AccountNumber;
```

### Requirement (5): Freight

**Step 9. Look at the freight postings.** The exercise asks only for the explanation; this query is the model answer's own.

```sql
-- Requirement (5): freight postings and their invoice lines
-- Population: GLEntry, 4050, SalesInvoice, fiscal 2026; expected: 1,628 postings, none with a SourceLineID
SELECT COUNT(*) AS FreightPostings,
    COUNT(gl.SourceLineID) AS WithSourceLine,
    ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS FreightRevenue
FROM GLEntry AS gl
    INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
WHERE a.AccountNumber = 4050 AND gl.FiscalYear = 2026
    AND gl.SourceDocumentType = 'SalesInvoice';
```

**Check:** 1,628 postings, 0 with a SourceLineID, $214,616.66. Freight is on the header, so no line can be matched to it.

## What a strong answer says

- **Requirement (4).** The ledger exceeds the lines by $4,582.37. The difference is three invoices dated in late 2025 and posted on 2026-01-01. The lines are selected by invoice date and the ledger by posting date, so these invoices fall in 2025 on one side and in 2026 on the other. Account by account, the ledger is the lines plus these invoices, so nothing is left unexplained. Exercises 5.2 and 6.1 decide, from the shipments, which side is right; this exercise explains the gap.
- **Requirement (5).** Freight is billed on the invoice header, not on a line, so its postings have no SourceLineID and no invoice line to match. The reconciliation covers the accounts that the lines feed; freight would be reconciled to the headers.

A weak answer says that the totals almost agree, or calls the difference rounding, or lists the invoices without saying that the two sides select by different dates.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **A wrong key in the four-table join.** *Symptom:* 328 rows. *Response:* ask what the count test says and which key the ER diagram names.
- **A header column summed over the lines.** *Symptom:* a total of $55,120,357.09. *Response:* ask at what level SubTotal is recorded.
- **No document type in the ledger query.** *Symptom:* a revenue total of $0.00. *Response:* ask which documents post to revenue accounts.
- **Account numbers used as AccountID.** *Symptom:* 0 rows. *Response:* ask which table holds the number.
- **The document ID alone in the join of (4).** *Symptom:* 8 rows and an invoice dated 2024-03-05. *Response:* ask which table the ID belongs to.
- **The difference called rounding.** *Symptom:* a note that the totals agree within cents. *Response:* ask what would explain $4,582.37 to the cent.
