## Purpose and learning outcomes

Chapter 8 aged the receivables once and found that the detail did not agree with the ledger. The audit committee now wants the aging and the reconciliation on the monitoring report, so the difference is followed at every refresh. The exercise rebuilds Chapter 8's work in Power Query, puts the ledger beside it, and asks what a monitoring report should do with a difference it already knows. The analytics type is descriptive (the aging) and diagnostic (the explanation of the difference).

Students can:

- build an open balance from an invoice, its applications and its credit memos, replacing the nulls the merge leaves (Requirements 1 and 2);
- age the open balances in Chapter 8's buckets at one date (Requirement 3);
- reconcile the detail to the ledger through AccountNumber and explain the difference (Requirement 4);
- find the invoices the open balance leaves out and say where they belong (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S2 M9 (audit procedures and audit data analytics) and Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions); IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping and statement analysis); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and (f) (audit); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Before you assign

- **Files.** The student's copy of Audit Monitoring after Tutorial 16.3, saved as `Ch16 Exercises.pbix`, and CharlesRiver.xlsx. The model answer is page Ex 16.1 of the project, the three queries named in the front matter, and the measures of the display folder Ex 16.1.
- **Data conditions.** Most open invoices have no application and no credit memo (SalesInvoice, CashReceiptApplication, CreditMemo), so the merges leave nulls. The ledger records AccountID, not the account number (GLEntry, Account). Account 1020 holds a line of the opening entry that no invoice supports. The part of a credit memo that exceeds an invoice's open balance is credited to account 2060, not 1020, so on an invoice already paid in full the whole memo goes there (Chapter 8).
- **Verify first.** The Account query of Tutorial 16.1 Step 9 exists in the copy (AccountID, AccountNumber, AccountName). Every query types the as-of date, 2026-12-31; a student who types another date in one of them reconciles to a different balance.

## How to run it

Individual or pairs, about 90 minutes: 20 for Requirements (1) and (2), 15 for (3), 25 for (4), 15 for (5), 15 for the note. The ledger query refreshes slowly, so tell students to start it early. Hold back the cause of the difference until the card shows it, and the home of the negative balances until students have counted them. Collect the .pbix and the note.

## Step-by-step solution

*The M blocks are the model answer's steps as the Advanced Editor names them, for comparison with a student's query. A Custom Column dialog takes the expression alone.*

### Requirement (1): The three imports

**Step 1. Import the Tables.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook** and select CharlesRiver.xlsx.
b. In the **Navigator**, select T16_SalesInvoice, T20_CashReceiptApplication and T23_CreditMemo, and choose **Transform Data**.
c. Rename the queries `ARInvoices`, `ARApplications` and `ARCredits`.
d. In each, choose **Home > Choose Columns**, keep the columns the exercise names, and set each date column to **Date**.

**Check:** each query ends with a Changed Type step on its dates. **Watch for:** the first Changed Type step types FreightAmount as a whole number, the trap of Chapter 13. No column kept here is affected, because Choose Columns drops it.

### Requirement (2): The open balance

**Step 2. Total the applications and credit memos by invoice.**

a. In **ARApplications**, filter ApplicationDate to dates on or before the as-of date, choose **Transform > Group By**, group by SalesInvoiceID, and name the sum of AppliedAmount `Applied`.
b. In **ARCredits**, filter CreditMemoDate the same way, group by OriginalSalesInvoiceID, and name the sum of GrandTotal `Credited`.
c. Clear **Enable load** on both.

Applied is the cash applied to the invoice through the as-of date, the sum of AppliedAmount: not a receipt's whole amount, and not net of credit memos or refunds. Credited is the sum of the credit memos' GrandTotal on OriginalSalesInvoiceID through the same date.

```
    #"Filtered Rows" = Table.SelectRows(#"Changed Type1", each [ApplicationDate] <= #date(2026, 12, 31)),
    #"Grouped Rows" = Table.Group(#"Filtered Rows", {"SalesInvoiceID"}, {{"Applied", each List.Sum([AppliedAmount]), type nullable number}})
```

**Step 3. Merge, replace the nulls and compute the balance.**

a. In **ARInvoices**, choose **Home > Merge Queries** with ARApplications on SalesInvoiceID and expand Applied; merge with ARCredits on SalesInvoiceID and OriginalSalesInvoiceID and expand Credited, both without the prefix.
b. Select Applied and Credited and choose **Transform > Replace Values**: replace `null` with `0`.
c. Choose **Add Column > Custom Column**, name it `OpenBalance`, and enter the rounded difference.
d. Filter InvoiceDate to the as-of date or earlier and OpenBalance to greater than 0.

```
    #"Replaced Value" = Table.ReplaceValue(#"Expanded ARCredits", null, 0, Replacer.ReplaceValue, {"Applied", "Credited"}),
    #"Added Custom" = Table.AddColumn(#"Replaced Value", "OpenBalance", each Number.Round([GrandTotal] - [Applied] - [Credited], 2)),
    #"Filtered Rows" = Table.SelectRows(#"Changed Type2", each [InvoiceDate] <= #date(2026, 12, 31) and [OpenBalance] > 0),
```

**Check:** the status bar and the card Open Invoices read 1,079 invoices, and Open Balance reads $4,418,804.32 over 203 customers. **Watch for:** the nulls. An invoice with no application or no credit memo leaves a total null, the subtraction returns null, and the filter drops the row. With both nulls left, ARInvoices keeps only the 6 invoices that have both, $6,413.86, and loses the rest, among them the 866 that nobody has paid or credited. The Checks tab proves the replacement with a count of those invoices.

### Requirement (3): The aging

**Step 4. Add the days past due and the bucket.**

a. Add the custom columns `DaysPastDue` (Whole Number), `Bucket` (Text) and `BucketOrder` (Whole Number, 1 to 5, for sorting), entering each expression below alone in the **Custom Column** dialog.
b. Choose **Home > Close & Apply**.
c. In **Report view**, select Bucket in the **Data** pane, and on the **Column tools** tab choose **Sort by column > BucketOrder**, as Tutorial 14.1 sorted MonthName by MonthNumber. Sorting by a column is a model setting, so it comes after Close & Apply.
d. Add the two measures below to the display folder Ex 16.1.
e. On a page named `Ex 16.1`, add a **Table** of Bucket, Open Balance and Open Invoices, titled "Receivables aged at 2026-12-31".

```
Duration.Days(#date(2026, 12, 31) - [DueDate])
```

```
if [DaysPastDue] <= 0 then "Current"
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 30 then "1-30 days"
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 60 then "31-60 days"
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 90 then "61-90 days"
else "Over 90 days"
```

```
if [DaysPastDue] <= 0 then 1
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 30 then 2
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 60 then 3
else if [DaysPastDue] <= 90 then 4
else 5
```

```
Open Balance = SUM ( ARInvoices[OpenBalance] )
Open Invoices = COUNTROWS ( ARInvoices )
```

**Check:** the table titled "Receivables aged at 2026-12-31" lists, in bucket order, Current $3,655,971.50; 1-30 $745,674.84; 31-60 $16,711.26; 61-90 $405.41; over 90 $41.31. The 3 balances over 90 days are residuals of invoices paid almost in full, the largest $40.85. **Watch for:** two quiet errors. `DaysPastDue < 0` moves the 10 invoices due on the as-of date ($28,964.63) from Current into 1-30 days. A Bucket that is not sorted by BucketOrder reads as text: 1-30 days, 31-60 days, 61-90 days, Current, Over 90 days.

### Requirement (4): The ledger

**Step 5. Import the ledger and keep account 1020.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook**, select T3_GLEntry, and choose **Transform Data**, never **Load**. Rename it `ARLedger`.
b. Keep GLEntryID, PostingDate, AccountID, Debit, Credit, VoucherNumber and SourceDocumentType, and set PostingDate to **Date**.
c. Merge with **Account** on AccountID and expand AccountNumber.
d. Filter AccountNumber to 1020 and PostingDate to the as-of date or earlier, then choose **Close & Apply**.

```
    #"Filtered Rows" = Table.SelectRows(#"Expanded Account", each [AccountNumber] = 1020 and [PostingDate] <= #date(2026, 12, 31))
```

**Step 6. Measure the difference.**

a. Add the two measures below to the display folder Ex 16.1.
b. Add a card of Open Balance, Ledger Receivables and Unreconciled Difference, and a table of SourceDocumentType and Ledger Receivables, titled "Account 1020 by source document".

```
Ledger Receivables = SUM ( ARLedger[Debit] ) - SUM ( ARLedger[Credit] )
Unreconciled Difference = [Ledger Receivables] - [Open Balance]
```

![A mock of the model answer's page, the aging beside its reconciliation to the ledger.](../figures/chapter-16-audit-monitoring/IF-16-1-A.pdf)

**Check:** the cards read $4,418,804.32, $4,798,804.32 and $380,000.00. In the table, the JournalEntry row is the difference exactly: one line, the opening entry JE-2024-000001, which no invoice supports. **Watch for:** the key. The ledger records AccountID, so a filter on AccountID equal to 1020 finds nothing: the Account keys run only to 104, and account 1020 is AccountID 3. Loading instead of transforming brings in the whole ledger, 815,921 rows, before the filter.

### Requirement (5): The credit balances

**Step 7. Duplicate the query with the filter reversed.**

a. Right-click **ARInvoices** in the query list and choose **Duplicate**. Rename the copy `ARCreditBalances`.
b. Open its filter step and change OpenBalance greater than 0 to less than 0.
c. Choose **Close & Apply**, add the two measures below to the display folder Ex 16.1, and show them on a card.

```
    #"Filtered Rows" = Table.SelectRows(#"Changed Type2", each [InvoiceDate] <= #date(2026, 12, 31) and [OpenBalance] < 0)
```

```
Credit Balances = SUM ( ARCreditBalances[OpenBalance] )
Credit Balance Invoices = COUNTROWS ( ARCreditBalances )
```

**Check:** the card reads 225 invoices and -$220,131.88. On these invoices the applications and credit memos together exceed the invoice total, and the excess, $220,131.88, is the customer credit that the credit memos record in account 2060, Customer Deposits and Unapplied Cash, not in receivables (Chapter 8); 222 of the 225 have refund records. On 197 the applications already equal the invoice total, so the credit memo came on an invoice paid in full. On 28 the invoice was only partly paid, and the credit memo splits between reducing the receivable in account 1020 ($26,599.42 in all) and the customer credit in 2060. The order of events varies. For 131 of the 225 an application is dated after the credit memo (134 application records), and for 98 even the first receipt is. SI-2024-000166 is one: an invoice of $8,148.44, $7,311.68 applied, and CM-2024-000005 of $2,584.25, which credits 1020 $836.76 and 2060 $1,747.49 on 2024-02-07, before the receipt of 2024-02-19 and the application of 2024-02-20. Do not call every one of them paid, then credited. **Watch for:** netting them into the open balance. The difference to the ledger then reads $600,131.88, which looks like a second finding and is not.

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** The aging by bucket, with most of the balance Current and the over-90 balances named as residuals, not collection problems.
- **(4)** The difference equals the opening entry's receivables line, which no invoice supports: Chapter 8's finding, presented as an audit finding. A strong answer adds what the report should do about it: carry the known difference as a reconciling item with its own disposition, so each refresh shows it unchanged and any other movement stands out.
- **(5)** The credit balances belong with the customers' deposits and refunds (account 2060), not in the aging.

A strong answer reconciles to the exact amount and names the entry. A weak one says "the ledger is higher".

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Nulls kept.** *Symptom:* 6 invoices. *Response:* ask what a subtraction with a null returns.
- **AccountID read as the number.** *Symptom:* an empty ledger table. *Response:* ask where the account number lives.
- **Boundary at zero.** *Symptom:* Current short by $28,964.63. *Response:* ask whether an invoice due today is past due.
- **Credit balances netted.** *Symptom:* a difference of $600,131.88. *Response:* ask which account holds them.
- **Buckets out of order.** *Symptom:* Current after 61-90 days. *Response:* ask what sorts a text column.
- **Typed as-of date forgotten.** *Symptom:* a query with another date than the rest. *Response:* ask which date the ledger query used.
