## Purpose and learning outcomes

Requirement 6 is where the case becomes a decision. The controller will carry the memo to the chief financial officer, so students must choose from five requirements' worth of results the few numbers that carry the argument, say what they do and do not show, and end with a recommendation someone can act on. The Part I case ended with a memo on whether the data could be trusted; this one ends with a decision. The analytics type is prescriptive.

By the end, students can:

- organize a short memo by perspective for a reader who will act on it;
- select the evidence that carries a recommendation, and state its limits;
- recommend a course of action with conditions and a way to learn more;
- support the memo with exhibits that a reviewer can trace to the workbook.

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 T5 to T6 (visualization and communication of results); IMA Leadership (communication skills); IFAC IES 3 (b), interpersonal and communication skills; AACSB A5 part 5.2 (technology judgment, interpretation and communication).

## Before you assign

- **From earlier work.** Requirements 1 to 5 complete, with their tables. No new data and no new Excel. Students write the memo and the appendix in a word processor; the model answer keeps its memo on a `Memo` sheet with the exhibits linked to the analysis sheets, which a student need not copy.
- **Length.** The memo is at most two pages and the appendix one. The model memorandum is a little under a thousand words, about what two pages hold.
- **The model's view.** It recommends declining the calendar as proposed: no promotion came near its break-even increase, and the calendar would cost about $350,000 of contribution against the 2027 budget. Students may reach a different recommendation from the same evidence; grade the reasoning, not the verdict, as long as it follows from the exhibits and states its limits.

## How to run it

Individual, about 60 minutes in class or as a take-home: 10 to plan the evidence, 35 to draft, 15 to check every number against its source and the length. In a workshop (the third plan of the case note), students swap drafts and review them against the grading guide below. **Collect** the memo, the appendix and the workbook.

## Step-by-step solution

### Phase 1: Plan the memo

**Step 1. Fix the reader and the decision.** The reader is the controller, who needs a recommendation for the chief financial officer. Write the recommendation first, in one sentence, and then select only the evidence that supports it or limits it.

**Step 2. Choose the evidence from each requirement.** Three to five numbers for each section, each traceable to a sheet:

| Section | What it must say | Source | Exhibit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial reporting | The cost, by year and as a share of revenue; why the ledger does not show it; when it reaches quarterly results | R2 Cost, R2 Gross to Net | 1, 2 |
| Managerial accounting | The effect on volume and on new customers; the break-evens; the cost against the budget; any alternative worth testing | R3 Volume, R4 Model, R4 Budgets | 3, 4 |
| Auditing | The exceptions and their dispositions; who approved; the conditions for running any promotion | R5 Controls | 5 |

### Phase 2: The three sections and the close

**Step 3. Financial reporting.** In three short paragraphs: the cost in all, $723,253.11, and by year ($126,607.64 in 2024, 0.46% of billed revenue; $234,534.72 in 2025, 0.80% of billed revenue; $362,110.75 in 2026, 1.22% of billed revenue); the ledger records revenue net, so account 4070 is unused and the income statement shows no sign of the cost, which only a gross-to-net schedule reveals; and the lag from order to invoice, which puts $228,114.18 (64%) of the proposed promotions' $354,589.72 in the fourth quarter.

**Step 4. Managerial accounting.** The differences from expectation run from -20.7% to +11.9%, all inside normal variation, and no first order came through a promotion. The break-evens run from 23% to 40%. The proposed calendar would cost about $354,589.72 of discounts, $347,840.88 of contribution, against a 2027 budget that plans no promotion. State the volume basis, and that the budget's quantities were not used. Offer one alternative, a small test at a discount near 4.5% (the rate the best measured increase would support), with a comparison group and a review afterward.

**Step 5. Auditing.** The exceptions with their dispositions (the collection promotions' dates as a master-data error, the two out-of-scope lines as an incomplete item master), the concentration of approvals in one role and the pending overrides that were billed, and the conditions that would make a promotion safe to run. Say which findings are design and which operation.

**Step 6. Recommend.** Decline the calendar as proposed, with the cost and the reason in one sentence. Say what would change the answer: evidence of a lift near a break-even, or a promotion designed to be measured. A test is offered only after the control changes of Step 5.

**Step 7. Build the appendix.** One page, five exhibits: the cost by year (R2 Cost), the gross-to-net schedule (R2 Gross to Net), the volume comparison (R3 Volume), the break-even results (R4 Model) and the summary of exceptions (R5 Controls). Each exhibit is numbered and cited in the memo.

**Check:** the memo names the report year's calendar and its three promotions (Harbor at 8%, Furniture at 10%, Design Trade at 12%), every number in it appears in an exhibit or a workbook cell, and the length holds.

> **Instructor notes, Requirement 6:** the evidence supports declining the calendar as proposed. None of nine promotions came near its break-even increase, the proposed three would cost about 350,000 of contribution against the 2027 budget, and about two thirds of that would land in the fourth quarter. A strong memo leaves room for a small, measured test: one scope, a discount near the roughly 4.5% that the best increase ever measured would support, a comparison with a similar scope or period, and a review afterward. It makes any promotion conditional on the control changes of Requirement 5.

## What a strong answer says

- **Opening.** The question and the answer in two sentences, before any detail.
- **Financial reporting.** The cost as a number and a share, the reason the income statement hides it, and the timing.
- **Managerial accounting.** The test and its result with the limits of the data, the break-evens set against the measured differences, and the cost against the plan.
- **Auditing.** Exceptions with dispositions, the cause and the fix placed in design or operation, and the conditions.
- **Recommendation.** A decision, the reason, what would change it, and a measured next step.

What separates strong from weak: a strong memo makes choices. It leaves out the findings that do not bear on the decision, states what the data cannot show (that promotions never attract customers, what would have been ordered without them), and keeps the recommendation consistent with the exhibits. A weak memo reports every result in the order it was found, hedges the recommendation, or recommends something the exhibits do not support.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Too long.** *Symptom:* three pages or a squeezed font. *Response:* ask which finding the controller would miss least, and move detail to the appendix.
- **A requirement-by-requirement report.** *Symptom:* headings that read Requirement 1 to 5. *Response:* ask what the controller's four questions were, and organize by them.
- **No recommendation, or a hedged one.** *Symptom:* "it depends". *Response:* ask what the student would do tomorrow, and what would change that.
- **A break-even presented as a forecast.** *Symptom:* "the promotion will need a third more orders". *Response:* ask what the measured differences were.
- **Overclaiming the customer test.** *Symptom:* "promotions never attract customers". *Response:* ask what a data set that begins in the first year can show.
- **Blame.** *Symptom:* a named individual at fault. *Response:* ask what design would have prevented it, and use the role.
- **Numbers that disagree.** *Symptom:* the memo and an exhibit differ by rounding or by year. *Response:* have the student trace each number to its cell and fix one source.
- **No limits.** *Symptom:* the results read as certain. *Response:* ask which assumption would reverse the answer.

## Grading guide

| Criterion | Points | Full credit | Partial credit |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Financial reporting | 20 | Cost, share, reason the ledger hides it, timing | Cost without the reason |
| Managerial accounting | 25 | Volume and customers, break-evens, cost against the plan, volume basis, alternative | Results listed without a conclusion |
| Auditing | 20 | Exceptions, dispositions, design or operation, conditions | Exceptions without causes |
| Recommendation | 15 | A decision with its reason, limits and a measured next step | A decision without limits |
| Exhibits and traceability | 10 | Five exhibits, cited, agreeing with the text | Exhibits missing or inconsistent |
| Form and audience | 10 | At most two pages, three sections, plain for the controller | Over length or unorganized |

## Extensions and discussion questions

- Write a five-sentence note to the sales team explaining the decision and what evidence would reopen it.
- Present the recommendation orally to the chief financial officer in three minutes, and name the one exhibit you would show.
- Discuss: the memo's recommendation is to decline. What would the memo say if one promotion had cleared its break-even by a wide margin?
