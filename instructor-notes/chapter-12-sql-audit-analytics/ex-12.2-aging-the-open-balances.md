## Purpose and learning outcomes

Tutorial 8.2 aged the receivables in Excel, and Tutorial 12.2 reconciled the goods received but not invoiced to account `2020` in total. The controller now wants both balances at the end of fiscal 2026 in SQL, so that they can be aged and reconciled at every year-end without a workbook. The audit mindset: a population is reconciled to the ledger before anything is built on it, an unexplained difference is the first finding, and how a balance is built decides what the test can see. The analytics type is descriptive for the aging and diagnostic for the reconciliations.

Students can:

- build an open balance from pre-summed receipts and credits, with the as-of date in a one-row CTE (Requirement 1);
- age it with CASE and julianday, reconcile it to account `1020` and explain the difference and the credit balances (Requirement 2);
- stratify customers by key and pick the key items (Requirement 3);
- reconcile receipts not yet invoiced to account `2020` and date the oldest (Requirement 4);
- report the two balances in a memo (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S2 M9 and Core S1 M1 to M3; IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping and financial statement analysis); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (f); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The open balance of each invoice

**Step 1. Subtract what was applied and credited.** Params holds the as-of date, Applied and Credited sum the receipts and credit memos of each invoice to that date, and Balances joins both to SalesInvoice and keeps the invoices dated by the as-of date. Every later test reuses these four CTEs.

```sql
-- Exercise 12.2, test A1: the open balance of each sales invoice at the as-of date
-- Population: invoices dated by 2026-12-31; expected: the open receivables
WITH Params AS (
    SELECT '2026-12-31' AS AsOfDate
),
Applied AS (
    SELECT SalesInvoiceID, SUM(AppliedAmount) AS Applied
    FROM CashReceiptApplication
    WHERE ApplicationDate <= (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
    GROUP BY SalesInvoiceID
),
Credited AS (
    SELECT OriginalSalesInvoiceID AS SalesInvoiceID,
        SUM(GrandTotal) AS Credited
    FROM CreditMemo
    WHERE CreditMemoDate <= (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
    GROUP BY OriginalSalesInvoiceID
),
Balances AS (
    SELECT si.SalesInvoiceID, si.InvoiceNumber, si.CustomerID,
        si.InvoiceDate, si.DueDate, si.GrandTotal,
        COALESCE(a.Applied, 0) AS Applied,
        COALESCE(c.Credited, 0) AS Credited,
        ROUND(si.GrandTotal - COALESCE(a.Applied, 0)
            - COALESCE(c.Credited, 0), 2) AS OpenBalance,
        julianday((SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params))
            - julianday(si.DueDate) AS DaysPastDue
    FROM SalesInvoice AS si
        LEFT JOIN Applied AS a ON a.SalesInvoiceID = si.SalesInvoiceID
        LEFT JOIN Credited AS c ON c.SalesInvoiceID = si.SalesInvoiceID
    WHERE si.InvoiceDate <= (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
)
SELECT InvoiceNumber, CustomerID, InvoiceDate, DueDate, GrandTotal,
    ROUND(Applied, 2) AS Applied, ROUND(Credited, 2) AS Credited,
    OpenBalance
FROM Balances
WHERE OpenBalance > 0
ORDER BY CustomerID, InvoiceDate;
```

**Check:** 1,079 rows, $4,418,804.32 from 203 customers. **Watch for:** without COALESCE (`a.Applied` and `c.Credited` as they come) an invoice with no receipt or credit has a NULL balance, and the query returns 6 rows: a clean-looking result that has lost almost the whole population.

### Requirement (2): The aging and the reconciliation

**Step 2. Assign each balance to a bucket.** The four CTEs stay; the final query is a CASE on DaysPastDue, the as-of date less DueDate.

```sql
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN DaysPastDue <= 0 THEN 'Current'
        WHEN DaysPastDue <= 30 THEN '1-30 days'
        WHEN DaysPastDue <= 60 THEN '31-60 days'
        WHEN DaysPastDue <= 90 THEN '61-90 days'
        ELSE 'Over 90 days'
    END AS AgingBucket,
    COUNT(*) AS Invoices, ROUND(SUM(OpenBalance), 2) AS OpenBalance
FROM Balances
WHERE OpenBalance > 0
GROUP BY AgingBucket
ORDER BY MIN(DaysPastDue);
```

**Check:** five rows: Current 780 invoices, $3,655,971.50; 1-30 days 258 invoices, $745,674.84; 31-60 days 28 invoices, $16,711.26; 61-90 days 10 invoices, $405.41; over 90 days 3 invoices, $41.31. **Watch for:** keeping `<> 0` in place of `> 0` returns 1,304 invoices and the oldest bucket reads 205 invoices and -$201,690.53, the credit balances. Aging from InvoiceDate puts 24 invoices in the oldest bucket.

**Step 3. Reconcile to account 1020.** A Ledger CTE sums Debit less Credit on account `1020` to the as-of date, joining Account on AccountID, and totals the opening line of JE-2024-000001.

**Check:** open invoices $4,418,804.32, account `1020` $4,798,804.32, difference $380,000.00, the opening line $380,000.00. The difference is the opening balance, an unsupported entry that Chapter 8 reported. Test A4 explains the credit balances: 225 invoices, -$220,131.88, 197 paid in full by the as-of date (76 of them in full before their first credit), 222 refunded; they are owed to customers through account `2060`, not negative receivables.

### Requirement (3): Customers

**Step 4. Total by customer and stratify.** A Customers CTE groups the positive balances by CustomerID; the final query assigns a stratum with CASE and counts customers and share.

```sql
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN OpenBalance < 1000 THEN 'Under 1,000'
        WHEN OpenBalance < 10000 THEN '1,000-9,999'
        WHEN OpenBalance < 50000 THEN '10,000-49,999'
        ELSE '50,000 and over'
    END AS Stratum,
    COUNT(*) AS Customers, ROUND(SUM(OpenBalance), 2) AS OpenBalance,
    ROUND(SUM(OpenBalance) / (SELECT SUM(OpenBalance) FROM Customers), 3)
        AS ShareOfBalance
FROM Customers
GROUP BY Stratum
ORDER BY MIN(OpenBalance);
```

**Check:** 203 customers in four strata: 32, $11,266.04; 65, $330,518.49; 83, $2,206,317.97; 23, $1,870,701.82. Test A6 lists the 23 customers of $50,000 or more, 42.3% of the balance. **Watch for:** grouping by CustomerName gives 202 customers, because two customers are called Brown LLC, and the stratum `1,000-9,999` reads 64 where 65 belong.

### Requirement (4): Receipts not yet invoiced

**Step 5. Sum the invoices to the receipt line, then compare.** InvoicedByReceipt sums the quantity invoiced for each receipt line, bounded by ReceivedDate; NotInvoiced keeps the receipt lines whose quantity invoiced is short by more than the tolerance and values the part not yet invoiced at the receipt value; test A8 compares the total with account `2020`.

```sql
InvoicedByReceipt AS (
    SELECT pil.GoodsReceiptLineID, SUM(pil.Quantity) AS QtyInvoiced
    FROM PurchaseInvoiceLine AS pil
        INNER JOIN PurchaseInvoice AS pi
            ON pi.PurchaseInvoiceID = pil.PurchaseInvoiceID
    WHERE pi.ReceivedDate <= (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
    GROUP BY pil.GoodsReceiptLineID
),
```

**Check:** 1,150 lines, $1,020,992.80 against $1,020,992.06 in the ledger, a difference of $0.74, the rounding of each invoice line's clearing amount. Test A8 adds the unrounded values and rounds once; the lines of test A7, each rounded to the cent, add to $1,020,992.73, so a student who totals the A7 column finds a few cents less, which is display rounding. **Watch for:** three wrong versions run.

- Joining the invoice lines without summing returns 21,738 lines and $21,076,540.66.
- Exact equality instead of the tolerance adds noise: 1,983 lines.
- The full receipt value of each open line gives $1,529,459.73.

**Step 6. Date and rank them.** Test A9 totals by receipt month and test A10 lists the five largest suppliers.

**Check:** 5 months, December largest at $804,428.99. The 3 months from August to October hold 87 lines, $27,668.25, more than two months old. The first three suppliers are 17 ($64,236.70), 52 ($49,449.79), 27 ($47,596.03), the first to call.

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** Almost everything past due is under a month late. The difference is the unsupported opening line, a finding to investigate and, if unsupported, to write off. The credit balances are liabilities to customers, not receivables.
- **(3)** The key items are the customers of $50,000 or more, chosen by key, not name.
- **(4)** The receipts agree with account `2020` except for rounding, which supports the liability but not its completeness: a receipt never recorded is missing from both. Old receipts need follow-up.
- **(5)** Each number comes with a disposition. A weak memo repeats the tables.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **A balance without COALESCE.** *Symptom:* 6 rows. *Response:* ask what a NULL does in subtraction.
- **Aging from the invoice date.** *Symptom:* 24 invoices over 90 days. *Response:* ask what "past due" is measured from.
- **Negative balances kept.** *Symptom:* an oldest bucket that is negative. *Response:* ask who owes whom.
- **Customers by name.** *Symptom:* 202 customers. *Response:* ask what identifies a customer.
- **Receipts joined to invoice lines directly.** *Symptom:* 21,738 lines. *Response:* ask how many invoice lines one receipt line has.
- **The difference called an error.** *Symptom:* a memo that asks for a correction. *Response:* ask what entry the amount equals.
