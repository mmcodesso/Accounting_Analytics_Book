## Purpose and learning outcomes

Tutorial 12.1 listed the invoices and pay periods that cross a year-end. The controller now needs the dollars before the fiscal 2026 statements are final. The exercise turns cutoff tests into measurements. The audit mindset: a cutoff test compares two dates of one transaction, no rows is evidence only if the test could have returned some, and an exception becomes a finding when its effect is measured and a disposition recorded. The analytics type is diagnostic; the estimate in (4) is a small expectation, the chapter's predictive element.

Students can:

- separate cutoff errors from invoice-dating errors with the delivery date (Requirement 1);
- measure each year's revenue effect and explain why margin moves by the whole amount (Requirement 2);
- say which date posts supplier invoices (Requirement 3);
- find work paid in a later year, estimate the unrecorded accrual and state assumptions (Requirement 4);
- write the memo with a recommendation (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S2 M9 and Core S1 M1 to M3; IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping and internal control); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (f); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Invoices posted outside the year of delivery

**Step 1. Compare the posting year with the delivery year.** The Shipped and Posted CTEs of test L5 give each invoice its first shipment date, last delivery date and first posting date.

```sql
-- Exercise 12.1, test C1: revenue posted in a different fiscal year from the delivery
-- Population: invoices for shipped goods (test L5); expected: no rows
WITH Shipped AS (
    SELECT sil.SalesInvoiceID, MIN(s.ShipmentDate) AS ShipmentDate,
        MAX(s.DeliveryDate) AS DeliveryDate
    FROM SalesInvoiceLine AS sil
        INNER JOIN ShipmentLine AS sl
            ON sl.ShipmentLineID = sil.ShipmentLineID
        INNER JOIN Shipment AS s ON s.ShipmentID = sl.ShipmentID
    GROUP BY sil.SalesInvoiceID
),
Posted AS (
    SELECT SourceDocumentID AS SalesInvoiceID,
        MIN(PostingDate) AS PostingDate
    FROM GLEntry
    WHERE SourceDocumentType = 'SalesInvoice'
    GROUP BY SourceDocumentID
)
SELECT si.InvoiceNumber, si.InvoiceDate, sh.ShipmentDate,
    sh.DeliveryDate, p.PostingDate, ROUND(si.SubTotal, 2) AS SubTotal
FROM SalesInvoice AS si
    INNER JOIN Shipped AS sh ON sh.SalesInvoiceID = si.SalesInvoiceID
    INNER JOIN Posted AS p ON p.SalesInvoiceID = si.SalesInvoiceID
WHERE strftime('%Y', p.PostingDate) <> strftime('%Y', sh.DeliveryDate)
ORDER BY p.PostingDate, si.InvoiceNumber;
```

**Check:** 23 rows: 15 invoices for deliveries of 2024, $90,138.93; 8 invoices for deliveries of 2025, $19,540.86. **Watch for:** three wrong versions run without an error.

- The invoice year, `si.InvoiceDate`, in place of the delivery year returns 6 rows and only 1 cutoff error: most December deliveries were invoiced in January.
- Joining at shipment-line grain, with the plain dates in place of MIN and MAX and no GROUP BY, returns 24 rows and $130,723.77 instead of 23 and $109,679.79: SI-2025-006743 bills two lines of one shipment, so its subtotal counts twice. Deleting only the GROUP BY leaves MIN and MAX as global aggregates and returns 0 rows.
- The inner joins drop the design-service invoices, so a clean result says nothing about services.

**Step 2. List the invoices dated before their shipment.** The same CTEs with `WHERE si.InvoiceDate < sh.ShipmentDate` and a CASE column that marks the invoice test C1 also lists.

**Check:** 9 rows. SI-2026-013742 is marked: dated before the year-end, posted after it.

### Requirement (2): The effect on each year

**Step 3. Net the effects by fiscal year.** Late holds the test C1 invoices, and Unbilled values the shipment lines with no invoice line at the order price, quantity shipped times UnitPrice times one less Discount. Effects stacks the amounts with UNION ALL and the final query nets them.

```sql
Effects AS (
    SELECT PostingYear AS FiscalYear, SubTotal AS FromYearBefore,
        0 AS ToNextYear, 0 AS NeverInvoiced
    FROM Late
    UNION ALL
    SELECT DeliveryYear, 0, SubTotal, 0
    FROM Late
    UNION ALL
    SELECT ShipmentYear, 0, 0, SalesValue
    FROM Unbilled
)
SELECT FiscalYear, ROUND(SUM(FromYearBefore), 2) AS FromYearBefore,
    ROUND(SUM(ToNextYear), 2) AS ToNextYear,
    ROUND(SUM(NeverInvoiced), 2) AS NeverInvoiced,
    ROUND(SUM(FromYearBefore) - SUM(ToNextYear) - SUM(NeverInvoiced), 2)
        AS NetEffect
FROM Effects
GROUP BY FiscalYear
ORDER BY FiscalYear;
```

![A mock of the net effect of cutoff on each fiscal year, the result that carries the memo.](../figures/chapter-12-sql-audit-analytics/IF-12-1-A.pdf)

**Check:** 3 rows: 2024, -$90,138.93; 2025, +$70,598.07; 2026, -$24,940.25. A negative figure is revenue recorded below the year's deliveries. **Watch for:** the last row changes with the method.

- Without Unbilled the last year reads +$19,540.86, the wrong sign.
- Valuing the unbilled lines at ExtendedStandardCost gives -$3,354.92: revenue is measured at the price, not the cost.

**Step 4. Test where the cost is posted.** Test C4 counts Shipment postings dated on another day than the shipment, and totals the unbilled lines.

**Check:** 0 postings on another date; 9 unbilled lines with $22,895.78 of cost posted and $44,481.11 of revenue missing.

### Requirement (3): Supplier invoices

**Step 5. Find the date they are posted on.**

```sql
-- Exercise 12.1, test C5: supplier invoices by the date their ledger rows are posted on
-- Population: all supplier invoices; expected: posted on one date, in the year of the invoice
WITH Posted AS (
    SELECT SourceDocumentID AS PurchaseInvoiceID,
        MIN(PostingDate) AS FirstPosting, MAX(PostingDate) AS LastPosting
    FROM GLEntry
    WHERE SourceDocumentType = 'PurchaseInvoice'
    GROUP BY SourceDocumentID
)
SELECT COUNT(*) AS Invoices,
    SUM(CASE WHEN p.FirstPosting = pi.InvoiceDate
        AND p.LastPosting = pi.InvoiceDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
        AS PostedOnInvoiceDate,
    SUM(CASE WHEN p.FirstPosting = pi.ReceivedDate
        AND p.LastPosting = pi.ReceivedDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
        AS PostedOnReceivedDate,
    SUM(CASE WHEN strftime('%Y', p.FirstPosting)
        <> strftime('%Y', pi.InvoiceDate) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
        AS PostedInAnotherYear
FROM PurchaseInvoice AS pi
    INNER JOIN Posted AS p ON p.PurchaseInvoiceID = pi.PurchaseInvoiceID;
```

**Check:** 40,204 invoices, 40,204 posted on the received date, 15,406 on the invoice date, 1 in another year than dated. **Watch for:** comparing full dates reads 24,798 invoices as exceptions; the test is about the year.

**Step 6. List the crossing invoice.** Test C6 returns it with the journal entry its line cites in AccrualJournalEntryID: ASV0050-2025-040073, supplier 50, $3,266.27, accrued by JE-2025-000188. It is not an unrecorded liability, although the accrual's amount and clearing are still to review.

### Requirement (4): Late-December payroll

**Step 7. Total hourly labor by work year and paid fiscal year.**

```sql
-- Exercise 12.1, test C7: hourly labor cost by the year of the work and of its pay period
-- Population: all labor time; expected: work and pay in the same fiscal year
SELECT strftime('%Y', lt.WorkDate) AS WorkYear, pp.FiscalYear AS PaidYear,
    MIN(lt.WorkDate) AS FirstWorkDate, MAX(lt.WorkDate) AS LastWorkDate,
    ROUND(SUM(lt.ExtendedLaborCost), 2) AS LaborCost,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN lt.LaborType <> 'NonManufacturing'
        THEN lt.ExtendedLaborCost ELSE 0 END), 2) AS ManufacturingCost
FROM LaborTimeEntry AS lt
    INNER JOIN PayrollPeriod AS pp
        ON pp.PayrollPeriodID = lt.PayrollPeriodID
GROUP BY WorkYear, PaidYear
ORDER BY WorkYear, PaidYear;
```

**Check:** 5 rows, two of them crossing: work of 2024 paid in 2025, $117,073.89 ($98,041.68 manufacturing); work of 2025 paid in 2026, $138,290.66 ($117,800.12 manufacturing).
**Step 8. Estimate the unrecorded days.** Test C9 builds Params (the dates), LastPeriods (the last 8 processed periods), PeriodCost and WorkingDays (the plant calendar), then divides the cost by the working days of the periods and multiplies by the unrecorded days.

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) AS Periods,
    ROUND(AVG(PayrollCost), 2) AS AveragePeriodCost,
    ROUND(AVG(ManufacturingCost), 2) AS AverageManufacturing,
    (SELECT InPeriods FROM WorkingDays) AS WorkingDays,
    (SELECT Unrecorded FROM WorkingDays) AS UnrecordedDays,
    ROUND(SUM(PayrollCost) / (SELECT InPeriods FROM WorkingDays)
        * (SELECT Unrecorded FROM WorkingDays), 2) AS EstimatedCost,
    ROUND(SUM(ManufacturingCost) / (SELECT InPeriods FROM WorkingDays)
        * (SELECT Unrecorded FROM WorkingDays), 2)
        AS EstimatedManufacturing
FROM PeriodCost;
```

**Check:** 8 periods averaging $287,283.61, 78 working days in them, 13 unrecorded, an estimate of $383,044.82 ($197,970.68 manufacturing). **Watch for:** two average periods give $574,567.22, but only 13 working days of the open periods are in the data. An alternative allocates the hourly earnings over the 13 plant working days of the 78 and the salaries over 14 weekdays of the 80, then applies the employer-charge rate: $391,916.16, $198,740.98 of it manufacturing. Accept it if the method is stated.

Two extras: C8 estimates the full cost of the work paid in 2025 and 2026 ($295,972.91 and $334,886.38), and C10 shows account `2030` at $95,000.00, the opening line, at every year-end. The data show that the accrual is omitted, not whether the opening credit is separately owed: if it is unsupported and reversed, each net increase is $95,000.00 smaller. Account `2030` holds net pay only, so the omitted cost is not a credit to `2030` alone.

### Requirement (5): The memo

**Step 9. Write the half page.** Revenue (effect by year, unbilled lines, dating errors apart); supplier invoices (posted on receipt, the one crossing invoice accrued); payroll (wages recorded when paid, no additional accrual, the opening credit of `2030` investigated separately; the change between year-end amounts, about $38,913.47 in 2025 and $48,158.44 in 2026, from different methods, so rough, and the 2024 amount not a known annual effect); a recommendation.

## What a strong answer says

- **(2)** Understated revenue in 2024, overstated in 2025, understated in 2026; the unbilled shipments decide the last sign. Cost posts at shipment, so margin moves by the whole revenue amount.
- **(3)** Supplier invoices post on receipt, so the payables cutoff risk is the receipt date. The limit: the test covers recorded invoices, and a receipt omitted from both the receiving records and the ledger is outside it, so unrecorded liabilities need independent receiving evidence, supplier statements and later invoices or payments. A plus: the invoice on both lists of (1), and the crossing invoice with its accrual.
- **(4)** No additional accrual is recorded, so each close omits about a pay period of cost. A strong answer states its method, calls the estimate rough, and keeps the opening credit of `2030` a separate question.
- **(5)** Effects by year, dating errors kept apart, the 2026 entries proposed, a recurring cutoff report recommended. A weak memo lists tests, not effects.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Invoice date for delivery date.** *Symptom:* 6 rows, one real error. *Response:* ask which date shows when control passed.
- **A fan-out in Shipped.** *Symptom:* 24 rows. *Response:* ask how many shipment lines an invoice can bill.
- **The sign of the 2026 effect.** *Symptom:* +$19,540.86. *Response:* ask what the unbilled shipments did to revenue.
