## Purpose and learning outcomes

Tutorial 11.2 showed Packing and Quality Assurance recording several times their planned hours, and Tutorial 12.3 showed that much direct time is recorded after its operation ended. The plant manager asks what the comparison looks like when only the time recorded while an operation was open is counted, and what the plant was doing in the first months of the data. The audit mindset: when a measure depends on a field of doubtful quality, test the field first, then repeat the measure on the part that can be trusted. A ratio that fails because of how time was recorded is not evidence of inefficiency. The analytics type is diagnostic.

Students can:

- flag late entries by comparing WorkDate with ActualEndDate (Requirement 1);
- repeat a comparison on the trustworthy part and rank the work centers both ways (Requirements 2 and 3);
- build a monthly fact table from separate CTEs and read a stock build in it (Requirement 4);
- say what the operation-level data can and cannot support (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution BAR M5 (cost accounting and variance analysis) and Core S1 M8 (data mining and analysis); IMA Cost Accounting and Operational Knowledge; IFAC IES 2 (b) and IES 3 (a); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Flag the late entries

**Step 1. Flag each direct entry, then count by year.** A Flagged CTE joins LaborTimeEntry to its operation and adds two 1-or-0 flags with CASE; the final query counts them by the year of WorkDate.

```sql
-- Exercise 12.3, test T1: direct labor time flagged as recorded while the operation was open
-- Population: direct labor time with an operation; expected: recorded by the operation's end
WITH Flagged AS (
    SELECT lt.LaborTimeEntryID, lt.WorkDate, op.ActualEndDate,
        lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours AS Hours,
        CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate <= op.ActualEndDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
            AS WhileOpen,
        CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate < op.ActualStartDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
            AS BeforeStart
    FROM LaborTimeEntry AS lt
        INNER JOIN WorkOrderOperation AS op
            ON op.WorkOrderOperationID = lt.WorkOrderOperationID
    WHERE lt.LaborType = 'Direct Manufacturing'
)
SELECT strftime('%Y', WorkDate) AS WorkYear, COUNT(*) AS Entries,
    SUM(WhileOpen) AS EntriesWhileOpen, SUM(BeforeStart) AS BeforeStart,
    ROUND(SUM(Hours), 0) AS DirectHours,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN WhileOpen = 1 THEN Hours ELSE 0 END), 0)
        AS HoursWhileOpen,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN WhileOpen = 0 THEN Hours ELSE 0 END)
        / SUM(Hours), 3) AS ShareAfterEnd
FROM Flagged
GROUP BY WorkYear
ORDER BY WorkYear;
```

**Check:** three rows (entries, entries while open, entries before the start, hours, hours while open, share after the end): 2024, 21,401, 19,644, 0, 60,442, 48,677, 0.195; 2025, 15,791, 11,535, 0, 57,557, 28,870, 0.498; 2026, 13,905, 8,082, 0, 62,742, 21,477, 0.658. **Watch for:** two wrong versions.

- Written with `<` in place of `<=`, every entry counts as late: 0 entries while open and a share of 1.000 in every year. Time is recorded on the operation's last day or after it, never before, so the day itself must count as open.
- Without the LaborType filter the result is the same, because indirect time has no operation and the inner join drops it. The filter is harmless here and a safeguard on other data.

### Requirement (2): The work-center comparison

**Step 2. Sum the entries to the operation, then join.** The derived table adds each operation's hours, all and while open, before the planned hours are summed, so the planned hours are counted once. The WHERE keeps the operations that ended by the last day with time records, and the LEFT JOIN keeps an operation with no direct time in the plan.

```sql
SELECT strftime('%Y', op.ActualEndDate) AS EndYear,
    wc.WorkCenterName,
    SUM(op.PlannedLoadHours) AS PlannedHours,
    SUM(lab.Hours) AS RecordedHours,
    SUM(lab.OpenHours) AS OpenHours,
    ROUND(SUM(lab.OpenHours) / SUM(op.PlannedLoadHours), 2)
        AS OpenPerPlanned
FROM WorkOrderOperation AS op
    INNER JOIN WorkCenter AS wc ON wc.WorkCenterID = op.WorkCenterID
    LEFT JOIN (
        SELECT lt.WorkOrderOperationID,
            SUM(lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours) AS Hours,
            SUM(CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate <= o.ActualEndDate
                THEN lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours ELSE 0 END)
                AS OpenHours
        FROM LaborTimeEntry AS lt
            INNER JOIN WorkOrderOperation AS o
                ON o.WorkOrderOperationID = lt.WorkOrderOperationID
        WHERE lt.LaborType = 'Direct Manufacturing'
        GROUP BY lt.WorkOrderOperationID
    ) AS lab ON lab.WorkOrderOperationID = op.WorkOrderOperationID
WHERE op.ActualEndDate <= (SELECT MAX(WorkDate) FROM LaborTimeEntry)
GROUP BY EndYear, wc.WorkCenterName
```

### Requirement (3): The ranking

**Step 3. Rank both ways.** Test T3 wraps the same derived table and adds two RANK() functions partitioned by year; the quote is the start of its outer query.

```sql
SELECT EndYear, WorkCenterName,
    ROUND(RecordedHours / PlannedHours, 2) AS RecordedPerPlanned,
    RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY EndYear
        ORDER BY RecordedHours / PlannedHours DESC) AS AllHoursRank,
    OpenPerPlanned,
    RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY EndYear
        ORDER BY OpenPerPlanned DESC) AS WhileOpenRank
```

**Check:** in 2026, all hours rank Packing, Quality Assurance, Finishing and Test, Cutting, Assembly; while open the order is Quality Assurance, Finishing and Test, Assembly, Cutting, Packing. Packing falls from first to last, which shows its excess was time charged late. Quality Assurance leads while open, but its planned hours, 331, are by far the smallest, so a few hours move its ratio.


**Check:** seven rows. Released 336, 568, 680, 524, 515, 460, 328; completed 348, 6,110, 6,280, 6,673, 4,524, 5,877, 3,201; shipped 2,018, 3,331, 3,182, 3,539, 3,673, 3,686, 3,311. From February to June the plant completed about 12,000 more units than it shipped, an inventory build that kept every hour on work orders, so almost no time was indirect. **Watch for:** without the SupplyMode filter February shows 5,838 units shipped and a build of 271 where 3,331 and 2,779 belong.

**Step 5. Find the month the late share passed a quarter.**

```sql
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', WorkDate) AS WorkMonth,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN WhileOpen = 0 THEN Hours ELSE 0 END)
        / SUM(Hours), 3) AS ShareAfterEnd
FROM Flagged
WHERE WorkDate BETWEEN '2024-01-01' AND '2024-12-31'
GROUP BY WorkMonth
ORDER BY WorkMonth;
```

**Check:** twelve rows, at most 0.16 through June, then 0.41 in 2024-07, the month the surge days began in earnest. In July the build ended, the same 64 employees kept their hours, and the days with no work order became indirect time.

### Requirement (5): The memo

**Step 6. Write the half page.** Late time is 65.8% of the direct hours of 2026 against 19.5% in 2024; counted while open, no work center exceeds plan; the work-center comparison measures where late time is parked, not efficiency; the plant-level measure of Chapter 11 does not depend on the operation. Recommendations: reject entries after the operation ends, record time with no open operation as indirect time with a reason, review late entries weekly.

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** Packing is the last operation of every routing, so late time lands there. The ranking changes because the late time is parked, not because Packing works hard.
- **(4)** Completions ran well above shipments for several months, so hours stayed on work orders. When completions fell to the rate of shipments, the same people had hours with no work order.
- **(5)** The plant-level conclusion stands and the operation-level one does not. A strong memo asks for a recording control, not a new standard.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **`<` for `<=`.** *Symptom:* every entry late, share 1.000. *Response:* ask on which day an operation's last hour is recorded.
- **Planned hours counted per entry.** *Symptom:* Packing at 1.93. *Response:* ask how many entries an operation has.
- **An inner join to the hours.** *Symptom:* Packing at 4.73. *Response:* ask what an operation with no time is in the plan.
- **All items shipped.** *Symptom:* a build of 271 in February. *Response:* ask which shipments the plant made.
- **Efficiency read from the ratio.** *Symptom:* "Packing is the least efficient". *Response:* ask where the late hours were charged.
