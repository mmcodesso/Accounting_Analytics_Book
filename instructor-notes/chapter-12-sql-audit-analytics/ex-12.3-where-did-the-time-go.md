## Purpose and learning outcomes

Tutorial 11.2 showed Packing and Quality Assurance recording several times their planned hours, and Tutorial 12.3 showed that much direct time is recorded after its operation ended. The plant manager asks what the comparison looks like when only the time recorded while an operation was open is counted, and what the plant was doing in the first months of the data. The audit mindset: when a measure depends on a field of doubtful quality, test the field first, then repeat the measure on the part that can be trusted. A ratio that fails because of how time was recorded is not evidence of inefficiency. The analytics type is diagnostic.

Students can:

- flag late entries by comparing WorkDate with ActualEndDate, keeping the entries with no operation apart (Requirement 1);
- repeat a comparison on the trustworthy part and rank the work centers both ways (Requirements 2 and 3);
- build a monthly fact table from separate CTEs and read a stock build in it (Requirement 4);
- say what the operation-level data can and cannot support (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution BAR M5 (cost accounting and variance analysis) and Core S1 M8 (data mining and analysis); IMA Cost Accounting and Operational Knowledge; IFAC IES 2 (b) and IES 3 (a); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): Flag the late entries

**Step 1. Flag each direct entry, then count by year.** A Flagged CTE joins LaborTimeEntry to its operation with a LEFT JOIN, so every direct entry stays, and adds three 1-or-0 flags with CASE. The final query, in the script, counts the flags by the year of WorkDate and takes the hours and the share after the end on the entries with an operation.

```sql
-- Exercise 12.3, test T1: direct labor time flagged as recorded while the operation was open
-- Population: all direct labor time; expected: every entry with an operation, recorded by its end
WITH Flagged AS (
    SELECT lt.LaborTimeEntryID, lt.WorkDate, op.ActualEndDate,
        lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours AS Hours,
        CASE WHEN op.WorkOrderOperationID IS NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
            AS NoOperation,
        CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate <= op.ActualEndDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
            AS WhileOpen,
        CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate < op.ActualStartDate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
            AS BeforeStart
    FROM LaborTimeEntry AS lt
        LEFT JOIN WorkOrderOperation AS op
            ON op.WorkOrderOperationID = lt.WorkOrderOperationID
    WHERE lt.LaborType = 'Direct Manufacturing'
)
```

**Check:** three rows (entries, with no operation, before the start, share after the end): 2024, 21,406, 5, 0, 0.195; 2025, 15,798, 7, 0, 0.498; 2026, 13,913, 8, 0, 0.658. The 20 entries with no operation (47.91 hours) are a group of their own, to repair from the original records. **Watch for:** three wrong versions.

- An INNER JOIN drops them and counts 51,097 entries, with the same shares, though the requirement asks for every direct entry.
- Written with `<` in place of `<=`, every entry counts as late: 0 entries while open and a share of 1.000 in every year. Time is recorded on the operation's last day or after it, never before, so the day itself must count as open.
- Without the LaborType filter, the indirect and non-manufacturing time joins the entries with no operation, 17,762 of them in place of 20.

### Requirement (2): The work-center comparison

**Step 2. Sum the entries to the operation, then join.** The derived table adds each operation's hours, all and while open, before the planned hours are summed, so the planned hours are counted once. The WHERE keeps the operations that ended by the last day with time records, and the LEFT JOIN keeps an operation with no direct time in the plan.

```sql
SELECT strftime('%Y', op.ActualEndDate) AS EndYear,
    wc.WorkCenterName,
    SUM(op.PlannedLoadHours) AS PlannedHours,
    SUM(lab.Hours) AS RecordedHours,
    SUM(lab.OpenHours) AS OpenHours,
    ROUND(SUM(lab.OpenHours) / SUM(op.PlannedLoadHours), 2)
        AS OpenPerPlanned
FROM WorkOrderOperation AS op
    INNER JOIN WorkCenter AS wc ON wc.WorkCenterID = op.WorkCenterID
    LEFT JOIN (
        SELECT lt.WorkOrderOperationID,
            SUM(lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours) AS Hours,
            SUM(CASE WHEN lt.WorkDate <= o.ActualEndDate
                THEN lt.RegularHours + lt.OvertimeHours ELSE 0 END)
                AS OpenHours
        FROM LaborTimeEntry AS lt
            INNER JOIN WorkOrderOperation AS o
                ON o.WorkOrderOperationID = lt.WorkOrderOperationID
        WHERE lt.LaborType = 'Direct Manufacturing'
        GROUP BY lt.WorkOrderOperationID
    ) AS lab ON lab.WorkOrderOperationID = op.WorkOrderOperationID
WHERE op.ActualEndDate <= (SELECT MAX(WorkDate) FROM LaborTimeEntry)
GROUP BY EndYear, wc.WorkCenterName
```

![A mock of the two work-center ratios side by side for the last year, the comparison the plant manager asked for.](../figures/chapter-12-sql-audit-analytics/IF-12-3-A.pdf)

**Check:** 15 rows. In 2026, recorded per planned on all hours and while open: Assembly 0.40 and 0.34; Cutting 0.41 and 0.29; Finishing and Test 0.56 and 0.39; Packing 4.19 and 0.22; Quality Assurance 4.02 and 0.81. Every work center is below plan on the time recorded while open in 2026, but not in the earlier years: Quality Assurance reads 1.28 in 2024 and 1.02 in 2025. A ratio below plan on the dated time is not evidence of efficiency, because the hours left out may still be work to reassign. **Watch for:** two wrong versions.

- An INNER JOIN to the labor summary drops operations with no direct time from the plan, and Packing reads 4.73 and Quality Assurance 6.28 on all hours.
- Joining the entries straight to the operations repeats the planned hours for every entry and cuts them to 1.93 and 2.45.

### Requirement (3): The ranking

**Step 3. Rank both ways.** Test T3 wraps the same derived table and adds two RANK() functions partitioned by year; the quote is the start of its outer query.

```sql
SELECT EndYear, WorkCenterName,
    ROUND(RecordedHours / PlannedHours, 2) AS RecordedPerPlanned,
    RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY EndYear
        ORDER BY RecordedHours / PlannedHours DESC) AS AllHoursRank,
    OpenPerPlanned,
    RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY EndYear
        ORDER BY OpenPerPlanned DESC) AS WhileOpenRank
```

**Check:** in 2026, all hours rank Packing, Quality Assurance, Finishing and Test, Cutting, Assembly; while open the order is Quality Assurance, Finishing and Test, Assembly, Cutting, Packing. Packing falls from first to last, which shows its excess was time charged late. Quality Assurance leads while open, but its planned hours, 331, are by far the smallest, so a few hours move its ratio.

### Requirement (4): The first months

**Step 4. Count work orders, completions and shipments by month.** Four CTEs group by `strftime('%Y-%m', ...)`: WorkOrder by ReleasedDate, completions from the line and header, shipments, and hours. Only manufactured items count as shipments.

```sql
Shipped AS (
    SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', s.ShipmentDate) AS Month,
        SUM(sl.QuantityShipped) AS UnitsShipped
    FROM ShipmentLine AS sl
        INNER JOIN Shipment AS s ON s.ShipmentID = sl.ShipmentID
        INNER JOIN Item AS i ON i.ItemID = sl.ItemID
    WHERE i.SupplyMode = 'Manufactured'
    GROUP BY Month
),
```

**Check:** seven rows. Released 336, 568, 680, 524, 515, 460, 328; completed 348, 6,110, 6,280, 6,673, 4,524, 5,877, 3,201; shipped 2,018, 3,331, 3,182, 3,539, 3,673, 3,686, 3,311. From February to June the plant completed about 12,000 more units than it shipped, an inventory build consistent with the little indirect time from February to May, although June already had 2,239 indirect hours. The build is a plausible explanation, to confirm with the production schedules, and a strong answer says so. **Watch for:** without the SupplyMode filter February shows 5,838 units shipped and a build of 271 where 3,331 and 2,779 belong.

**Step 5. Find the month the late share passed a quarter.**

```sql
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', WorkDate) AS WorkMonth,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN WhileOpen = 0 THEN Hours ELSE 0 END)
        / SUM(Hours), 3) AS ShareAfterEnd
FROM Flagged
WHERE WorkDate BETWEEN '2024-01-01' AND '2024-12-31'
    AND NoOperation = 0
GROUP BY WorkMonth
ORDER BY WorkMonth;
```

**Check:** twelve rows, at most 0.16 through June, then 0.41 in 2024-07, the month the surge days began in earnest. In July the build ended and the same 64 employees kept recording their hours, more of them as indirect time.

### Requirement (5): The memo

**Step 6. Write the half page.** Late time is 65.8% of the direct hours of 2026 against 19.5% in 2024. Counted while open, no work center exceeds plan in 2026. The work-center comparison measures where late time is parked, not efficiency. The plant-level measure of Chapter 11 does not depend on the operation, although its hours and the standards still need corroboration. The entries with no operation need repair. Recommendations: reject entries after the operation ends, record time with no open operation as indirect time with a reason, review late entries weekly.

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** Packing is the last operation of every routing, so late time lands there. The ranking changes because the late time is parked, not because Packing works hard.
- **(4)** Completions ran well above shipments for several months, which is consistent with hours staying on work orders, a plausible explanation that the production schedules would confirm. The answer notices that indirect time had already risen in the last month of the build.
- **(5)** The plant-level totals do not depend on the operation charged, and the operation-level comparison cannot be relied on. A strong memo asks for a recording control, not a new standard, and does not read a ratio below plan as efficiency.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **An inner join in Step 1.** *Symptom:* 51,097 entries. *Response:* ask what happened to the entries whose operation does not exist.
- **`<` for `<=`.** *Symptom:* every entry late, share 1.000. *Response:* ask on which day an operation's last hour is recorded.
- **Planned hours counted per entry.** *Symptom:* Packing at 1.93. *Response:* ask how many entries an operation has.
- **An inner join to the hours.** *Symptom:* Packing at 4.73. *Response:* ask what an operation with no time is in the plan.
- **All items shipped.** *Symptom:* a build of 271 in February. *Response:* ask which shipments the plant made.
- **Efficiency read from the ratio.** *Symptom:* "Packing is the least efficient", or "every work center is efficient". *Response:* ask where the late hours were charged, and what the hours left out still are.
- **The build as a proven cause.** *Symptom:* "the build kept every hour on work orders". *Response:* ask what the last month of the build shows, and which records would confirm it.
