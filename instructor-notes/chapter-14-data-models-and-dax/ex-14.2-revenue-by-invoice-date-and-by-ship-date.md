## Purpose and learning outcomes

Chapter 12's auditor found invoices posted in the year after the goods were delivered. The controller now wants each year's revenue by invoice date beside its revenue by ship date, and the lines that cross a year listed. The exercise is the inactive relationship at work: one Date table, two roles, and a measure that chooses. It also practices flattening, because ShippedLines carries its header's and its shipment's dates. The analytics type is descriptive, with a first step toward diagnosis.

Students can:

- build a fact table from three merges and keep a left join where one side may be missing (Requirements 1 and 2);
- relate a second date inactively and say why it cannot be active (Requirement 3);
- switch the relationship on in a measure and explain the (Blank) row (Requirement 4);
- flag and sort the lines that cross a year and tie them to Chapter 12 (Requirement 5);
- recommend a revenue date and say what to disclose (Requirement 6).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution BAR M10 (advanced data analytics: data models and joins) and Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions); IMA Reporting and Control (financial recordkeeping) and Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Before you assign

- **Data conditions.** SalesInvoiceLine.ShipmentLineID is empty on the 439 Services lines, because design services do not ship; every other line finds its ShipmentLine and Shipment. Every shipment is delivered in the month it ships. No line is dated outside the Date table.
- **Verify first.** In **Model view**, SalesInvoiceLine relates to Item, Customer and Date, the Date on InvoiceDate. ShippedLines, the exercise's second fact table, shares Item and Date with it, with InvoiceDate active and ShipmentDate inactive. Every relationship is many to one and **Single**.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The shipment Tables

**Step 1. Import the two Tables and stop them loading.**

a. Choose **Home > Get data > Excel workbook**, select CharlesRiver.xlsx, and in the **Navigator** select T14_Shipment and T15_ShipmentLine. Click **Transform Data**.
b. Rename the queries `ShipmentHeaders` and `ShipmentLines`.
c. Choose **Home > Choose Columns** in each and keep the columns the exercise names.
d. Right-click each query and clear **Enable load**.

**Check:** ShipmentHeaders keeps ShipmentID and ShipmentDate, and ShipmentLines keeps ShipmentLineID and ShipmentID. Neither appears in the **Data** pane.

### Requirement (2): ShippedLines

**Step 2. Import the lines again and merge three times.**

a. Import T17_SalesInvoiceLine as `ShippedLines` and keep the five columns the exercise names.
b. Choose **Home > Merge Queries** with SalesInvoice on SalesInvoiceID and expand InvoiceNumber and InvoiceDate.
c. Merge with ShipmentLines on ShipmentLineID and expand ShipmentID.
d. Merge with ShipmentHeaders on ShipmentID and expand ShipmentDate. Use **Left Outer** each time and clear **Use original column name as prefix**.
e. Set InvoiceDate and ShipmentDate to **Date**, and choose **Close & Apply**.

```m
    #"Merged Queries1" = Table.NestedJoin(#"Expanded SalesInvoice", {"ShipmentLineID"}, ShipmentLines, {"ShipmentLineID"}, "ShipmentLines", JoinKind.LeftOuter),
    #"Merged Queries2" = Table.NestedJoin(#"Expanded ShipmentLines", {"ShipmentID"}, ShipmentHeaders, {"ShipmentID"}, "ShipmentHeaders", JoinKind.LeftOuter),
```

**Check:** ShippedLines has 27,494 lines with a ship date and 439 without, the Services lines, so every line of SalesInvoiceLine is there. **Watch for:** an **Inner** join chosen by mistake. It drops the Services lines, and Shipped Revenue falls short of the Validation page by their revenue in every year (the last column of the Step 4 table). The tie in Step 4 catches it.

### Requirement (3): The relationships

**Step 3. Relate ShippedLines.**

a. In **Model view**, drag ItemID from **ShippedLines** onto ItemID in **Item**.
b. Drag InvoiceDate from **ShippedLines** onto Date in **Date**.
c. Drag ShipmentDate from **ShippedLines** onto Date in **Date**.

**Check:** the third line is dashed: the table pair already has an active path, and only one path between two tables can be active. **Watch for:** a student who makes the ship date the active one. The invoice-date line then goes inactive, so Shipped Revenue follows the ship date too and no longer ties to Revenue, which stays on the invoice date. The columns do not swap, because both ship-date columns use the ship date. Ask which line is dashed, and restore the invoice date as the active one.

### Requirement (4): The measures and the matrix

**Step 4. Add Shipped Revenue and Revenue by Ship Date.**

a. Add the two measures below to Key Measures, with the display folder `Ex 14.2`.
b. Add a **Matrix** with Year from **Date** on **Rows** and Revenue, Shipped Revenue and Revenue by Ship Date in **Values**.

```dax
Shipped Revenue = SUM ( ShippedLines[LineTotal] )
```

```dax
Revenue by Ship Date =
CALCULATE (
    [Shipped Revenue],
    USERELATIONSHIP ( ShippedLines[ShipmentDate], 'Date'[Date] )
)
```

**Check:** the page titled "Revenue by invoice date and by ship date":

| Year | Revenue and Shipped Revenue | Revenue by Ship Date | Services revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | $27,313,723.48 | $26,358,117.76 | $1,045,095.60 |
| 2025 | $29,376,702.28 | $28,040,804.94 | $1,261,365.95 |
| 2026 | $29,756,420.08 | $28,528,117.59 | $1,213,344.00 |

The (Blank) row holds $3,519,805.55 in the ship-date column and nothing in the others: the 439 Services lines, which have no shipment, so the inactive relationship finds no date for them. Both measures total $86,446,845.84. Each year is lower by ship date mainly because Services drop to (Blank). **Watch for:** Revenue by Ship Date equal to Shipped Revenue. Two causes give it: a measure without USERELATIONSHIP, or the ship date made active in Step 3. The Revenue column tells them apart, because it equals the other two only in the first case. Look at the measure, **Model view** and one line that crosses a year (Step 5): matching totals do not show the cause.

### Requirement (5): The lines that cross a year

**Step 5. Flag and list them.**

a. In **Transform data**, choose **Add Column > Custom Column** in ShippedLines, name it `CrossesYear` and enter the formula below.
b. Set its type to **True/False** and choose **Close & Apply**.
c. Add a **Table** visual with SalesInvoiceLineID, InvoiceNumber, InvoiceDate, ShipmentDate and LineTotal, and add CrossesYear to **Filters on this visual** with the value true.
d. Open the menu of SalesInvoiceLineID in the **Columns** well and choose **Don't summarize**, so each line has a row of its own.

```m
    #"Added Custom" = Table.AddColumn(#"Changed Type1", "CrossesYear", each [ShipmentDate] <> null and Date.Year([ShipmentDate]) <> Date.Year([InvoiceDate])),
```

**Check:** the table titled "Invoice lines whose invoice date and ship date fall in different years" lists 28 lines in two kinds, one row for each SalesInvoiceLineID, with a total of $108,444.85.

- *Invoiced after the year of shipment:* 16 lines on 15 invoices dated 2025-01-01 to 01-06, for shipments of December 2024, $90,138.93; 7 lines on 7 invoices dated 2026-01-01 to 01-06, for shipments of December 2025, $16,307.68.
- *Dated before their shipment:* 3 lines on SI-2025-006733 to 006735, dated 2024-11-23 to 12-15 and shipped 2025-01-01, $649.05; 2 lines on SI-2026-013741 and 013743, dated 2025-11-29 and 12-03 and shipped 2026-01-01, $1,349.19. Their numbers carry the next year's prefix (Chapter 5).

Chapter 12's test counts 8 invoices, $19,540.86, for deliveries of 2025, by posting date: the 7 here plus SI-2026-013742, dated in 2025 and posted on 2026-01-01. **Watch for:** a CrossesYear with no test for a blank ShipmentDate. In Power Query a missing date is not equal to a year, so the flag then reaches 467 lines: the 28 and every Services line.

**Watch for:** a table without the line key, which groups lines that share an invoice number and both dates. SI-2025-006743 has two, $7,278.71 and $13,765.27, which show as one row of $21,043.98 and the table has 27 rows for 28 lines.

### Requirement (6): The recommendation

**Step 6. Outline the memo.** Use the ship date for goods revenue. Chapter 12 assumes that control passes on delivery, and every shipment is delivered in the month it ships, so the ship date dates revenue as delivery would; invoice-date revenue misstates the years by the late invoices. Services have no ship date, so the report needs a rule, such as the invoice date as a documented proxy for when the work was done, and it should disclose the invoice-date figure beside it. Accept any recommendation that gives its reason, discloses the other date, and has a Services rule if it chooses the ship date.

**Instructor check.** In **DAX query view**, run these two rows from the Checks tab.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Lines that cross a year", CALCULATE ( COUNTROWS ( ShippedLines ), ShippedLines[CrossesYear] = TRUE () ), "Revenue with no ship date", CALCULATE ( [Shipped Revenue], ISBLANK ( ShippedLines[ShipmentDate] ) ) )
```

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** The pair of tables has an active path already, so the second date is inactive; a measure switches it on.
- **(4)** The tie of Shipped Revenue to the Validation page, and the (Blank) row as the Services lines that do not ship.
- **(5)** One row for each crossing line, the two kinds of crossing, each with its cause, and the comparison with Chapter 12: a different date basis, and one invoice more.
- **(6)** A date, the Services rule, the disclosure, and the assumption that control passes on delivery.

A strong answer ties each figure to a table and says which date it uses. A weak one chooses a date by preference.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **An inner join.** *Symptom:* Shipped Revenue short of Revenue by the Services amount each year. *Response:* ask which lines have no shipment, and which join keeps them.
- **No USERELATIONSHIP, or the ship date made active.** *Symptom:* the two ship-date columns agree. *Response:* ask which relationship each measure uses and which line is dashed.
- **A guardless flag.** *Symptom:* 467 lines in the table. *Response:* ask what the Services lines' ship date is.
- **The line key left out.** *Symptom:* 27 rows. *Response:* ask what one row of the table should be.
- **The (Blank) row treated as an error.** *Symptom:* a plan to delete it. *Response:* ask what a design service shipping date would be.
- **A difference from Chapter 12.** *Symptom:* "the model disagrees with the audit". *Response:* ask which date each one uses.
- **A recommendation by preference.** *Symptom:* "the ship date is more accurate". *Response:* ask what assumption makes it so.
