## Purpose and learning outcomes

The controller wants a balance sheet beside the income statement of Tutorial 14.3, at the end of whichever month the reader selects and before that year's closing entries, so that it compares with Chapter 6's pre-closing balance sheet. The exercise is the cumulative measure: a balance is the sum of every posting to a date, so the measure removes the Date filter and puts its own bound back. It also settles what the closes do to a balance sheet. The analytics type is descriptive.

Students can:

- say what each of a cumulative measure's three filter arguments does (Requirement 1);
- build a month-end matrix that totals zero and ties to net income and to Chapter 6 (Requirement 2);
- explain why revenue and expense hold only the current year at a month-end (Requirement 3);
- compare three treatments of the closes and name the post-closing one (Requirement 4);
- say why signs taken from NormalBalance misstate subtotals (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics: data models and joins, dashboards); IMA Reporting and Control (financial statement preparation) and Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The Balance measure

**Step 1. Add Balance to the Key Measures table.**

a. In **Report view**, select **Key Measures** in the **Data** pane and choose **Modeling > New measure**.
b. Type the formula from the exercise in the formula bar (Shift+Enter starts a new line) and press Enter.
c. On the **Measure tools** tab, format it as an amount.
d. In **Model view**, select the measure and type `Ex 14.1` in **Display folder** in the **Properties** pane.

The model answer is this measure.

```dax
Balance =
VAR AsOf = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE (
        SUM ( GLEntry[Debit] ) - SUM ( GLEntry[Credit] ),
        REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ),
        'Date'[Date] <= AsOf,
        NOT ( GLEntry[IsYearEndClose] && GLEntry[PostingDate] = AsOf )
    )
```

**Check:** the measure sits in the folder Ex 14.1. The model note, one sentence for each argument:

- Removing the filters on Date drops the month in the filter context, so the balance can reach back to the first posting.
- The date bound keeps every posting up to the last day selected, which makes the balance cumulative.
- The third argument leaves out a closing entry only when it is posted on that last day, so a year-end reads before its close while the closes of earlier years, dated before it, stay in and move each year's income to retained earnings.

**Watch for:** a balance that does not accumulate, which carries the month's filter. The matrix then reads the month's own movement, assets of $373,560.56 against $39,328,524.11, and still totals zero, because the postings of one month balance. A total of zero does not test the cumulation.

### Requirement (2): The matrix and its ties

**Step 2. Build the page.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 14.1`.
b. Select **Matrix**, drag AccountType and then AccountSubType from **Account** into **Rows** and Balance into **Values**, and expand the rows so each type shows its subtypes.
c. Add a **Slicer** with YearMonth from **Date**, and turn on **Single select** in its format settings.
d. Select `2026-12`.

**Check:** the matrix on the page titled "Balance at the selected month-end, before that day's closing entries" reads debits less credits, so it totals zero, liabilities and equity are negative, and revenue and expense together are minus the year's net income. Three ties hold. The total is zero. The assets equal the liabilities and the equity with their signs reversed plus the year's net income, which is the revenue and expense balance with its sign reversed, as in Chapter 6's balance sheet. That net income equals the Net Income card of the Income Statement page. The view is a pre-closing trial balance arranged as a balance sheet, in which revenue and expense hold the profit of the year not yet closed.

### Requirement (3): June and the first year-end

**Step 3. Change the month.** Select `2026-06` in the slicer, then `2024-12`.

**Check:** both months total zero. In June, revenue and expense hold six months of postings; at the first year-end, they hold the year, a net income of $5,110,308.27. The reason is the third argument: the closes of earlier years are dated before the as-of date, so they stay in the balance and set revenue and expense to zero at each earlier year-end. Only the current year's postings remain.

### Requirement (4): Two variants of the measure

**Step 4. Add the variants and compare.**

a. Add the two measures below to the same display folder.
b. Add a matrix with AccountType on **Rows** and the three balance measures in **Values**.

```dax
Balance with Closes =
VAR AsOf = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE (
        SUM ( GLEntry[Debit] ) - SUM ( GLEntry[Credit] ),
        REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ),
        'Date'[Date] <= AsOf
    )
```

```dax
Balance on GL Amount =
VAR AsOf = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE ( [GL Amount], REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ), 'Date'[Date] <= AsOf )
```

**Check:** the page titled "Three balance measures at the selected month-end", at `2026-12`:

| Type | Balance | Balance with Closes | Balance on GL Amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Asset | $39,328,524.11 | $39,328,524.11 | $39,328,524.11 |
| Liability | -$13,896,428.43 | -$13,896,428.43 | -$13,896,428.43 |
| Equity | -$20,928,484.44 | -$25,432,095.68 | -$10,683,821.46 |
| Revenue | -$29,638,557.92 | $0.00 | -$86,339,955.07 |
| Expense | $25,134,946.68 | $0.00 | $71,591,680.85 |
| Total | $0.00 | $0.00 | $0.00 |

![A mock of the Ex 14.1 page at the selected month-end, the end state of Requirements 2 and 4.](../figures/chapter-14-data-models-and-dax/IF-14-1-A.pdf)

At this year-end Balance with Closes is the post-closing balance sheet, because the ledger holds the year's closes: the year's income is already in retained earnings and revenue and expense are zero. At any other month-end no close is dated on the as-of date, so it equals Balance, and the measure's name alone does not make a balance post-closing. Balance on GL Amount leaves out every close, so no year's income reaches retained earnings. Equity is the opening equity alone and revenue and expense hold all three years. It is wrong for a balance sheet at any date after the first year-end.

### Requirement (5): The contra accounts

**Step 5. List the accounts with the opposite side.**

a. Add a **Table** visual with AccountNumber, AccountName, AccountType and NormalBalance from **Account**.
b. Add the measure Contra Accounts to **Values**; accounts for which it is blank drop out of the table. Another route is to read the two columns for the accounts whose NormalBalance disagrees with their type.

```dax
Contra Accounts =
COUNTROWS (
    FILTER (
        Account,
        ( Account[AccountType] IN { "Asset", "Expense" } && Account[NormalBalance] = "Credit" )
            || ( Account[AccountType] IN { "Liability", "Equity", "Revenue" } && Account[NormalBalance] = "Debit" )
    )
)
```

**Check:** the table titled "Accounts whose normal balance is opposite to the usual side for their type" lists eight accounts: 1030 Allowance for Doubtful Accounts; 1150, 1160, 1170, 1186, accumulated depreciation; 3040 Dividends or Owner Distributions; 4060 Sales Returns and Allowances and 4070 Sales Discounts. A statement that took each sign from NormalBalance would show accumulated depreciation as a positive amount and add it to the assets: $43,853,037.03 against $39,328,524.11, an overstatement of $4,524,512.92, twice the $2,262,256.46 of depreciation. Revenue would read $30,124,087.04, $485,529.12 too high, twice the contra revenue.

**Instructor check.** In **DAX query view**, run this row from the Checks tab.

## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** Three sentences, one for each argument, and the reason for the third: it keeps a year-end before its close and the earlier closes after theirs.
- **(2)** The signs reversed, the three ties (zero, assets, net income, the revenue and expense balance read with its sign reversed) and the source of each.
- **(3)** The cumulation reaches back, and the earlier closes zero the earlier years, so revenue and expense show the current year only.
- **(4)** Names Balance with Closes as post-closing and Balance on GL Amount as the one that never moves income to equity, with the equity figures as evidence.
- **(5)** The accounts by name, the overstatement by contra accounts, and that a statement signs by type.

A strong answer ties each figure to a source. A weak one reports the matrix and stops at "it totals zero".

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **A balance for the month.** *Symptom:* assets of a few hundred thousand dollars. *Response:* ask what a balance is the sum of.
- **The zero as proof.** *Symptom:* "it totals zero, so it is right". *Response:* ask whether a month of balanced postings would also total zero.
- **The closes left in.** *Symptom:* revenue and expense of zero at the year-end. *Response:* ask which balance sheet Chapter 6 built.
- **GL Amount as the base.** *Symptom:* equity at its opening level and three years of revenue. *Response:* ask where the year's net income went.
- **A multiple selection.** *Symptom:* the page reads the last day of what was selected. *Response:* turn on **Single select**.
- **Signs by NormalBalance.** *Symptom:* assets above Chapter 6's. *Response:* ask what accumulated depreciation does to the assets it reduces.
