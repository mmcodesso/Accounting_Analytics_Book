## Purpose and learning outcomes

The sales manager asks how much more volume the Design Trade Customer Promotion would need, at each discount, to pay for itself. The Part II case answered once with Goal Seek. The exercise turns that answer into a page the manager can use: a slider for the discount and a measure that returns the break-even lift. The lift is a condition, not a forecast. What decides the advice is the evidence of the case, which found no unusual increase in volume in any promotion's months. The analytics type is prescriptive, resting on a model of unit margins.

Students can:

- create a what-if parameter with a whole-number range and say why (Requirement 1);
- scope measures to one promotion and one year (Requirement 2);
- write the break-even lift from unit margins (Requirement 3);
- record the lift at five discounts and compare one with the Part II case (Requirement 4);
- weigh a required lift against evidence and advise (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution BAR M8 (planning techniques, forecasting and decision models) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics); IMA Strategy, Planning, and Performance (decision analysis) and Data Analytics and Insights (applied forecasting, predictive and prescriptive analytics); IFAC IES 2 (b) (management accounting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The parameter

**Step 1. Create the Discount parameter.**

a. Choose **Modeling > New parameter > Numeric range**.
b. Set **Name** to `Discount`, **Data type** to **Whole number**, **Minimum** to 0, **Maximum** to 20, **Increment** to 1 and the default to 12.
c. Keep **Add slicer to this page**.

```dax
GENERATESERIES ( 0, 20, 1 )
```

```dax
Discount Value = SELECTEDVALUE ( 'Discount'[Discount], 12 )
```

**Check:** the table Discount has 21 rows, from 0 to 20, and Discount Value returns the slider's value, 12 until the slider moves. The slider reads whole percents, so every measure divides its value by 100. The Checks tab proves the row count and the two ends.

**Watch for:** a decimal range from 0 to 0.2 in steps of 0.01. In Power BI Desktop 2.158 it stopped at 0.19, 20 values, so the manager could not choose `0.20`. A step of 0.01 is not stored exactly, and twenty of them add to slightly more than 0.2, which is the likely reason. The whole-number range avoids the question, and the check is that its slider reaches 0 and 20.

### Requirement (2): The unit measures

**Step 2. Add the three measures.**

a. Select **Key Measures** and choose **Modeling > New measure** for each.
b. Type `Ex 15.4` in **Display folder** in **Model view**.

```dax
Design Trade Units = CALCULATE ( SUM ( SalesInvoiceLine[Quantity] ), PromotionProgram[PromotionName] = "Design Trade Customer Promotion", 'Date'[Year] = 2026 )
Design Trade Price per Unit = DIVIDE ( CALCULATE ( [Price Before Discount], PromotionProgram[PromotionName] = "Design Trade Customer Promotion", 'Date'[Year] = 2026 ), [Design Trade Units] )
Design Trade Cost per Unit = DIVIDE ( CALCULATE ( [Standard Cost], PromotionProgram[PromotionName] = "Design Trade Customer Promotion", 'Date'[Year] = 2026 ), [Design Trade Units] )
```

**Check:** 561.74 units, $416.23 of price before discount a unit and $215.99 of standard cost a unit, the Part II case's inputs. The filter names the promotion and the year.

**Watch for:** a price taken from revenue divided by units. That is the price after the discount, $366.28, and the lift then counts the discount twice: 43.0% at 12 percent in place of 34.2%.

### Requirement (3): The break-even lift

**Step 3. Write the measure.**

```dax
Break-even Lift =
VAR Price = [Design Trade Price per Unit]
VAR Cost = [Design Trade Cost per Unit]
VAR Commission = 0.01903
VAR Disc = [Discount Value] / 100
RETURN
    DIVIDE (
        Price * ( 1 - Commission ) - Cost,
        Price * ( 1 - Disc ) * ( 1 - Commission ) - Cost
    ) - 1
```

**Check:** the contribution a unit earns without the discount is $192.32 and with a 12 percent discount $143.32; their ratio less one is 34.2%, the break-even lift, so volume must rise by that share for discounted sales to earn what full-price sales earned.

**Watch for:** two silent failures. Leaving the division by 100 makes the discount 12, the denominator turns negative and the lift reads -104.1%. Leaving the commission out reads 33.2% at 12 percent and 11.6% at 5 percent: about a point low at 12 percent, and plausible enough to pass unless the student compares with the case.

### Requirement (4): The page and the record

**Step 4. Build the page and read five discounts.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 15.4` with the slider, cards for the five measures, and a **Table** with Discount and Break-even Lift.
b. Select the slider, choose **Format > Edit interactions**, and click the **None** icon above the table.
c. Read the lift at 5, 8, 10, 12 and 15 percent.

**Check:** the table, titled "Break-even lift at each discount", lists every discount and the cards follow the slider. The lift at 12 percent equals the Part II case.

**Watch for:** a table with one row. Without step (b) the slicer filters the table to the chosen discount. A slicer set to several selections makes SELECTEDVALUE return its default, so the cards stay at 12 whatever the student picks.

### Requirement (5): The advice

**Step 5. Weigh the lift against the evidence.** The Part II case measured volume in each promotion's months against the other months of its year and found no lift outside ordinary variation: from -20.7% to 11.9%. A 12 percent discount needs 34.2%. Even 5 percent needs 11.9%, about the largest increase ever measured. The advice is not to repeat the promotion at 12 percent, and, if the manager wants to test one, to offer a small discount to a defined group of customers and compare its volume with a comparable group.

![The lift the promotion needs at each discount, set against the largest lift ever measured, the picture behind the recommendation.](../figures/chapter-15-management-reports/IF-15-4-B.pdf)

|---|---:|

| 8 percent | 20.5% |
| 10 percent | 27.0% |
| 12 percent | 34.2% |
| 15 percent | 46.7% |

| Quantity | Value | Step |
|---|---|---|

> **Instructor notes, Exercise 15.4 (CR15 reference model and Python):** (1) a whole-number parameter, GENERATESERIES ( 0, 20, 1 ), 21 values with a default of 12, divided by 100 in the measures (a decimal range from 0 to 0.2 by 0.01 stopped at 0.19 in Desktop 2.158, 20 values). Promotion 9 2026 lines: 561.74 units, price before discount 416.23 per unit, standard cost 215.99 per unit (the Part II case's inputs). Break-even lift = (p (1 - 0.01903) - c) / (p (1 - d) (1 - 0.01903) - c) - 1: 5% 11.9%, 8% 20.5%, 10% 27.0%, 12% 34.2% (the Part II case), 15% 46.7%. The case found no unusual increase in volume in any promotion's months (from -20.7% to +11.9% against the other months of the year), so even a 5% discount needs about the largest increase ever measured; a defensible answer is not to repeat it, or to test a small discount on a defined group first.

## What a strong answer says

- **(1)** Whole numbers are exact; the decimal range loses its last step.
- **(4)** The five lifts, and that the 12 percent figure equals the case.
- **(5)** The required lift against the measured range; the lift is a break-even condition, not a forecast; the volume of the promotion months cannot show what the promotion caused, so a test with a comparison group is the next step.

A strong answer ends with an action and its limit. A weak one repeats the table.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **A decimal range.** *Symptom:* the largest choice is 0.19. *Response:* ask how many steps of 0.01 reach 0.2 exactly.
- **No division by 100.** *Symptom:* a lift of -104.1%. *Response:* ask what the slider's 12 means in the formula.
- **Net price as the price.** *Symptom:* 43.0% at 12 percent. *Response:* ask where the discount is taken in the formula.
- **Commission left out.** *Symptom:* 33.2% at 12 percent. *Response:* ask what a sale costs beyond its standard cost.
- **A one-row table.** *Symptom:* only the chosen discount. *Response:* ask what the slider filters.
- **The lift as a forecast.** *Symptom:* "volume will rise by 34.2%". *Response:* ask what the evidence says volume did.
