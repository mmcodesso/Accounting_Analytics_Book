## Purpose and learning outcomes

A manager has seen the forecast in the Analytics pane and wants it in the plan. Before an auditor accepts it, the auditor does what Chapter 7 did with its own forecasts: run it on a quarter whose result is known and compare it with simple benchmarks. The forecast is computed inside the visual, so no measure can return it, and the exercise is a lesson in evidence: a number that cannot be read from the model needs a test and a disclosure before anyone relies on it. The analytics type is predictive, judged by an analytical procedure.

Students can:

- prepare a continuous date axis for a forecast (Requirement 1);
- add a forecast, and read its points and intervals (Requirements 2 and 3);
- compare a forecast with the actual quarter and three benchmarks on one definition of error (Requirement 4);
- test the forecast's sensitivity to the start-up month (Requirement 5);
- write an audit note on fitness and disclosure (Requirement 6).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S2 M9 (audit procedures, analytical procedures and audit data analytics) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (applied forecasting, predictive and prescriptive analytics, statistical analysis); IFAC IES 2 (f) (audit) and IES 4 (a) (professional skepticism and professional judgment); AACSB A5 parts 5.1 and 5.2 (technology judgment, interpretation and communication).

## How to run it

Individual or pairs, about 75 minutes: 10 for the column, 15 for the chart and forecast, 10 to record the points, 15 to compare, 10 to repeat from February, 10 for the note, 5 spare. ## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): A continuous axis

**Step 1. Add MonthStart.**

a. In **Report view**, select **Date** in the **Data** pane and choose **Modeling > New column**.
b. Type the formula below and format the column as a date.

```dax
MonthStart = DATE ( YEAR ( 'Date'[Date] ), MONTH ( 'Date'[Date] ), 1 )
```

**Check:** 48 values, one for each month of the Date table. A forecast needs a continuous date axis, and the text column YearMonth cannot supply one.

### Requirement (2): The chart and the forecast

**Step 2. Chart the history and forecast three months.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 15.5` and a **Line chart** with MonthStart on the **X-axis** and Revenue on the **Y-axis**.
b. In **Filters on this visual**, keep the months from January 2024 to September 2026.
c. In the **Analytics** pane, add a **Forecast** with a **Forecast length** of three months and a **Confidence interval** of 95 percent.
d. Check that MonthStart itself is on the **X-axis**, set to a continuous type and sorted oldest first, that Revenue is the only series and the legend is empty, and record the Desktop version and the forecast settings (length and unit, confidence interval, **Ignore last** and seasonality), so that Step 5 uses the same ones.

**Check:** 33 months of history and three forecast points; the model answer titles the chart "Revenue, January 2024 to September 2026, with a three-month forecast". The Checks tab proves the 33-month window.

**Watch for:** a chart left open to the end of the data. It then holds 36 months and forecasts into fiscal 2027, so the known quarter is part of the history and there is no hindcast.

![A mock of the hindcast chart, revenue through the month before the quarter, with the forecast drawn forward over the known quarter.](../figures/chapter-15-management-reports/IF-15-5-A.pdf)

### Requirement (3): Recording the forecast

**Step 3. Read and total the three months.** Hover over each forecast point and record the value and the interval. Record the three points and the six interval bounds of each run, add the three values for the quarter, and leave the intervals alone: the monthly intervals cannot be added into an interval for the quarter. The actual revenue for the same months, from the table "Actual revenue of the quarter forecast" and the Checks tab:

| Month | Actual revenue |
|---|---:|
| 2026-10 | $2,297,493.38 |
| 2026-11 | $2,469,972.14 |
| 2026-12 | $2,519,722.16 |

**Check:** the quarter's actual revenue is $7,287,187.68. The Checks tab proves it and each month.

### Requirement (4): Against the actual and the benchmarks

**Step 4. Compute one error for every method.** The error of a quarter is the forecast total over the actual less one, so a positive error means over-forecast, and its size, not its sign, ranks the methods. The monthly error is the average of the absolute difference between each month's forecast and actual, over the actual, across the three months. Calculate both for every method. Chapter 7's benchmarks for the same quarter, fitted from February 2024, are in the table below. Students compute their forecast's error the same way and place it beside them.

**Watch for:** mixing the two measures of error. The quarter error nets the months' misses against each other, the monthly error does not, and the ranking changes: the same quarter last year is +0.32% by the quarter and 11.48% by the month.

![The same three benchmarks measured two ways, which changes their ranking.](../figures/chapter-15-management-reports/IF-15-5-B.pdf)

### Requirement (5): Without the start-up month

**Step 5. Start the chart in February.**

a. Copy the chart, and change the months to February 2024 through September 2026; the model answer titles it "Revenue, February 2024 to September 2026, with a three-month forecast". Treat the January run as the run that includes the start-up month, and compare the February run with Chapter 7's benchmarks, which were fitted on the same months.
b. Repeat Step 3 and report how much each month and the quarter changed.

**Check:** the change is read from Desktop. The cause is checkable: January 2024 is 60% of the other months of its year, and the Checks tab proves the ratio.

### Requirement (6): The audit note

**Step 6. Outline the note.** A note may recommend adopting the forecast, a limited trial or rejecting it. Whichever it recommends, it names an error and the planning risk, and it asks that the forecast travel with its test, because required disclosures are necessary but not sufficient and more forecast origins should be tested before the method is adopted. The disclosures are the fitted period and the treatment of the start-up month, the hindcast error beside the benchmark it beat or did not beat, and the limit of three years of history for any seasonal pattern. In the model answer the mean benchmark has the smallest quarter error of the methods compared, while the two recorded forecasts have a smaller average error by month than the mean. Ask the student which error the plan needs, and whether the forecast beat the benchmarks on it.

**Instructor check.** In **DAX query view**, run this and compare it with the Expected results.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Actual 2026 Q4", CALCULATE ( [Revenue], 'Date'[YearQuarter] = "2026-Q4" ), "Mean benchmark error", DIVIDE ( 3 * AVERAGEX ( FILTER ( VALUES ( 'Date'[YearMonth] ), 'Date'[YearMonth] >= "2024-02" && 'Date'[YearMonth] <= "2026-09" ), [Revenue] ), CALCULATE ( [Revenue], 'Date'[YearQuarter] = "2026-Q4" ) ) - 1, "January against the other months", DIVIDE ( CALCULATE ( [Revenue], 'Date'[YearMonth] = "2024-01" ), AVERAGEX ( FILTER ( VALUES ( 'Date'[YearMonth] ), 'Date'[YearMonth] >= "2024-02" && 'Date'[YearMonth] <= "2024-12" ), [Revenue] ) ) )
```

| Benchmark monthly error, in the same order | 4.88%; 3.65%; 11.48% | 4 |

> **Instructor notes, Exercise 15.5:** actual fiscal 2026 Q4 revenue 7,287,187.68 (invoice lines; 33 months from January 2024 to September 2026 fit the forecast). Chapter 7's backtest for the same quarter: trend +4.72% error, mean +0.03% (monthly error 3.65%), same quarter last year +0.32% (11.48% monthly). Power BI's forecast is computed in the visual (exponential smoothing, as Microsoft (2014) described it when the forecast was introduced in Power View) and its values cannot be read from the model; students read them from the forecast's tooltip. A good answer reports the forecast's quarter and monthly errors beside the benchmarks, names the error the plan needs, notes that three years give limited evidence of a stable seasonal pattern, and checks the effect of the start-up month, January 2024 at 60% of the year's other months, on the fit (requirement 5). The note may recommend adoption, a limited trial or rejection; any forecast shown to managers carries its hindcast error and the benchmark it beat or did not beat, and more forecast origins are tested before it is adopted.

## What a strong answer says

- **(4)** The forecast's quarter error beside the three benchmarks, on one definition, then the same for the monthly error, and a sentence on which method did best on each.
- **(5)** How much the forecast moved when one month went, and why: a start-up month at 60% of its neighbors pulls a method that weights history.
- **(6)** The forecast is not fit unless the test, the fitted period, the start-up treatment and the benchmark come with it; three years give limited evidence of a stable seasonal pattern.

A strong answer recommends adoption, a limited trial or rejection using a named error and the planning risk, and a reasoned rejection supported by the comparisons earns full credit. A weak one trusts the line because it looks smooth.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Benchmarks refitted on the start-up month.** *Symptom:* a mean error of -1.25% and a trend of +7.20%, against +0.03% and +4.72%. *Response:* ask which months Chapter 7 fitted.
- **No hindcast.** *Symptom:* the forecast runs into the next year. *Response:* ask which quarter the student already knows.
- **A text axis.** *Symptom:* no forecast to add. *Response:* ask which column is a date.
- **Intervals added.** *Symptom:* an interval for the quarter built from three. *Response:* ask whether three intervals are three independent errors.
- **Two error measures mixed.** *Symptom:* the forecast's quarter error against the benchmarks' monthly errors. *Response:* ask what each error nets.
- **A smooth line trusted.** *Symptom:* no disclosure in the note. *Response:* ask what a manager needs to know to weigh the number.
