## Purpose and learning outcomes

The production manager wants one chart on the plant page that can show hours per standard hour, the overtime share or the labor cost per standard hour, chosen from a slicer. A field parameter does that. The larger lesson is in the numbers: the cost per standard hour rose faster than the hours per standard hour, and the reason is arithmetic that students should be able to write down. The analytics type is descriptive, and the explanation is diagnostic.

Students can:

- write a cost per standard hour with the filter discipline of Manufacturing Hours (Requirement 1);
- create a field parameter and chart it with its slicer (Requirements 2 and 3);
- record three measures by year from one page (Requirement 3);
- split a cost per standard hour into hours and a rate, and say which moved (Requirement 4);
- state what a field parameter cannot be used for (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics and dashboards); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Strategy, Planning, and Performance (performance management); IFAC IES 2 (b) (management accounting) and IES 3 (b) (communicating effectively); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## How to run it

Pairs or individual, about 75 minutes: 10 for Requirement (1), 15 for (2) and the chart, 10 to record the three measures, 20 for (4), 10 for (5), 10 spare. Hold back the identity of (4) until the numbers are on the table. Collect the .pbix and the note.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The measure

**Step 1. Add Labor Cost per Standard Hour.**

a. Select **Key Measures** and choose **Modeling > New measure**.
b. Type the formula below, format it with two decimals, and type `Ex 15.3` in **Display folder** in **Model view**.

```dax
Labor Cost per Standard Hour =
DIVIDE (
    CALCULATE (
        SUM ( LaborTimeEntry[ExtendedLaborCost] ),
        KEEPFILTERS ( LaborTimeEntry[LaborType] <> "NonManufacturing" )
    ),
    [Standard Hours to Cut-off]
)
```

**Check:** the measure divides the manufacturing labor cost by the hours the standards allow to the cut-off, and the Checks tab proves each year's value. KEEPFILTERS keeps the filter on LaborType from replacing one a reader sets, as in Manufacturing Hours. Nothing on this page filters LaborType, so a student who leaves it out passes every test here: the measure is wrong the day it sits under a LaborType filter (Chapter 14).

**Watch for:** two wrong versions, each with a symptom in the last year.

- A sum of every labor type. It adds the non-manufacturing cost and reads $49.92 in 2026 in place of $44.21.
- Standard hours without the cut-off. The completions after 2026-12-11 enter, and 2026 reads $42.98. The earlier years do not move, so a test on them passes.

### Requirement (2): The field parameter

**Step 2. Create the parameter.**

a. Choose **Modeling > New parameter > Fields**.
b. Name it `Plant Measure`, and add Hours per Standard Hour, Overtime Share and Labor Cost per Standard Hour, in that order.
c. Keep the slicer that Power BI adds to the page.

**Check:** a table Plant Measure appears in the **Data** pane, written as a calculated table like this one.

```dax
{
    ("Hours per Standard Hour", NAMEOF ( 'Key Measures'[Hours per Standard Hour] ), 0),
    ("Overtime Share", NAMEOF ( 'Key Measures'[Overtime Share] ), 1),
    ("Labor Cost per Standard Hour", NAMEOF ( 'Key Measures'[Labor Cost per Standard Hour] ), 2)
}
```

### Requirement (3): The chart and the record

**Step 3. Chart the parameter.**

a. Add a **Clustered column chart** with Year on the **X-axis** and the Plant Measure field on the **Y-axis**.
b. In **Filters on this visual**, keep the years 2024 to 2026.
c. Add a **Table** with Year and the three measures, filtered the same way, and read the three measures for each year.

**Check:** the chart is titled "The plant measure chosen in the slicer, by year" and the slicer "Plant Measure"; choosing an entry changes the measure the columns show. The table, titled "The three measures by year (requirement 3)", holds the Expected results.

**Watch for:** two failures. Measures dragged onto the **Y-axis** one by one are not a parameter, so the slicer changes nothing. A slicer that allows several selections would put the cost on the same axis as the share: $44.21 against 0.198, a ratio of about 223 to one, and the share's columns vanish.

![A mock of the model answer's page, a slicer choosing the measure that one column chart shows, above the table of the three measures.](../figures/chapter-15-management-reports/IF-15-3-A.pdf)

### Requirement (4): Why the cost rose faster

**Step 4. Write the identity.** Labor cost per standard hour is hours per standard hour times the labor cost per labor hour. From 2024 to 2026 the hours per standard hour rose 28.0%, the cost per labor hour 4.1% (from $28.11 to $29.26), and so the cost per standard hour 33.2%. The straight-time rate barely moved ($26.57, $26.59, $26.62 a hour), so the rise in cost per hour is the overtime premium on a growing overtime share.

![A board sketch of why the cost per standard hour rose faster than the hours per standard hour.](../figures/chapter-15-management-reports/IF-15-3-B.pdf)

### Requirement (5): The drill-through

**Step 5. Try the parameter as a drill-through field.** Power BI does not accept a field parameter as the field of a drill-through or tooltip page; link the underlying fields instead (Learn, as the chapter says). Neither the project nor the Checks tab holds this, so ask students to report what Desktop does.

**Instructor check.** In **DAX query view**, run this and compare it with the Expected results.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Labor cost per standard hour 2026", CALCULATE ( [Labor Cost per Standard Hour], 'Date'[Year] = 2026 ), "Parameter fields", CONCATENATEX ( 'Plant Measure', 'Plant Measure'[Plant Measure], ", ", 'Plant Measure'[Plant Measure Order], ASC ) )
```

> **Instructor notes, Exercise 15.3 (CR15 reference model):** Labor Cost per Standard Hour = DIVIDE ( CALCULATE ( SUM ( LaborTimeEntry\[ExtendedLaborCost\] ), KEEPFILTERS ( LaborTimeEntry\[LaborType\] \<\> "NonManufacturing" ) ), \[Standard Hours to Cut-off\] ). By year: hours per standard hour 1.18, 1.41, 1.51; overtime share 0.116, 0.167, 0.198; labor cost per standard hour 33.18, 40.71, 44.21 (manufacturing labor cost 2,351,220.56; 2,691,548.63; 2,961,742.51). (5) Learn: field parameters cannot be the linked fields of a drill-through or tooltip page; link the underlying fields instead.

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** The three measures for each year, with the cost per standard hour at $44.21 in 2026.
- **(4)** The identity, the two growth rates, the steady straight-time rate, and overtime as the reason the cost per hour rose.
- **(5)** A field parameter is not a drill-through field; the underlying fields are.

A strong answer separates hours from rate. A weak one says "wages rose".

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **All labor types.** *Symptom:* $49.92 in 2026. *Response:* ask which labor type the plant page leaves out.
- **No cut-off.** *Symptom:* $42.98 in 2026, the earlier years unchanged. *Response:* ask which year the time records end inside.
- **Measures on the axis.** *Symptom:* three series and a dead slicer. *Response:* ask which field the slicer filters.
- **Cost per labor hour.** *Symptom:* $28.11 read as the cost per standard hour. *Response:* ask which denominator the standards give.
- **Wages blamed.** *Symptom:* "pay rates rose". *Response:* ask what the straight-time rate did.
- **No identity.** *Symptom:* two growth rates quoted with no link. *Response:* ask how the three measures multiply.
