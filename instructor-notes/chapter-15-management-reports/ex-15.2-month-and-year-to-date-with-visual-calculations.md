## Purpose and learning outcomes

A monthly package shows a month, its change from the month before and the year to date. A visual calculation writes each in one short formula, with no new measure. The exercise shows the price of that convenience: the calculation sees only the rows of its visual, so a filter on the months changes what it means, while a measure written with time intelligence does not change. Students build both versions, set them side by side, and decide which one a package that managers rely on should show. The analytics type is descriptive, and the two lowest months move into the diagnostic.

Students can:

- add visual calculations for a running sum and a change from the previous row (Requirement 1);
- write a TOTALYTD measure and tie it out against the visual and against Tutorial 14.3 (Requirement 2);
- explain why a filter on the months parts the two columns (Requirement 3);
- explain a low month with a finding of an earlier chapter (Requirement 4);
- choose between a measure and a visual calculation for a shared report (Requirement 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 (visualization and communicating results) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics: data models, predictive analysis, dashboards); IMA Data Analytics and Insights (data visualization) and Strategy, Planning, and Performance (performance management); IFAC IES 2 (a) (financial accounting and reporting) and IES 2 (i) (using information and communications technologies to analyze data); AACSB A5 parts 5.1 and 5.3 (learning to learn and continuous technological adaptability).

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The table and its two calculations

**Step 1. Build the table.**

a. Add a page named `Ex 15.2` and a **Table** visual with YearMonth from **Date** and Net Income.
b. In **Filters on this visual**, keep the year 2026.
c. With the table selected, choose **Home > New visual calculation**, type the first formula below in the formula bar, and press Enter. Add the second the same way, and click **Back to report**.

```dax
YTD = RUNNINGSUM ( [Net Income] )
Change = [Net Income] - PREVIOUS ( [Net Income] )
```

**Check:** twelve rows, with Net Income, YTD and Change. The Net Income column and the year-end total are proved by the Checks tab. The two calculations are computed inside the visual, so no Checks row can read them: compare what Desktop shows with the Expected results.

**Watch for:** a table without the year filter. The running sum has no level to restart at, so it keeps adding across the years: the January row of 2026 reads $10,555,512.91 and its December row $14,748,274.22, three years of net income. This is how the function works, visible only in Desktop. The exercise filters to the year for this reason.

### Requirement (2): The measure and the tie-out

**Step 2. Add the measure and place it in the table.**

a. Select **Key Measures**, choose **Modeling > New measure** and type the formula below. In **Model view**, type `Ex 15.2` in **Display folder**.
b. Add Net Income YTD to the table.

```dax
Net Income YTD = TOTALYTD ( [Net Income], 'Date'[Date] )
```

**Check:** Net Income YTD equals the YTD calculation in every month and reaches $4,503,611.24 in December, the net income of Tutorial 14.3 and the credit of the close JE-2026-000297. The Checks tab proves the measure's value for each month.

**Watch for:** a measure that keeps the closes. December then reads -$4,000,917.54, because the close takes the year's net income back, and the year to date ends at zero. Net Income already leaves the closes out; a student who builds the measure on raw credits and debits has bypassed it.

### Requirement (3): The second half of the year

**Step 3. Filter the table to July through December.**

a. Copy the table onto the page again (the model answer titles the copy "Filtered to July to December (requirement 3)").
b. In **Filters on this visual**, keep only the months July to December.

**Check:** the YTD calculation starts again at July and reaches $2,150,939.31 in December, the sum of six months. Net Income YTD shows $2,467,193.90 in July and $4,503,611.24 in December. The measure reads the model's dates, whatever the visual shows; the calculation reads only the rows it is given. The Checks tab proves the three measure values and the six-month sum.

### Requirement (4): The two lowest months

**Step 4. Name the months and explain one.** The two lowest months of net income are July ($114,521.97) and October ($255,812.14). July has three pay dates, as does January (Chapters 11 and 14). October is the peak of the promotion's discounts, with Furniture revenue 22.7 percent below October of the year before (Chapter 11).

**Watch for:** "three pay dates" offered for both months. January has three pay dates too, yet it is only the third lowest month, at $310,849.93: ask what else differs.

### Requirement (5): Which column the package shows

**Step 5. Recommend the measure.** It is right whatever filter the reader sets, it can be exported and reused on another page, and a test can check it. The visual calculation suits a single chart whose shape is the point.

**Instructor check.** In **DAX query view**, run this and compare it with the Expected results.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "YTD at June", CALCULATE ( [Net Income YTD], 'Date'[YearMonth] = "2026-06" ), "Two lowest months", CONCATENATEX ( TOPN ( 2, CALCULATETABLE ( VALUES ( 'Date'[YearMonth] ), 'Date'[Year] = 2026 ), [Net Income], ASC ), 'Date'[YearMonth], ", ", 'Date'[YearMonth], ASC ) )
```

## What a strong answer says

- **(3)** The measure reads the model and the visual calculation only the visual's rows, so removing the first six months restarts the calculation and leaves the measure alone.
- **(4)** July: three pay dates, recorded when paid. October: the promotion's peak, with Furniture revenue below the year before. A strong answer says why the pay dates matter (payroll is recorded on the pay date with no month-end accrual).
- **(5)** The measure, for a package that is exported, reused and tested; a visual calculation where the chart alone matters.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **No year filter.** *Symptom:* January reads $10,555,512.91. *Response:* ask what the running sum would restart on.
- **Closes kept.** *Symptom:* December reads -$4,000,917.54 and the year ends at zero. *Response:* ask which measure already leaves the closes out.
- **A difference called an error.** *Symptom:* "the visual calculation is wrong in the second half". *Response:* ask which rows it can see.
- **One explanation for both months.** *Symptom:* three pay dates for October. *Response:* ask how many pay dates it has.
- **A visual calculation in the package.** *Symptom:* the column missing from an export. *Response:* ask which column a reader can export.
- **January's change.** *Symptom:* a student reports a bug in the first row. *Response:* ask what the first month has to be compared with.
