## Purpose and learning outcomes

The memo is where five requirements become a decision. The controller takes it into the audit committee's meeting, so students must choose the few numbers that carry the argument, word each finding as what the records show, say what the evidence cannot settle and end with a recommendation someone can act on. It follows the memos of the Part I to Part III cases. The analytics type is prescriptive.

Students can:

- organize two pages by the controller's questions, not by the order of the work (Steps 1 and 2);
- choose and state the evidence of each section with its limits (Steps 3 to 5);
- recommend actions with a way to learn more (Step 6);
- support the memo with exhibits a reviewer can trace to the file (Step 7).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M8 T5 to T6 (visualization and communicating results); IMA Leadership (communication skills); IFAC IES 3 (b), interpersonal and communication skills; AACSB A5 part 5.2 (technology judgment, interpretation and communication).

## Before you assign

- **From earlier work.** Requirements 1 to 5 with their pages, and the memos of the earlier cases as models. No new modeling: students write in a word processor.
- **Length.** At most two pages with a one-page appendix of 5 exhibits. The model memo on the Memo page is about 660 words, an outline and not a finished memo.
- **The model's view.** The profit was real but went into inventories; unremitted tax and payables held cash up; the cash on the balance sheet overstates what the company can use; the first step is to settle the tax position. Another recommendation from the same evidence earns full credit if it follows from the exhibits and states its limits.

## How to run it

Individual, about 90 minutes in class or as a take-home: 15 to plan the evidence, 50 to draft, 25 to check every number against its page and the length. In a workshop, students swap drafts and review them against the grading guide. **Collect** the memo, the appendix and the .pbix.

## Step-by-step solution

### Part 1: Plan the memo

**Step 1. Fix the reader and the recommendation.** The reader is the controller. Write the recommendation first, in one sentence, then keep only evidence that supports it or limits it.

**Step 2. Choose the evidence.** Three to five numbers for each section, each traceable to a page of the report:

| Section | What it must say | Source | Exhibit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial reporting | Where the cash went, the restated sections with the noncash disclosure, what the balance sheet needs | Requirement 2 | 1, 2 |
| Managerial accounting | Which balances absorbed the cash and how fast each turns, what drove the build, a target with its cash effect and assumptions | Requirements 3 and 4 | 3, 4 |
| Auditing | Reconciliations and findings with dispositions, outside evidence, requisition controls, the report's own controls | Requirements 1 and 5 | 5 |

### Part 2: The sections and the close

**Step 3. Financial reporting.** Net income was $4,503,611.24 but cash fell $941,310.30, from $9,383,566.10 to $8,442,255.80, after rising $3,494,591.86 the year before. Inventories absorbed $8,754,515.25, $4,250,904.01 more than the year earned, while unremitted tax ($1,932,519.29) and payables ($821,074.85) held cash up. Restated, operating activities used $807,189.75, investing $0.00 and financing $134,120.55 in principal, with $280,000.00 of equipment acquired by a note disclosed apart. The balance sheet needs the tax position settled ($5,620,840.66 with no remittance recorded, so cash net of it is $2,821,415.14), $147,503.76 of notes reclassified to current, and the opening receivables ($380,000.00) and payables ($2,096,371.12) supported or written off.

**Step 4. Managerial accounting.** Receivables and payables barely moved (DSO 62.2 to 54.9 days, DPO without the opening line 66.2 to 65.7); the cycle grew from 173.7 to 425.7 days through inventory, materials at 422.1 days against a target of 28. The shortfall channel carried the build ($11,219,993.21 of receipts in 2026 against issues near $10,161,204.64), with 5,702 of 10,365 work order and item pairs requisitioned more than once. A target of 28 days leaves an excess of $10,972,164.25, about ten times the fall in cash even at 90 days; the estimate assumes issues continue and the stock is usable, and leaves out finished goods, 522 unordered requisitions and the open orders.

**Step 5. Auditing.** Receivables reconcile to the open invoices except $380,000.00, and payables except $1,456,371.12. Dispositions: the opening balances and the sales tax, follow up, with the schedules, the returns filed, the tax authority's account and the bank reconciliation as outside evidence; the note invoices, expected; the current portion and the unrecorded payroll accrual ($383,044.82), misstatements. One manager, the Production Manager, approved every shortfall requisition, and the records show no check against stock on hand or open orders. The report needs its Tests tab rerun and its Data Through card checked on every refresh.

**Step 6. Recommend.** Settle the sales tax position first. Check every shortfall requisition against stock on hand and open orders before conversion, route it through the supply plan with an approver outside production, and review the 522 open requisitions. Approve 2027 inventory targets and a purchase budget that draws the stock toward them. Present the restated cash flow with its disclosure, reclassify the notes' current portion and follow up the opening balances.

**Step 7. Build the appendix.** One page, five numbered exhibits, each cited in the memo: the cash bridge for fiscal 2025 and 2026; the restated cash flow sections; the working-capital measures by quarter; the materials build by channel with the 2027 target; the findings with their dispositions. They come from the visuals titled "The bridge by year (drill through on a step to its postings)", "Cash flows by activity, as recorded and restated by the indirect method", "The measures at each quarter-end (icons: days of supply against the policy target)", "Materials by year and channel" with "Targets tested at the end of fiscal 2026", and "Findings".

**Check:** the memo answers the controller's questions in the order asked, every number appears in an exhibit or a page, wording describes the records and not a person, and the length holds.

> **Instructor notes, Requirement 6:** a strong memo describes rather than accuses: "no remittance is recorded in the ledger", "requisitions repeated for one work order and item". It explains that the profit was real but went into inventories, materials above all, while unremitted sales tax and growing payables held cash up, so the cash on the balance sheet overstates what the company can use. The recommendation: settle the sales tax position first, because it decides how much of the cash is available; check every shortfall requisition against stock on hand and open orders before it is converted, route it through the supply plan with an approver outside production, and review the 522 open requisitions; approve 2027 inventory targets, since the policies ended with 2026, and a materials purchase budget that draws the stock down toward them; present the restated cash flow with its noncash disclosure and reclassify the current portion of the notes; follow up the unsupported opening balances with Chapter 8's findings; and keep the Tests tab and the Data Through check for every refresh of the report. A memo may raise the valuation of more than a year of materials under ASC Topic 330 as a question, not a conclusion.

## What a strong answer says

- **Opening.** The question and the answer in two sentences: the profit was real and went into inventories.
- **Financial reporting.** The bridge, the restated sections with the noncash disclosure, and three balance sheet items.
- **Managerial accounting.** Which balances moved, the channel behind the build, and a target with its cash effect and its assumptions.
- **Auditing.** Differences equal to recorded items, dispositions, outside evidence, controls over requisitions and over the report.
- **Recommendation.** Specific actions in an order the controller can follow.

A strong memo makes choices and says what the data cannot show; a weak one reports every result in the order found.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Too long.** *Symptom:* three pages or a squeezed font. *Response:* ask which finding the controller would miss least.
- **Organized by requirement.** *Symptom:* headings that read Requirement 1 to 5. *Response:* ask what the controller's questions were.
- **Accusing wording.** *Symptom:* "unpaid" or "withheld" for the sales tax. *Response:* ask what the ledger shows and what it cannot.
- **Cash and profit confused.** *Symptom:* "the company lost money". *Response:* ask where the profit went.
- **No recommendation, or a hedged one.** *Symptom:* "it depends". *Response:* ask what the student would do tomorrow.
- **Numbers that disagree.** *Symptom:* memo and exhibit differ. *Response:* trace each to its page and fix one source.

## Grading guide

| Criterion | Points | Full credit | Partial credit |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Financial reporting | 25 | Bridge, restated sections, noncash disclosure, three balance sheet items | Bridge without the restatement |
| Managerial accounting | 20 | Turnover, build, target with cash effect and assumptions | Results without assumptions |
| Auditing | 25 | Reconciliations, dispositions, outside evidence, both sets of controls | Differences without dispositions |
| Recommendation | 15 | Specific, ordered actions | A decision without actions |
| Exhibits and traceability | 10 | 5 exhibits, cited, agreeing with the text | Missing or inconsistent |
| Form and audience | 5 | Two pages, three sections, plain for the controller | Over length |

## Extensions and discussion questions

- Write a five-sentence note to the Production Manager that explains the requisition control without assigning blame.
- Present the first section to the committee in three minutes and name the exhibit you would show.
- Discuss: if the tax authority's account showed the tax remitted, what would change in the memo and what would stay?
- Discuss: the report will be refreshed every quarter. Which of its tests should the committee see?
