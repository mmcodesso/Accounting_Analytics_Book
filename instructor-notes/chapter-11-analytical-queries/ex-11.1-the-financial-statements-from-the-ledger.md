## Purpose and learning outcomes

Exercise 6.1 built the income statement from the pre-closing trial balance in Excel, and Tutorial 10.2 rebuilt the trial balance in SQL. The controller now wants both statements from one saved query that moves to another year-end when one date changes. It is the chapter's widest use of CASE: one CASE maps each account to a statement line, a CASE inside SUM turns the lines into the columns of a totals row, and a CASE in ORDER BY sorts the lines in statement order. The analytics type is descriptive, with the diagnostic habit of tying the result to an independent figure.

By the end, students can:

- build the pre-closing trial balance for an as-of date held in one place, leaving out only that year's closes (outcomes 1 and 4);
- assign accounts to statement lines with a searched CASE whose order and ELSE they can defend, and derive the subtotals by conditional aggregation (outcome 1);
- test the balance sheet equation against an independent figure, and save the query as a view sorted by a CASE in the outer query (outcome 5).

**Competencies practiced.** CPA Evolution Core S1 M1 to M3 (financial statements, accounts and transactions) and BAR M10 (advanced data analytics); IMA Reporting and Control (financial statement preparation) and Data Analytics and Insights (data tools and techniques); IFAC IES 2 (a) and (i); AACSB A5 part 5.1.

## Step-by-step solution

### Requirement (1): The date, the closes and the trial balance

**Step 1. Find the closing entries of the as-of date.**

```sql
-- Requirement (1), step 1: the closing entries dated on the as-of date
-- Population: journal entries; expected: the two closes of 2026-12-31
WITH Params AS (
    SELECT '2026-12-31' AS AsOfDate
),
Closes AS (
    SELECT EntryNumber
    FROM JournalEntry
    WHERE EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%'
        AND PostingDate = (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
)
SELECT EntryNumber
FROM Closes
ORDER BY EntryNumber;
```

**Check:** two rows, JE-2026-000296 and JE-2026-000297. **Watch for:** `EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%'` alone returns the closes of every year, 6 in all; the date condition makes the year the parameter.

**Step 2. Total the trial balance.** The model script's next query reads the same two CTEs and a TrialBalance CTE (credits less debits by account, through the as-of date, without the closes), and counts the accounts, their net and the credit balances. Step 3 contains the same three CTEs, so students may skip it.

**Check:** 71 accounts, a net of zero and credit balances of $67,107,722.36.

### Requirement (2): The statement lines

**Step 3. Assign every account to a statement line with CASE.** Credits less debits makes assets and expenses negative, so the sum for the asset lines flips the sign.

```sql
-- Requirement (2): each account assigned to a statement line with CASE
-- Population: the trial balance accounts; expected: eleven lines, none unassigned
WITH Params AS (
    SELECT '2026-12-31' AS AsOfDate
),
Closes AS (
    SELECT EntryNumber
    FROM JournalEntry
    WHERE EntryType LIKE 'Year-End Close%'
        AND PostingDate = (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
),
TrialBalance AS (
    SELECT a.AccountID, a.AccountNumber, a.AccountName,
        a.AccountType, a.AccountSubType,
        ROUND(SUM(gl.Credit) - SUM(gl.Debit), 2) AS Balance
    FROM GLEntry AS gl
        INNER JOIN Account AS a ON a.AccountID = gl.AccountID
    WHERE gl.PostingDate <= (SELECT AsOfDate FROM Params)
        AND gl.VoucherNumber NOT IN (SELECT EntryNumber FROM Closes)
    GROUP BY a.AccountID, a.AccountNumber, a.AccountName,
        a.AccountType, a.AccountSubType
),
Lines AS (
    SELECT
        CASE
            WHEN AccountType = 'Revenue'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Operating Revenue'
                THEN 'Operating revenue'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Revenue'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Contra Revenue'
                THEN 'Contra revenue'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Expense' AND AccountSubType = 'COGS'
                THEN 'Cost of goods sold'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Expense'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Operating Expense'
                THEN 'Operating expenses'
            WHEN AccountType IN ('Revenue', 'Expense')
                THEN 'Other income and expense'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Asset' AND AccountSubType
                IN ('Current Asset', 'Contra Current Asset')
                THEN 'Current assets'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Asset'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Fixed Asset'
                THEN 'Fixed assets'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Asset'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Contra Fixed Asset'
                THEN 'Contra fixed assets'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Liability'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Current Liability'
                THEN 'Current liabilities'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Liability'
                AND AccountSubType = 'Long-Term Liability'
                THEN 'Long-term liabilities'
            WHEN AccountType = 'Equity' THEN 'Equity'
            ELSE 'Unassigned'
        END AS StatementLine,
        SUM(CASE WHEN AccountType = 'Asset'
            THEN -Balance ELSE Balance END) AS Amount
    FROM TrialBalance
    GROUP BY StatementLine
)
SELECT StatementLine, ROUND(Amount, 2) AS Amount
FROM Lines
ORDER BY StatementLine;
```

**Check:** 11 lines and no Unassigned line; the amounts are in the Expected results. **Watch for:** the order of the branches decides everything.

- A catch-all branch for Revenue and Expense placed first absorbs every income statement account: 7 lines remain, and in Step 4 net revenue reads $0.00 and the percentages are NULL (a division by zero) while net income is still right.
- Without the sign flip, total assets read -$39,328,524.11.
- The `Contra Current Asset` branch assigns nothing on this data (0 accounts have postings); it stops such an account falling into Unassigned.
- Closes left in put every revenue and expense line at zero and equity at $25,432,095.68, the year's income already inside it, and the balance sheet still balances. Every year's closes left out make net income $14,748,274.22, the income of all the years.

### Requirement (3): Net revenue, margin and net income

**Step 4. Total the lines by conditional aggregation.** The model script adds a Totals CTE with one `SUM(CASE StatementLine WHEN 'Operating revenue' THEN Amount ELSE 0 END)` for each line. The final SELECT adds revenue and contra revenue for net revenue, then the cost of goods sold, the operating expenses and the other line in turn, and divides by net revenue for the two percentages. The alternative is UNION ALL, as in Step 6.

**Check:** net revenue $29,731,057.92, gross margin 37.32%, operating income 15.56% and net income $4,503,611.24. **Watch for:** the expenses are already negative, so gross margin adds the cost line; subtracting it reads $48,365,361.55.

### Requirement (4): The balance sheet check and the closing entry

**Step 5. Test the equation, and compare net income with the closing entry.** A `Closing` CTE sums the credit less debit that the year's closes post to retained earnings (account `3030`) with `gl.VoucherNumber IN (SELECT EntryNumber FROM Closes)`. The final SELECT shows total assets, liabilities, equity, net income, the difference `assets - liabilities - equity - net income` and the closing entry.

**Check:** total assets $39,328,524.11, liabilities plus equity plus net income $39,328,524.11, a difference of zero, and a closing entry of $4,503,611.24. **Watch for:** a zero difference proves little. It holds with the closes left in, with every year's closes left out and with the date dropped, because each still balances. The closing entry against net income, and Chapter 6, catch them. With the date dropped, total assets fall by $271,327.17, the cash paid after the as-of date.

### Requirement (5): The view in statement order

**Step 6. Save the statements as a view.** The model script drops `Ex11_1_IncomeStatement` if it exists and creates it from the Requirement (4) CTEs, with no ORDER BY. It stacks the eleven lines and six totals (net revenue, gross margin, operating income, net income, total assets, and liabilities, equity and net income) with UNION ALL: 17 rows.

**Step 7. Query the view in statement order.**

```sql
-- Requirement (5), step 2: the view in statement order, sorted by a CASE expression
-- Population: the view; expected: seventeen lines in statement order
SELECT StatementLine, Amount
FROM Ex11_1_IncomeStatement
ORDER BY CASE StatementLine
    WHEN 'Operating revenue' THEN 1
    WHEN 'Contra revenue' THEN 2
    WHEN 'Net revenue' THEN 3
    WHEN 'Cost of goods sold' THEN 4
    WHEN 'Gross margin' THEN 5
    WHEN 'Operating expenses' THEN 6
    WHEN 'Operating income' THEN 7
    WHEN 'Other income and expense' THEN 8
    WHEN 'Net income' THEN 9
    WHEN 'Current assets' THEN 10
    WHEN 'Fixed assets' THEN 11
    WHEN 'Contra fixed assets' THEN 12
    WHEN 'Total assets' THEN 13
    WHEN 'Current liabilities' THEN 14
    WHEN 'Long-term liabilities' THEN 15
    WHEN 'Equity' THEN 16
    WHEN 'Liabilities, equity, and net income' THEN 17
    END;
```

![A mock of the saved view queried in statement order, the exercise's end state.](../figures/chapter-11-analytical-queries/IF-11-1-A.pdf)

**Check:** 17 rows from operating revenue to the final total. **Watch for:** a label the CASE does not list gets NULL and sorts first, so a misspelled label jumps to the top. An ORDER BY inside the view works in SQLite but leaves the outer query unable to re-sort.

## What a strong answer says

The deliverable is a short note comparing the results with Chapter 6.

- **The statements agree with Chapter 6.** Net income is $4,503,611.24, the credit of JE-2026-000297 to retained earnings.
- **Why assets exceed liabilities plus equity before the closes.** The year's revenue and expenses are not yet in retained earnings, so equity is short by net income. After the closes net income reads zero and equity is larger by the same amount.
- **What the one parameter does.** The as-of date drives the date condition and the closes, which are found by EntryType and that date, so another year-end needs one edit.
- **What agreement does not show.** The SQL reproduces the ledger and Chapter 6, which is consistency, not completeness of the ledger.

A weak note reports a zero difference as proof, or does not say which entries were left out and why.

## Common mistakes and how to respond

- **Closes left in.** *Symptom:* net income of $0.00. *Response:* ask what the closes do to the revenue accounts.
- **Every year's closes left out.** *Symptom:* net income of $14,748,274.22. *Response:* ask which entry moves this year's income.
- **No date condition.** *Symptom:* assets $271,327.17 lower. *Response:* ask what the ledger holds after the as-of date.
- **A catch-all branch first.** *Symptom:* 7 lines and NULL percentages. *Response:* ask which condition each row meets first.
- **Assets or the margin with the wrong sign.** *Symptom:* total assets of -$39,328,524.11, or a margin of $48,365,361.55. *Response:* ask which accounts have debit balances.
- **A zero difference taken as the check.** *Symptom:* no comparison to the closing entry. *Response:* ask what else would still balance.
